Dear Editors,

Please consider *Precision-dependent invariance costs and the geometry of initial sets* for publication in *Discrete and Continuous Dynamical Systems*.

The paper asks how many open-loop controls are needed to keep every actual initial state near a constraint set when the tolerance decreases with the time horizon. Its main theorem shows that nearly full area does not determine this cost. In a single planar system whose two control maps are contractions in a common norm, every compact initial set with interior has one explicitly computed precision spectrum. For every prescribed loss of area, we construct one compact initial set with a strictly smaller spectrum on a nontrivial range of precision rates. That same set works for all tolerance sequences in the theorem, while the exact and outer invariance rates remain unchanged.

The main proof compares arbitrary approximate controls with stopped feasible words and bounds the area covered after a first deviation from the feasible coding. These geometric estimates address trajectories that may leave the constraint and apply throughout the horizon. Classical stopping-scale and symbolic counting methods then evaluate the rates. A separate common-radial theorem and one fixed expanding example delineate the roles of control-dependent contraction and transverse expansion.

This contribution concerns the relationship between constrained dynamics, initial-set geometry and invariance complexity. The introduction distinguishes it from the fixed-tolerance complexity results of Chen and Zhong, the hyperbolic-control-set theory of Da Silva and Kawan, and partition-based invariance pressure. We believe that the precise limitation of a positive-area hypothesis will be of interest to DCDS readers working on entropy and controlled dynamics.
