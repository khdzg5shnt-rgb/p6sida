Precision and reliability costs in contracting control systems

Abstract
Even when every input drives the state to zero, keeping a trajectory near a prescribed set can require an increasing number of control choices as the tolerance shrinks. We study this cost for two $C^2$ planar maps and a triangular constraint. A controller observes the initial state and selects one input sequence from a finite catalogue. The triangle is controlled invariant, both maps contract toward zero, and their critical sets have area zero. Their prescribed linear parts have radial factors equal to a common power $\beta>1$ of the contraction factors of the inverse angular maps. For tolerances $\delta_n=\exp(-\gamma n+o(n))\to0$, we compute the exponential catalogue growth rate $S(\gamma)$ for full coverage. One compact set obtained by removing an arbitrarily small fixed area has a lower saturation rate. If neither map has a critical point in the triangle, allowing failure probability $\exp(-\alpha n+o(n))$, $\alpha>0$, gives the rate $\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}$. Here $L_a(\alpha)$ is the growth rate of the number of exactly feasible words needed in the linear model with that failure allowance. The proof bounds the area served by one input and constructs regions that force different control prefixes. Finally, an injective example preserves every finite-horizon full-cover count of the linear model but has a reliability lower rate strictly above the linear rate. Thus critical sets of area zero do not suffice for the probability formula.
