\begin{abstract}
We study the minimum number of open-loop controls needed to keep every
actual initial state within one Euclidean tolerance of a constraint set
at all times of a finite horizon. For smooth, globally invertible planar
systems whose initial angle affects subsequent radial contraction, we
prove a common-cost threshold $\gamma_*=\beta h_a$, where
$h_a=-\sum_i a_i\log a_i$ and
$\delta_n=\exp(-\gamma n+o(n))$. At and below this threshold, every
fixed positive-area compact initial set has rate $\gamma/\beta$.
Above it, one compact set of arbitrarily nearly full area has rate
$h_a$ simultaneously for all finite higher exponents and their precision
sequences, while the full constraint set has a strictly larger lower
rate. An invariant cone for moving feasible graphs and a first-deviation
estimate compare arbitrary approximate controls with stopped physical
witnesses at a loss of at most $1+Bn$. In a separate radius-autonomous
family, unequal matching powers yield distinct full-set and nearly
full-area rates at every sufficiently small positive exponent. This
contrast identifies the role of matching without asserting a general
classification. A scoped topological obstruction excludes coordinate
reduction of genuine feedback instances, and a smooth collapse example
shows why matching only near the attracting origin is insufficient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Invariance entropy measures the control information required to keep
an initial set inside a controlled invariant set; see
Kawan \cite{Kawan2013}. For a system contracting toward a point, outer
invariance complexity can be bounded at every fixed positive tolerance.
The cost can nevertheless grow exponentially when the tolerance
decreases with the horizon. Which features of feasible control choices
determine that growth, and when does it depend on the actual initial set?

We consider all binary inputs and assign a member of a finite control
catalogue to each physical initial state. The same tolerance $\delta_n$
is imposed at every integer time from $0$ through $n$; departures from
and returns to the constraint set are permitted within that tolerance.
Our question concerns independence of the exponential cost across
\emph{all} fixed positive-area compact initial sets. A set of almost
full area need not contain the rare states that determine the full-set
cost, so positive area alone does not settle this question.

The main theorem establishes an exact common-cost boundary for a
matched angular-feedback family. Here even a fixed control word on
an initial-radius fibre produces different radial trajectories as the
initial angle varies. A logarithmic slope cone controls the moving
feasible graphs and proves that complete feasible initial sections
remain intervals. We then estimate a wrong first branch in physical
coordinates. These estimates, together with complete tails, connect
classical stopping counts to the optimal catalogue of arbitrary
approximate controls. The same nearly full-area compact set works for
\emph{every} higher precision exponent and its precision sequences.

A second theorem concerns a different, radius-autonomous family.
When its two radial powers differ, nearly full-area sets and the full
constraint already have distinct rates at arbitrarily small positive
precision exponents. This yields a matching criterion within that
family, rather than a necessary and sufficient classification of
angular-feedback systems. A topological feasible-image obstruction
shows that a nonempty subfamily of the main result is not a simultaneous
coordinate corollary of the radius-autonomous models. A local-matching
collapse example delineates another restriction of the positive theorem.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

The finite-horizon object below is the outer invariance complexity
of Chen and Zhong \cite[Section 2.2]{CZ2024}, with $n+1$ constrained
times instead of $n$. Their bounded-complexity and equi-invariability
results concern fixed tolerances. Exponential and practical
stabilization entropies \cite{Colonius2012,CH2021} address accuracy
in different stabilization tasks. These works give the information
context; we do not introduce a new catalogue definition.

Measure-theoretic invariance entropies and variational principles
are developed in \cite{WHS2019,NWH2022}. The entropy dimensions of
Chen, Huang and Zhong \cite{CHZ2026} use prescribed invariant-partition
cylinders and weights $\exp[-s(N\tau)^\alpha]$; their measure-covering
tolerance permits missing measure. Their proofs provide cylinder
covering and variational tools, but do not estimate Euclidean
first-deviation bands for arbitrary controls at $\delta_n$. On our
connected $Q$, a finite clopen invariant partition would have only
one nonempty cell, whereas neither single input preserves all of $Q$
at the first step. Their main clopen variational theorems therefore
do not directly apply. This does not dispose of their measurable
cylinder estimates: those still require the physical comparison
proved here. Ordinary area need not be invariant, and its use here
is not a new measure-theoretic framework.

Graph and volume-distortion methods in control entropy are existing
tools; Kawan and Da Silva \cite[Appendix]{KD2018} prove a graph-transform
volume lemma near a partially hyperbolic all-time lift. In our
uniformly contracting family, every bounded bi-infinite trajectory is
the origin: $\|x_0\|_*\le L^k\sup_j\|x_j\|_*$ for all $k$. The physical
angular coordinate is singular there. That volume lemma alone does
not give the complete feasible-graph splits or a uniform
diagonal-precision comparison for all approximate controls.

Prefix stopping, product weights, types, and typical sets are classical
\cite{Hutchinson1981,Csiszar1998}. Yang, Chen, Yang and Zhou
\cite{YCYZ2025} develop cost-threshold pressure and Bowen-equation
results on a given invariant partition. Their prescribed-address
spanning/separated comparison is not a comparison with all approximate
physical inputs. The additional controlled step here is a
first-deviation band and a stopped-witness count with only linear
loss. The mismatch root and the type estimates are applications of
existing counting tools, not new pressure or graph-transform theory.

\section{Counting problem and main results}

Let
\[
Q=\{(s,z)\in\R^2:0\le s\le1,\ |z|\le s\},\qquad \area(Q)=1.
\]
For any two maps $F_0,F_1:\R^2\to\R^2$ and
$u=(u_1,u_2,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\N}$, write
$F_u^0x=x$ and $F_u^kx=F_{u_k}\circ\cdots\circ F_{u_1}x$.
Distances are Euclidean, and $Q^\delta=\{x:\dist(x,Q)<\delta\}$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:catalogue}
For a nonempty compact $K\subset Q$, let $r_F(n,\delta;K)$ be
the least size of a finite catalogue $\mathcal U\subset\{0,1\}^{\N}$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalogue}
\forall x\in K\ \exists u\in\mathcal U\
\forall k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:\quad F_u^kx\in Q^\delta.
\end{equation}
The input assignment may depend on the actual state. No exact
itinerary is imposed. A trajectory may leave and reenter $Q$ provided
every required time satisfies \eqref{eq:catalogue}. Only the first
$n$ letters matter, but infinite inputs make the tail constructions
unambiguous. Finiteness will follow from exact controlled feasibility.
\end{definition}

Throughout, a precision sequence is positive, tends to zero, and
satisfies $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$ for a finite $\gamma\ge0$.
It need not be monotone. All logarithms are natural. Fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
a_0,a_1\in(0,1),\quad a_0+a_1=1,\quad a_0\ne a_1,\quad\beta>1,
\end{equation}
and put $a_* =\min(a_0,a_1)$ and
$h=-a_0\log a_0-a_1\log a_1$.
We also use
\[
H_b(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\qquad
\chi_a(t)=-t\log a_0-(1-t)\log a_1,
\]
with $0\log0=0$, so $H_b(a_0)=\chi_a(a_0)=h$.

\subsection{Matched angular feedback}
For
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feedbackepsilon}
0\le\ee<\frac{a_*}{16(\beta+1)},
\end{equation}
define on the whole plane
\begin{align}
p_0(s,z)&=a_0+\frac{\ee z}{1+s^2+z^2},&
p_1(s,z)&=a_1-\frac{\ee z}{1+s^2+z^2},\label{eq:pfeedback}\\
H_0(s,z)&=(sp_0^\beta,p_0^{\beta-1}(z+p_1s)),&
H_1(s,z)&=(sp_1^\beta,p_1^{\beta-1}(z-p_0s)).\label{eq:H}
\end{align}
All factors in \eqref{eq:H} are evaluated at the input state.
The endpoint $\ee=0$ is the matched linear family.

\begin{theorem}[Common-cost boundary with angular feedback]\label{thm:main}
The maps \eqref{eq:H} are global $C^\infty$ diffeomorphisms, fix
the origin, drive bounded initial sets uniformly exponentially to
the origin under all inputs, and make $Q$ controlled invariant.
Their derivatives at the origin are those of the matched linear maps.
For every fixed positive-area compact $K\subset Q$ and every
precision sequence of finite exponent $\gamma\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal}
\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}\le
\liminf_n\frac{\log r_H(n,\delta_n;K)}n
\le\limsup_n\frac{\log r_H(n,\delta_n;K)}n
\le\min\{\log2,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $0\le\gamma\le\beta h$ the limit exists and
equals $\gamma/\beta$.

For every $\zeta\in(0,1)$ there is a fixed compact $K_\zeta\subset Q$
of area greater than $1-\zeta$ such that, simultaneously for every
finite $\gamma>\beta h$ and every precision sequence with that exponent,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
\lim_n\frac{\log r_H(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)}n=h
<\liminf_n\frac{\log r_H(n,\delta_n;Q)}n.
\end{equation}
The set is chosen before the exponent, sequence, and horizon.
\end{theorem}

The threshold concerns all positive-area compact initial sets. It
does not classify them into two possible costs above the threshold,
nor determine the exact high-precision spectrum or its limit for $Q$.
The positive result holds on the entire range
\eqref{eq:parameters}--\eqref{eq:feedbackepsilon}; the coordinate
obstruction in Section~\ref{sec:conjugacy} has a smaller scope.

\subsection{A different family: radius-autonomous mismatch}
Keep $a_0,a_1$ as above, but now let $\beta_0,\beta_1>1$ and
$\beta_{\max}=\max_i\beta_i$. For
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialepsilon}
0\le\ee<\frac{a_*}{4(\beta_{\max}+1)},\qquad
q_0(s)=a_0+\frac{\ee s}{1+s^2},\quad
q_1(s)=a_1-\frac{\ee s}{1+s^2},
\end{equation}
define
\begin{align}
G_0(s,z)&=(sq_0(s)^{\beta_0},q_0(s)^{\beta_0-1}(z+q_1(s)s)),\nonumber\\
G_1(s,z)&=(sq_1(s)^{\beta_1},q_1(s)^{\beta_1-1}(z-q_0(s)s)).\label{eq:G}
\end{align}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatchconstants}
\rho_i=a_i^{\beta_i},\quad
\chi=-\sum_i a_i\log\rho_i,\quad
\gamma_0=-\log\max_i\rho_i,\quad
\rho_0^D+\rho_1^D=1\quad(D>0).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Low-precision separation under radial mismatch]\label{thm:mismatch}
Suppose $\beta_0\ne\beta_1$. The maps \eqref{eq:G} are global
smooth diffeomorphisms with a common attracting origin under all
inputs, and $Q$ is controlled invariant. For every $\zeta\in(0,1)$
there is one compact $K_\zeta\subset Q$ of area greater than $1-\zeta$
such that, simultaneously for every $0<\gamma<\gamma_0$ and every
precision sequence of exponent $\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatchrates}
\lim_n\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;Q)}n=\gamma D,
\qquad
\lim_n\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)}n=\gamma h/\chi,
\qquad D>h/\chi.
\end{equation}
Every fixed positive-area compact $K\subset Q$ also has lower rate
at least $\gamma h/\chi$ in this range.
\end{theorem}

Thus a fixed system can exhibit strict initial-set dependence at
every sufficiently small positive exponent. The theorem is restricted
to \eqref{eq:G}; it makes no claim about unequal powers in the
angular-feedback family. The precise matching criterion in this
radius-autonomous class is given in Corollary~\ref{cor:criterion}.

\section{Moving feasible graphs and physical distortion}

In this section the system is \eqref{eq:H}. Put
\[
p_-=a_*-\ee/2,\quad p_+=1-p_-,\quad r=p_+^\beta<1,
\quad\sigma_0=-1,\quad\sigma_1=1.
\]
These bounds hold on the whole plane, not just on $Q$.

\begin{lemma}[Global dynamics and one-step feasibility]\label{lem:dynamics}
The dynamical assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} hold. For
$s>0$, write $y=z/s$ and $P_i(s,y)=p_i(s,sy)$. There is a unique
$\theta(s)\in(-1,1)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:divider}
\theta(s)=2P_0(s,\theta(s))-1.
\end{equation}
The complete feasible angular domains are $[-1,\theta(s)]$ for
input $0$ and $[\theta(s),1]$ for input $1$. Choosing $0$ at the
shared endpoint defines a natural exactly feasible code.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $f=z/(1+s^2+z^2)$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:globalderivatives}
|f|\le\tfrac12,\quad |sf_s|\le1,\quad
|zf_z|\le\tfrac12,\quad |sf_z|\le\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
Use $f_s=-2sz/(1+s^2+z^2)^2$,
$f_z=(1+s^2-z^2)/(1+s^2+z^2)^2$, and
$2|sz|\le s^2+z^2$ to obtain these bounds.
In the shear $v=z-\sigma_i s$, with output shear $V=Z-\sigma_i S$,
the map is $(S,V)=(sp_i^\beta,vp_i^{\beta-1})$. Consequently
\[
\det DH_i=p_i^{2\beta-2}
 [p_i+\beta s(p_i)_s+((\beta-1)z+\sigma_i s)(p_i)_z]>0,
\]
since the added terms have absolute value at most $3\beta\ee/2<p_-$.
To prove global bijectivity, for any output $(S,V)$ solve
\[
t-p_i(St^{-\beta},Vt^{1-\beta}+\sigma_i St^{-\beta})=0,
\qquad p_-\le t\le p_+.
\]
The left side has opposite weak signs at the endpoints. Its derivative
is at least $1-3\beta\ee/(2p_-)>0$, by
\eqref{eq:globalderivatives}. It has exactly one root; that root
recovers the unique preimage. The implicit function theorem supplies
the smooth global inverse. Positive factors make the real powers
smooth, and differentiation at zero gives
\[
DH_0(0)=\begin{pmatrix}a_0^\beta&0\\a_0^{\beta-1}a_1&a_0^{\beta-1}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
DH_1(0)=\begin{pmatrix}a_1^\beta&0\\-a_1^{\beta-1}a_0&a_1^{\beta-1}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Choose $M\ge1$ with $M>r/(1-p_+^{\beta-1})$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:origincontraction}
\|x\|_*=\max(M|s|,|z|),\quad
L=\max\{r,p_+^{\beta-1}+r/M\}<1,
\qquad\|H_i x\|_*\le L\|x\|_*.
\end{equation}
Indeed $|s'|\le r|s|$ and
$|z'|\le p_+^{\beta-1}|z|+r|s|$ on the whole plane.
This proves convergence relative to the origin, without asserting
pairwise contraction, and controls tails outside $Q$.

In calculation coordinates the actual map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angularmap}
S=sP_i^\beta,\qquad Y=\sigma_i+(y-\sigma_i)/P_i.
\end{equation}
On $0<s\le1$, $|y|\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partialbounds}
|(P_i)_s|\le\ee,\qquad |(P_i)_y|\le\ee s.
\end{equation}
The separating function $y-2P_0(s,y)+1$ has positive derivative
at least $1-2\ee$ and opposite signs at $-1,1$. Its unique zero
is \eqref{eq:divider}. The condition $-1\le Y\le1$ is exactly
the asserted pair of domains; their angular endpoints map to
$-1,1$. Since $S<s\le1$, these are the full one-step constraints.
Both controls fix the vertex, completing controlled invariance.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Invariant graph cone and complete intervals]\label{lem:graphs}
Fix an actual initial radius $0<s_0\le1$. For every finite word $w$,
the set $I_w(s_0)$ of initial angles whose trajectory remains in $Q$
at \emph{all} times through $|w|$ is a nondegenerate closed interval.
Its terminal angular map is increasing onto $[-1,1]$. The terminal
radial graph $s=S(y)$ over that entire interval satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone}
|S'(y)/S(y)|\le1.
\end{equation}
Every complete prefix-free family gives a cover of $[-1,1]$ by
these intervals, with disjoint interiors and shared endpoints retained.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a feasible-prefix image is a graph obeying \eqref{eq:cone}.
Write $k=S'/S$ and, along that graph,
$A=(P_i)_sS'+(P_i)_y$, so $|A|\le2\ee S$. By
\eqref{eq:angularmap},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graphupdate}
q_i=1-(y-\sigma_i)A/P_i,\qquad
\frac{dY}{dy}=q_i/P_i,\qquad
k_{\rm new}=(P_i k+\beta A)/q_i.
\end{equation}
The parameter bound gives $p_->63a_*/64$ and
$(2\beta+4)\ee<3a_*/16<p_-/2$.
Since $P_i(1-P_i)\ge p_-(1-p_-)\ge p_-/2$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coneinvariance}
q_i\ge1-4\ee S/P_i>0,
\qquad
|k_{\rm new}|\le
\frac{P_i+2\beta\ee S}{1-4\ee S/P_i}<1.
\end{equation}
Along the graph, $\Psi(y)=y-2P_0(S(y),y)+1$ has derivative
at least $1-4\ee S>0$ and opposite endpoint signs.
It therefore divides the parent graph at exactly one point.
On each feasible side, \eqref{eq:angularmap} is increasing onto
the entire $[-1,1]$, and \eqref{eq:coneinvariance} preserves the cone.
The original fibre has $S\equiv s_0$, hence slope zero. Induction
proves all assertions, retaining every intermediate constraint and
closed endpoint. The terminal angular map has positive derivative,
including one-sided endpoint derivatives. Smooth dependence of the
endpoints on $s_0>0$ follows from the implicit function theorem.
\end{proof}

For $w=w_1\cdots w_\ell$, put
$\lambda_w=\prod_{j=1}^\ell a_{w_j}$ and
$\mathcal P_w(x)=\prod_{j=1}^\ell p_{w_j}(x_{j-1})$.
The actual product depends on the initial angle; there is no shared
radial trajectory on an initial-radius fibre.
Write
\[
A_w=\{(s_0,s_0y):0<s_0\le1,\ y\in I_w(s_0)\}\cup\{(0,0)\}
\]
for its physical complete feasible initial strip.

\begin{lemma}[Derived radial and angular comparisons]\label{lem:products}
There are fixed $C_P,C\ge1$ such that, for every complete feasible
word and every point of its initial interval,
\begin{align}
C_P^{-1}\lambda_w&\le\mathcal P_w(x)\le C_P\lambda_w,\nonumber\\
C_P^{-\beta}s_0\lambda_w^\beta&\le s_\ell\le C_P^\beta s_0\lambda_w^\beta,
&C^{-1}\lambda_w^{-1}&\le\partial_{y_0}Y_{w,s_0}\le C\lambda_w^{-1}.
\label{eq:products}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervalsize}
2C^{-1}\lambda_w\le |I_w(s_0)|\le2C\lambda_w,
\end{equation}
and the physical complete feasible strip $A_w$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinderarea}
C^{-1}\lambda_w\le\area(A_w)\le C\lambda_w.
\end{equation}
All finite-word boundary curves have zero area and remain in the covers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Along a feasible word, $s_j\le r^js_0$ and
$|p_i(x_j)-a_i|\le\ee s_j$. Thus
\[
|\log(\mathcal P_w/\lambda_w)|\le B_P:=\frac{\ee}{p_-(1-r)},
\qquad C_P=e^{B_P}.
\]
Set $d_0=4\ee/p_-<1$. Equation \eqref{eq:graphupdate} gives
$|q_i-1|\le4\ee s_j/p_-$, and hence
\[
\sum_j|\log q_i|\le B_q:=\frac{4\ee}{p_-(1-d_0)(1-r)}.
\]
The chain rule along the moving graphs yields
$\partial_{y_0}Y_{w,s_0}=\prod_j q_{w_{j+1}}/p_{w_{j+1}}$.
Taking $C=e^{B_P+B_q}$ proves the angular comparison.
The radial identity $s_\ell=s_0\mathcal P_w^\beta$ proves the
remaining inequalities in \eqref{eq:products}.
The full-range assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:graphs} gives
\eqref{eq:intervalsize}. Integrating the length against the physical
Jacobian $s_0\,ds_0$ proves \eqref{eq:cylinderarea}, since
$\int_0^1s_0\,ds_0=1/2$. Smooth endpoints for $s_0>0$ and
Fubini give zero area to each boundary curve. Their countable union,
and the fixed vertex, are null for the subsequent area argument.
\end{proof}

\section{Arbitrary approximate inputs and stopped physical witnesses}

For any input $u$, denote its actual admissible initial subset by
$V(n,\delta,u)=\{x\in Q:H_u^jx\in Q^\delta, 0\le j\le n\}$.
The natural code is a proof tool; it does not restrict these inputs.

\begin{lemma}[Physical first-deviation band]\label{lem:band}
If $u$ covers $(s_0,s_0y_0)$ and first differs from its natural
exact code at time $j$, let $a=u|_{j-1}$. There is a separating
point $b_a(s_0)\in I_a(s_0)$, depending only on that prefix and
radius, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
|y_0-b_a(s_0)|\le
C_b\delta\lambda_a^{1-\beta}/s_0,
\qquad C_b=\frac{2C_P^\beta p_-^{1-\beta}C}{1-4\ee}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $u,j$ the possible points lie on one side of this moving
boundary and have physical area at most $C_b\delta\lambda_a^{1-\beta}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common prefix is exact, and its image is the full graph of
Lemma~\ref{lem:graphs}. Let $Y_*$ be its unique separating zero
of $\Psi(Y)=Y-2P_0(S(Y),Y)+1$, and let
$b_a=Y_{a,s_0}^{-1}(Y_*)$. If the differing branch is $i$, direct
substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wrongbranch}
|Y_j|-1=|\Psi(Y)|/p_i,\qquad
|z_j|-s_j=S p_i^{\beta-1}|\Psi(Y)|.
\end{equation}
At the shared endpoint both excesses are zero; either input is
included. A necessary condition for $Q^\delta$ is
$|z_j|-s_j<2\delta$, by comparison with a point of $Q$ at
Euclidean distance less than $\delta$.
Use $S\ge C_P^{-\beta}s_0\lambda_a^\beta$ and $p_i\ge p_-$
in \eqref{eq:wrongbranch}. The derivative of
$\Psi(Y_{a,s_0}(y_0))$ is at least
$(1-4\ee)C^{-1}\lambda_a^{-1}$. The mean value theorem proves
\eqref{eq:band}. The branch $u_j$ fixes its side. Integrating the
one-sided width against $s_0\,ds_0$ cancels $1/s_0$ and gives
the asserted physical area, without a lower radial cutoff.
Later reentry does not remove the constraint at time $j$.
No cone estimate is imposed on the subsequent approximate trajectory.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal L(n,t)$ stop every branch at its first word with
$\lambda_w\le t$, or at depth $n$ if no earlier crossing occurs.
Write $N(n,t)=\#\mathcal L(n,t)$, for $0<t<1$. This is a complete
prefix-free counting tree, not an assumed optimal catalogue. It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stoppingweights}
\sum_{w\in\mathcal L(n,t)}\lambda_w=1,\qquad
\lambda_w>a_*t,\qquad N(n,t)\le(a_*t)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
These follow by replacing a parent weight by its two child weights,
and observing that every proper prefix of a leaf precedes a crossing.

\begin{lemma}[Comparison with the actual catalogue]\label{lem:catalogue}
For fixed constants $B,C_0,C_U$, all $n\ge1$ and all sufficiently
small $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realcomparison}
\frac{N(n,\delta^{1/\beta})}{1+Bn}
\le r_H(n,\delta;Q)
\le N\bigl(n,(\delta/C_0)^{1/\beta}\bigr)
\le C_U\delta^{-1/\beta}.
\end{equation}
Also $r_H(n,\delta;Q)\le2^n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the lower bound take $t=\delta^{1/\beta}$ and choose the
physical witness $(1,y_w)$ at the midpoint of each $I_w(1)$.
The intervals partition the fibre. By \eqref{eq:intervalsize}
and \eqref{eq:stoppingweights}, ordered witnesses have successive
gaps greater than $2C^{-1}a_*t$.
An arbitrary input $u$ has at most one stopping leaf as a prefix.
Every other covered witness first differs at some $j\le n$,
with the one common prefix $a=u|_{j-1}$ and $\lambda_a>t$.
At radius $1$, \eqref{eq:band} confines these witnesses to one side
of a fixed centre in a band of width at most
$C_b\delta t^{1-\beta}=C_bt$. Spacing limits their number to
$B=\lceil1+CC_b/(2a_*)\rceil$. Thus $u$ covers at most $1+Bn$
witnesses. Their midpoints are interior to every ancestor, so their
natural prefixes are precisely the chosen leaves. Any catalogue
covering $Q$ must cover all witnesses, proving the lower bound.

For the upper bound take $t=(\delta/C_0)^{1/\beta}$, use every
leaf as an exact prefix, and append the same infinite tail, say
$0^\infty$. Lemma~\ref{lem:graphs} covers every initial fibre with
all intermediate constraints. A depth-$n$ leaf needs no further
constrained time. An earlier stopped leaf has radius at most
$C_P^\beta t^\beta$, so its terminal adapted norm is at most
$MC_P^\beta t^\beta$. With $C_0=2\sqrt2 MC_P^\beta$,
\eqref{eq:origincontraction} bounds the Euclidean norm of its
entire remaining tail by $\delta/2$. This is strictly inside
$Q^\delta$ at every later required time. The apex is fixed, and
all closed endpoints have been retained. Equation
\eqref{eq:stoppingweights} gives $C_U=C_0^{1/\beta}/a_*$.
Alternatively every exactly feasible length-$n$ prefix, extended
arbitrarily, gives the $2^n$ upper bound.
\end{proof}
\section{Area-typical initial states and proof of the threshold}
\label{sec:threshold}

Let $c_k(x)$ denote the first $k$ letters of the natural exact code,
with the vertex assigned $0^\infty$. The continuous maps and separating
curves make every finite assigned-code set Borel measurable.

\begin{lemma}[Physical typical sets]\label{lem:typical}
For area-almost every $x\in Q$, the frequency of zero symbols
in $c_k(x)$ tends to $a_0$.
Every compact $K\subset Q$ of positive area contains a compact
positive-area set $T$ on which this convergence is uniform.
For every $0<\eta<h$, there is $D_\eta\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalproducts}
 D_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)k}
 \le\lambda_{c_k(x)}
 \le D_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)k}
 \quad(x\in T,\ k\ge0).
\end{equation}
For each $\zeta\in(0,1)$ such a compact $T$ can be chosen with
$\area(T)>1-\zeta$ inside $Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Normalize area on $Q$, whose area is one.
For every assigned word $w$, \eqref{eq:cylinderarea} bounds
its probability by $C\lambda_w$. Thus the area of states
whose length-$k$ prefix has zero frequency at distance at
least $\eta$ from $a_0$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typeprob}
 C(k+1)e^{-2\eta^2 k}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $t=j/k$,
put $D_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)=t\log(t/a_0)
+(1-t)\log((1-t)/a_1)$. Then
\[
 \binom{k}{j}a_0^j a_1^{k-j}
 \le e^{-kD_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)},\qquad
 D_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)\ge2(t-a_0)^2.
\]
The first inequality uses $\binom{k}{j}\le e^{kH_b(j/k)}$:
the binomial mass at $j$ with parameter $j/k$ is at most one.
The endpoint types follow by continuity. The second inequality follows from
$D_{\rm KL}''(t)=1/[t(1-t)]\ge4$, with its value and first
derivative zero at $a_0$. Summing over types gives
\eqref{eq:typeprob}. Borel--Cantelli, followed by a countable
sequence of positive $\eta$ tending to zero, proves almost
everywhere convergence. No independence of the state-dependent
digits has been used.

Remove the null set of finite-word boundary curves and the
vertex. Egorov's theorem and inner regularity of area give
a compact subset of any positive-area $K$ with positive area
and uniform frequency convergence. On $Q$ they give a
compact set of area greater than $1-\zeta$.
Since $-\log\lambda_{c_k(x)}/k$ is $\chi_a$ of the zero
frequency, it converges uniformly to $\chi_a(a_0)=h$ on each
chosen set. Absorb the finitely many early times into
$D_\eta$ to obtain \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}.
All sets are chosen before any precision exponent or sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Area covered by one arbitrary input]\label{lem:typicalarea}
If $T$ satisfies \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}, then for each
$0<\eta<h$ there is a constant $C_\eta$ such that, uniformly
in $u,n,\delta$ and $0\le m\le n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalarea}
 \area(T\cap V(n,\delta,u))
 \le C_\eta\left[e^{-(h-\eta)m}
       +\delta e^{(\beta-1)(h+\eta)m}\right].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Agreement through $m$ confines the state to $A_{u|_m}$.
If this agreement class meets $T$, its reference product is
at most $D_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}$, and
\eqref{eq:cylinderarea} gives the first term.

For a first disagreement at $j\le m$, a nonempty class in
$T$ has common prefix $a=u|_{j-1}=c_{j-1}(x)$ for some $x\in T$.
Equation \eqref{eq:typicalproducts} bounds its product from
below by $D_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)(j-1)}$.
Lemma \ref{lem:band} bounds its area by
$C_bD_\eta^{\beta-1}\delta
 e^{(\beta-1)(h+\eta)(j-1)}$.
The sum over $j\le m$ is a geometric sum with ratio
greater than one, proving the second term. Empty classes
give no contribution. Later disagreement times are already
included in the agreement class through $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
The dynamical assertions follow from Lemma \ref{lem:dynamics}.
For a given positive-area compact $K$, choose $T\subset K$
as in Lemma \ref{lem:typical}. For small $\delta$ set
\[
 m=\min\left\{n,\left\lfloor
       \frac{-\log\delta}{\beta(h+\eta)}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Then $\delta e^{\beta(h+\eta)m}\le1$, and
Lemma \ref{lem:typicalarea} gives
\[
 \area(T\cap V(n,\delta,u))\le
             2C_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}.
\]
Every catalogue for $K$ must cover $T$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowerK}
 r_H(n,\delta;K)\ge
       \frac{\area(T)}{2C_\eta}e^{(h-\eta)m}.
\end{equation}
For $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$, passage to rates and then
$\eta\downarrow0$ yields the lower bound
$\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}$. Lemma \ref{lem:catalogue} and
$K\subset Q$ give both upper bounds in \eqref{eq:universal}.
They coincide with the lower bound when $\gamma\le\beta h$.
The argument includes $\gamma=0$ and equality at the
threshold, and needs no monotonicity of $\delta_n$.

For the high side choose once a compact $T_\zeta$ from
Lemma \ref{lem:typical} with area greater than $1-\zeta$,
and set $K_\zeta=T_\zeta\cup\{(0,0)\}$.
For every $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, all
natural prefixes on $T_\zeta$ have zero frequency within
$\eta$ of $a_0$. The number of these prefixes is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalcount}
 (n+1)\exp\left[n
    \max_{\substack{0\le t\le1\\|t-a_0|\le\eta}}H_b(t)\right].
\end{equation}
Use each as an exact length-$n$ prefix with any infinite
extension, and one extra input for the vertex. This covers
$K_\zeta$ exactly for all required times, for every
$\delta>0$. Continuity of $H_b$ gives upper rate $h$.
For every finite $\gamma>\beta h$, \eqref{eq:universal}
gives lower rate $h$ on this same set. Its rate therefore
exists and equals $h$ simultaneously for all those
exponents and all their precision sequences.

It remains to obtain a strictly larger lower rate on $Q$.
Since $a_0\ne a_1$,
\[
 \Delta=\chi_a(1/2)-h
       =(a_0-1/2)\log(a_0/a_1)>0 .
\]
For a given $\gamma>\beta h$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pgamma}
 \theta_\gamma=\min\left\{\frac12,
             \frac{\gamma/\beta-h}{2\Delta}\right\},
 \qquad t_\gamma=(1-\theta_\gamma)a_0+\theta_\gamma/2 .
\end{equation}
By affinity of $\chi_a$ and strict concavity of $H_b$,
$\chi_a(t_\gamma)<\gamma/\beta$ and $H_b(t_\gamma)>h$.
Choose integers $j_n$ with $j_n/n\to t_\gamma$.
For all sufficiently large $n$, every word of length $n$
with exactly $j_n$ zeros has
\[
 -\log\lambda_w=n\chi_a(j_n/n)<-\log\delta_n/\beta.
\]
Every proper prefix has still smaller cost. All these words
are therefore terminal leaves of
$\mathcal L(n,\delta_n^{1/\beta})$.
The elementary type bound
$\binom{n}{j}\ge e^{nH_b(j/n)}/(n+1)$ and
Lemma \ref{lem:catalogue} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:highlower}
 r_H(n,\delta_n;Q)\ge
   \frac{\exp[nH_b(j_n/n)]}{(n+1)(1+Bn)}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the type bound follows since $j$ is a
mode of the binomial law with parameter $j/n$, so its
mass is at least $1/(n+1)$; endpoint types are immediate.
Taking lower rates in \eqref{eq:highlower} yields
$H_b(t_\gamma)>h$, proving \eqref{eq:high}.
The strict gap may tend to zero as $\gamma\downarrow\beta h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched linear systems]\label{cor:linear}
For
\[
 A_0(s,z)=(a_0^\beta s,a_0^{\beta-1}(z+a_1s)),\qquad
 A_1(s,z)=(a_1^\beta s,a_1^{\beta-1}(z-a_0s)),
\]
the conclusions of Theorem \ref{thm:main} hold with the
same threshold $\beta h$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $\ee=0$ in the theorem. Then $\mathcal P_w(x)=\lambda_w$
and the product and angular comparison constants are $C_P=C=1$.
\end{proof}


\section{Radius-autonomous matching and mismatch}\label{sec:radius}

We now use \eqref{eq:G}, with parameters \eqref{eq:radialepsilon}.
The quantities in this section are independent of the feedback
constants. For words let
$\lambda_w=\prod_j a_{w_j}$ and $R_w=\prod_j\rho_{w_j}$.
Write $\beta_{\min}=\min_i\beta_i$ and put
\begin{gather*}
q_-=a_*-\ee/2,\qquad q_+=1-q_-,\qquad r_R=q_+^{\beta_{\min}}<1,\\
C_R^P=\exp\!\left(\frac{\ee}{q_-(1-r_R)}\right),\qquad
C_R^s=(C_R^P)^{\beta_{\max}}.
\end{gather*}

\begin{lemma}[Actual strips and radial products]\label{lem:radialstrips}
The maps \eqref{eq:G} are global smooth diffeomorphisms and admit
a common origin-contracting norm on the whole plane. At a fixed
initial radius, each complete feasible word has a closed angular
interval $I_w(s)$, and every complete prefix-free tree partitions
that fibre with endpoints included. Along a word set
\[
\mathcal Q_w(s)=\prod_jq_{w_{j+1}}(s_j),\qquad
\mathcal R_w(s)=\prod_jq_{w_{j+1}}(s_j)^{\beta_{w_{j+1}}}.
\]
Then, uniformly in word and radius $0\le s\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialproducts}
(C_R^P)^{-1}\lambda_w\le\mathcal Q_w\le C_R^P\lambda_w,
\qquad (C_R^s)^{-1}R_w\le\mathcal R_w\le C_R^sR_w,
\end{equation}
$s_{|w|}=s\mathcal R_w(s)$ and $|I_w(s)|=2\mathcal Q_w(s)$.
Physical strip area is between $(C_R^P)^{-1}\lambda_w$ and
$C_R^P\lambda_w$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $f(s)=s/(1+s^2)$, $|f|\le1/2$ and $|sf'|\le1/2$.
Thus $g_i(s)=sq_i(s)^{\beta_i}$ has derivative
\[
g_i'(s)=q_i^{\beta_i-1}(q_i+\beta_i s q_i')>0,
\]
with the bracket at least $a_*-(\beta_{\max}+1)\ee/2>0$.
Since $q_i(s)\to a_i$ at infinity, $g_i$ is onto $\R$.
Its smooth inverse recovers $s$, then the positive coefficient
$q_i^{\beta_i-1}$ recovers $z$. This proves global invertibility.
With $M_R\ge1$, $M_R>r_R/(1-q_+^{\beta_{\min}-1})$,
the norm $\max(M_R|s|,|z|)$ contracts relative to the origin
by at most
$\max\{r_R,q_+^{\beta_{\min}-1}+r_R/M_R\}<1$.

At radius $s$, the angular maps are
$T_{0,s}(y)=(y+q_1)/q_0$ and $T_{1,s}(y)=(y-q_0)/q_1$.
The full domains are $[-1,\xi(s)]$, $[\xi(s),1]$, where
$\xi=q_0-q_1$, with inverse branches
$B_{0,s}(y)=q_0(s)y-q_1(s)$ and
$B_{1,s}(y)=q_1(s)y+q_0(s)$.
The radius after the first letter does not depend on the angle, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialintervals}
I_{iv}(s)=B_{i,s}(I_v(g_i(s))).
\end{equation}
This induction retains all intermediate constraints and gives the
partition and length assertions. Controlled feasibility follows.

The radial path obeys $s_j\le r_R^js$. Since
$|q_i(s_j)-a_i|\le\ee s_j$, summing logarithmic errors proves
\eqref{eq:radialproducts}; the radial error is at most
$\beta_{\max}$ times the angular one. Integration against $s\,ds$
gives the area bounds. Finite-word boundary curves have zero area,
and the vertex is fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relative radial-stop witness comparison]\label{lem:radialcomparison}
Let $\mathcal L_R(n,t)$ stop words at first $R_w\le t$, or at
depth $n$, and let $N_R(n,t)$ be its size. Fixed constants
$B_R,C_{0,R}$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialcomparison}
\frac{N_R(n,\delta)}{1+B_Rn}
\le r_G(n,\delta;Q)
\le N_R(n,\delta/C_{0,R})
\end{equation}
for all horizons and sufficiently small positive tolerances.
For a first difference at time $j$, common prefix $a$, an arbitrary
covered physical state lies in a one-sided band of width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialband}
|y-b_a(s)|\le C_{b,R}\delta\lambda_a/(sR_a),\qquad
C_{b,R}=2C_R^P C_R^s\max_i(a_i/\rho_i).
\end{equation}
Its physical area is at most $C_{b,R}\delta\lambda_a/R_a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The common word is exact. Its angle map is affine with slope
$\mathcal Q_a(s)^{-1}$. For the differing branch $i$, the excess
at the first difference is
\[
|y_j|-1=|y-b_a(s)|/\mathcal Q_{ai}(s),\qquad
s_j=s\mathcal R_{ai}(s).
\]
The necessary condition $|z_j|-s_j<2\delta$ and
\eqref{eq:radialproducts} give \eqref{eq:radialband}.
The side is fixed by the differing input; integration cancels $1/s$.
This includes ties and subsequent reentry.

For the lower bound use midpoint witnesses at radius $1$ for
leaves stopped at $t=\delta$. Write $\rho_* =\min_i\rho_i$.
Every stopped or capped leaf satisfies $R_w>\rho_*t$.
For any descendant $w=av$ of a fixed proper prefix $a$,
$\lambda_v\ge R_v=R_w/R_a>\rho_*t/R_a$, because $\beta_i>1$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:radialstrips}, all leaf intervals below $a$
have length greater than
$2(C_R^P)^{-1}\lambda_a\rho_*t/R_a$.
Their ordered midpoints have at least that spacing.
For one input and one first-difference time, \eqref{eq:radialband}
at $s=1$ confines these descendant witnesses to a band of width
$C_{b,R}\delta\lambda_a/R_a$. Hence at most
\[
B_R=\left\lceil1+\frac{C_{b,R}C_R^P}{2\rho_*}\right\rceil
\]
can be covered at that time. Add the one possible leaf prefix
of the input and sum over times to get $1+B_Rn$.
This is a conditional spacing comparison within the shared
prefix, rather than a global minimum-length assertion.

For the upper bound, the full leaf intervals of
\eqref{eq:radialintervals} cover each physical fibre exactly
through the leaf. Append the same infinite tail to each leaf
at $t=\delta/C_{0,R}$, with
$C_{0,R}=2\sqrt2M_R C_R^s$. An early terminal radius is
at most $C_R^st$, and the global origin-contraction norm of
Lemma~\ref{lem:radialstrips} keeps the entire tail within
Euclidean distance $\delta/2$ of the origin. Capped leaves
are exactly feasible through $n$. All endpoints and the vertex
are covered, proving \eqref{eq:radialcomparison}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:radialstrips} proves the dynamical assertions.
Write $V_G(n,\delta,u)=\{x\in Q:G_u^jx\in Q^\delta,
0\le j\le n\}$ for the actual all-time initial set.
For $0<\gamma<\gamma_0$, the bound $R_w\le e^{-\gamma_0n}$
at depth $n$ implies that every branch crosses either of the
thresholds in \eqref{eq:radialcomparison} by time $n$, eventually
for any precision sequence of exponent $\gamma$.
For an uncapped radial stopping tree, replacement of a parent
weight $R_w^D$ by its children preserves total weight, since
$\rho_0^D+\rho_1^D=1$. All leaves have
$\rho_*t<R_w\le t$, and thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialcount}
t^{-D}\le N_R(t)\le(\rho_*t)^{-D}.
\end{equation}
The comparison proves the full-set limit $\gamma D$.

By the area bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:radialstrips}, the type
probabilities of actual assigned codes are bounded by $C_R^P$
times their Bernoulli weights. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:typical}
therefore gives one uniformly typical compact $T_\zeta$ of area
greater than $1-\zeta$. Set $K_\zeta=T_\zeta\cup\{0\}$ before
choosing any exponent, sequence, or horizon. On this same set,
for each $\eta>0$ a constant $A_\eta\ge1$ satisfies
\begin{align}
A_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)k}&\le\lambda_{c_k(x)}
\le A_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)k},\nonumber\\
A_\eta^{-1}e^{-(\chi+\eta)k}&\le R_{c_k(x)}
\le A_\eta e^{-(\chi-\eta)k}.\label{eq:twotypical}
\end{align}
Both follow from the same uniform frequency convergence.
For any positive-area compact $K$, the same construction supplies
a positive-area compact $T\subset K$ with these bounds.

Agreement with an arbitrary input through $m$ has area at most
$C_R^P A_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}$. A nonempty first-difference
class at time $j\le m$ has a common typical prefix. Equations
\eqref{eq:radialband} and \eqref{eq:twotypical} bound its
area by a constant times
$\delta e^{(\chi-h+2\eta)(j-1)}$.
Since $\chi>h$, summation gives, uniformly in the input,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialtypicalarea}
\area(T\cap V_G(n,\delta,u))\le
A'_\eta[e^{-(h-\eta)m}+\delta e^{(\chi-h+2\eta)m}].
\end{equation}
For $m=\min\{n,\lfloor(-\log\delta)/(\chi+\eta)\rfloor\}$,
the second term is at most $e^{-(h-\eta)m}$.
Since $\gamma<\gamma_0\le\chi$, covering the positive area
of $T$ yields lower rate $\gamma h/\chi$, after $\eta\downarrow0$.

For the matching upper rate on $K_\zeta$, use all radial
stopping prefixes realized on $T_\zeta$, with the full tails
from Lemma~\ref{lem:radialcomparison}. Put $T=-\log\delta$
and $c_{\max}=-\log\rho_*$. Each such stop has length at most
\[
\ell\le\frac{T+\log C_{0,R}+c_{\max}+\log A_\eta}{\chi-\eta}.
\]
At a fixed length $\ell$, distinct realized words have
$\lambda_w\ge A_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)\ell}$, whereas
all length-$\ell$ word weights sum to one. Their number is
at most $A_\eta e^{(h+\eta)\ell}$. Summing up to the displayed
length, and adding one input for the apex, gives upper rate
$\gamma(h+\eta)/(\chi-\eta)$. Taking $\eta\downarrow0$
proves the second limit in \eqref{eq:mismatchrates}, on one
fixed $K_\zeta$ for all the stated sequences.

Finally let $b_i=\rho_i^D$, so $b_0+b_1=1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KLmismatch}
D\chi-h=\sum_i a_i\log(a_i/b_i)\ge0.
\end{equation}
The inequality follows from $-\log t\ge1-t$; equality holds
only when $a_i=b_i$ for both digits. This would imply
$\beta_0D=\beta_1D=1$, which is impossible under mismatch.
Hence $D>h/\chi$. The root and the strict entropy comparison
are classical counting, used only after the physical catalogue
comparison has been established.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Matched radius-autonomous subfamily]\label{prop:radialmatched}
If $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$, the system \eqref{eq:G}, on its
full range \eqref{eq:radialepsilon}, has all the rate conclusions
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, including the single compact set for
every finite high-side exponent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $R_w=\lambda_w^\beta$ and $\mathcal R_w=\mathcal Q_w^\beta$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:radialstrips} gives the full intervals and area
bounds required in Lemma~\ref{lem:typical}.
Equation \eqref{eq:radialband} becomes a one-sided physical
band of area at most $C_{b,R}\delta\lambda_a^{1-\beta}$.
Equation \eqref{eq:radialcomparison} becomes
\eqref{eq:realcomparison} with fixed constants and angular
stopping thresholds $\delta^{1/\beta}$ and
$(\delta/C_{0,R})^{1/\beta}$. Its upper count follows from
\eqref{eq:stoppingweights}; the $2^n$ exact bound also holds.
Thus the proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:typicalarea} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} use exactly these already derived
estimates: choose the same $m$ for the lower bound, one
uniformly typical compact before all exponents for the high-side
upper bound, and the type $t_\gamma$ in \eqref{eq:pgamma}
for the full-set strict lower bound. Every inequality and tail
needed for those deductions has been supplied in this section.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matching criterion within the radius-autonomous class]\label{cor:criterion}
For a fixed system \eqref{eq:G}, there is a nonempty interval
of exponents adjacent to zero on which all fixed positive-area
compact initial sets have the same rate for every corresponding
precision sequence if and only if $\beta_0=\beta_1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Under equality, Proposition~\ref{prop:radialmatched} gives
the interval $0<\gamma\le\beta h$. Under inequality,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} separates $Q$ and one fixed
nearly full-area compact set for every $0<\gamma<\gamma_0$.
\end{proof}

This criterion concerns only \eqref{eq:G}. It does not give
a necessity statement for \eqref{eq:H}, overlapping feasible
branches, or general smooth perturbations.

\section{Genuine feedback and the scope of coordinate reduction}
\label{sec:conjugacy}

\begin{proposition}[Nonnested feasible images]\label{prop:conjugacy}
For the feedback system \eqref{eq:H}, assume additionally
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonconjugacyrange}
0<|a_0-a_1|<\ee/4.
\end{equation}
There is no homeomorphism preserving $Q$ and simultaneously
conjugating both controls to the matched linear maps or to any
radius-autonomous system \eqref{eq:G}, even after permuting the
input labels. The conjugacy identities are required on $Q$,
with its domain containing $Q\cup H_0(Q)\cup H_1(Q)$.
In particular, no fixed $C^1$ or bi-Lipschitz coordinate change
of this kind reduces these feedback instances to those models.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $D_i=Q\cap H_i^{-1}Q$ and $J_i=H_i(D_i)$.
For \eqref{eq:G}, $g_i(s)=sq_i(s)^{\beta_i}$ is increasing,
independent of the angle, and the feasible angular image is
all of $[-1,1]$. Its feasible image is therefore the triangle
\[
\{(s,z):0\le s\le g_i(1),\ |z|\le s\}.
\]
The two images are nested. This also holds for the matched
linear model.

For the feedback system put $\xi=a_0-a_1$ and
$\theta_1=\theta(1)$. Equation \eqref{eq:divider} gives
$|\theta_1|\le|\xi|/(1-\ee)$.
The largest radial coordinates on the lower and upper
output rays of the two feasible images are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&z=-s&z=s\\\hline
J_0&(a_0-\ee/3)^\beta&((1+\theta_1)/2)^\beta\\
J_1&((1-\theta_1)/2)^\beta&(a_1-\ee/3)^\beta.
\end{array}
\]
To verify that these are maxima, not just sample values, note
from \eqref{eq:angularmap} that each output ray has as preimage
the relevant outer source ray or the dividing curve
$y=\theta(s)$. On an outer ray the derivative of
$sP_i(s,\pm1)^\beta$ is positive because $p_- -\beta\ee>0$.
On the divider, implicit differentiation gives
$|\theta'|\le2\ee/(1-2\ee)$ and
$|dP_i(s,\theta(s))/ds|\le\ee/(1-2\ee)$.
Thus the radial derivative is also positive there, since
$p_- -\beta\ee/(1-2\ee)>0$.
Each maximum is attained at $s=1$, with the full ray
segment from zero included. At the divider
$P_0=(1+\theta)/2$ and $P_1=(1-\theta)/2$; on the
outer rays the feedback term at $s=1$ is $\pm\ee/3$.
These facts establish the table.

Put $d=(\xi+\theta_1)/2$. Under
\eqref{eq:nonconjugacyrange},
$|d|\le |\xi|/(1-\ee)<\ee/3$, since
\eqref{eq:feedbackepsilon} implies $\ee<1/64$.
The difference of the lower-ray bases is $d-\ee/3<0$,
whereas that of the upper-ray bases is $d+\ee/3>0$.
Hence $J_1$ extends farther on the lower ray and $J_0$
farther on the upper ray. Their images are not nested.

A simultaneous homeomorphism taking $Q$ onto $Q$ maps
$D_i$ and $J_i$ to the corresponding target feasible
domains and images. This follows from the conjugacy identities
on $Q$ and bijectivity; membership in $Q$ is preserved in
both directions. Such a map preserves inclusion, even with
label permutation. It cannot change nonnested images into
nested ones, proving the proposition.
\end{proof}

The additional range is nonempty: take $a_0,a_1$ sufficiently
close to $1/2$ and unequal, and choose a permitted $\ee$
with $4|a_0-a_1|<\ee$. Outside that range this argument
does not decide coordinate reduction. It also does not exclude
changes of coordinates that change the constraint set. Neither
qualification restricts the boundary theorem on its full range.
\section{Local matching does not suffice}
\label{sec:local}

The following example keeps the common attracting origin
and even agrees with the linear maps on a neighbourhood
of it. It violates global injectivity by collapsing
positive-area regions onto an invariant ray.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:collapse}
For every choice of $a_0,a_1,\beta$ in
\eqref{eq:parameters} and every $0<\sigma<1/2$, there are
$C^\infty$ maps $S_0,S_1:\R^2\to\R^2$ with these properties:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item They equal $A_0,A_1$ on $\{s\le\sigma\}$, so
       all derivatives at the origin agree.
 \item All inputs drive bounded sets uniformly
       exponentially to the origin, and $Q$ is
       controlled invariant.
 \item The compact set
       $K_\sigma=Q\cap\{s\ge2\sigma\}$ has area
       $1-4\sigma^2$ and $r_S(n,\delta;K_\sigma)=1$
       for every $n$ and every $\delta>0$.
 \item For every finite $\gamma\ge0$ and every sequence
       $\delta_n\to0$ with $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$,
       the full-set cost satisfies
       \[
       \min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}
       \le\liminf\frac{\log r_S(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
       \le\limsup\frac{\log r_S(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
       \le\min\{\log2,\gamma/\beta\}.
       \]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for $0<\gamma\le\beta h$ the full-set rate
is $\gamma/\beta$, but the positive-area set $K_\sigma$
has rate zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $e(t)=0$ for $t\le0$ and $e(t)=\exp(-1/t)$ for $t>0$,
and define
\[
 \psi(s)=\frac{e(s-\sigma)}
               {e(s-\sigma)+e(2\sigma-s)}.
\]
The denominator is positive everywhere. Thus $\psi$ is
smooth, zero for $s\le\sigma$, and one for $s\ge2\sigma$.
Put $\rho_i=a_i^\beta$ and define
\begin{align}
 S_0(s,z)&=\bigl(\rho_0s,\
 (1-\psi(s))a_0^{\beta-1}(z+a_1s)-\psi(s)\rho_0s\bigr),
       \label{eq:S0}\\
 S_1(s,z)&=\bigl(\rho_1s,\
 (1-\psi(s))a_1^{\beta-1}(z-a_0s)+\psi(s)\rho_1s\bigr).
       \label{eq:S1}
\end{align}
They have the claimed local identity.
Each $S_i(s,z)$ is a convex combination of $A_i(s,z)$
and $R_i(s,z)$, where
$R_0=(\rho_0s,-\rho_0s)$ and
$R_1=(\rho_1s,\rho_1s)$.
All four linear maps contract toward zero in one adapted
norm $\max\{M|s|,|z|\}$ for sufficiently large $M$:
the second-coordinate factor for $A_i$ is at most
$a_i^{\beta-1}+a_i^{\beta-1}a_{1-i}/M<1$, and
$R_i$ has factor at most $\rho_i$.
Convexity of the norm gives the same contraction bound
for $S_i$ on all of $\R^2$.

For controlled feasibility let $t=\psi(s)$, $y=z/s$,
and $\xi=a_0-a_1$. If $t<1$ the full feasible intervals
are
\begin{align*}
 J_0(s)&=\left[-1,\min\left\{1,
                         \xi+\frac{2a_0t}{1-t}\right\}\right],\\
 J_1(s)&=\left[\max\left\{-1,
                         \xi-\frac{2a_1t}{1-t}\right\},1\right].
\end{align*}
They follow by imposing $-1\le z'/s'\le1$ in
\eqref{eq:S0}--\eqref{eq:S1}, and their union is
$[-1,1]$. For $t=1$ both branches are feasible on the
whole fibre. The radius always decreases, so $Q$ is
controlled invariant.

On $K_\sigma$, one application of $S_0$ sends every
state to the lower ray. At every radius
$S_0(s,-s)=(\rho_0s,-\rho_0s)$, so input $0^\infty$
keeps the whole set exactly in $Q$. Its area is
$\int_{2\sigma}^1 2s\,ds=1-4\sigma^2$.

To obtain the full-set lower bound, use
$Q_c=Q\cap\{s\le c\}$ with $c=\sigma/2$.
For every input, the radius of a state in $Q_c$ stays
at most $c$. Thus $\psi=0$ forever along that trajectory,
even when its angle leaves $[-1,1]$, and its entire
trajectory is exactly that of $A_i$.
Consequently
$r_S(n,\delta;Q)\ge r_A(n,\delta;Q_c)$.
Corollary \ref{cor:linear} and \eqref{eq:universal}
give the lower bound.

For the upper bound choose an integer $t_0$ such that
$(\max_i\rho_i)^{t_0}\le\sigma$.
Every initial state admits an exact feasible prefix of
length $t_0$, ending in the region $s\le\sigma$.
All subsequent trajectories there coincide with the
linear system, for all inputs, since the radius continues
to decrease independently of the angle.
Concatenate all $2^{t_0}$ possible prefixes with a
catalogue for the linear full set over the remaining
horizon. For $n\ge t_0$ this gives
\[
 r_S(n,\delta;Q)\le
       2^{t_0}r_A(n-t_0,\delta;Q)
       \le 2^{t_0}C_U\delta^{-1/\beta}.
\]
The $2^n$ exact-prefix bound also holds.
These yield the upper rates for every stated precision
sequence. Finally
$\det DS_i=(1-\psi(s))a_i^{2\beta-1}$ vanishes on the
saturated region, identifying the noninjective mechanism.
\end{proof}

For fixed $\sigma$ this proposition gives one large
zero-cost initial set in one fixed system. Making its
area arbitrarily close to one changes $\sigma$ and the
system. This differs from Theorem \ref{thm:main}, where
all $K_\zeta$ are chosen inside a single fixed system.
The collapse example is a counterexample to sufficiency
of local matching and uniform convergence; it is not a
counterexample to the reversible theorem.


\section{Scope and consequences}

The common-cost boundary is a property of actual initial-set
coverage, rather than convergence of controlled states alone.
Below the boundary, a positive-area typical portion supplies
a lower bound for every compact set, including sets with empty
interior. The singular $1/s$ in the first-deviation band is
cancelled by the physical area element. Above the boundary,
rare terminal types force more controls for the full set, while
one uniformly typical compact set excludes those persistent
deviations. Its selection precedes all precision exponents.

Angular feedback removes the shared-radial-trajectory property
of radius-autonomous words. The graph cone, complete feasible
intervals, derivative comparison, and physical first-deviation
band supply the missing connection to arbitrary approximate
controls. The topological obstruction shows genuine instances
of this extension within its stated subrange. The separate
radial mismatch theorem makes precise how unequal matching
powers destroy an initial common-cost interval. Its pressure
root is meaningful for the real catalogue only after the
relative-prefix spacing comparison has been proved.

The conclusions retain important restrictions. The feedback
family has a common instantaneous power, nonoverlapping
feasible branches, and a specific small perturbation with
summable effect under origin contraction. We prove no
general perturbation theorem from invertibility or a matching
first derivative alone. The matching criterion belongs to
the radius-autonomous class; necessity in an unequal-power
angular-feedback class is not established here. The full-set
high-precision bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} do not give
an exact spectrum or its limit. These limitations concern the
generality of the result, not gaps in the stated proofs.
