\begin{abstract}
We study the number of open-loop controls required to keep every actual initial
state near a constraint set when the tolerance decreases with the time horizon.
For a planar linear family whose feasible branches have different contraction
rates, we determine the cost for every compact initial set with interior.
We also construct compact initial sets with area arbitrarily close to that of
the constraint and a strictly smaller cost, although their exact and outer invariance rates
are unchanged. The lower bounds apply to arbitrary approximate controls,
including trajectories that leave the constraint set. A separate theorem for
common radial dynamics gives one precision law for all positive-area compact
initial sets, allowing nonlinear transverse terms. Finally, a fixed example
with an expanding branch is resolved by cyclically selected symbolic witnesses.
The results distinguish the geometry of actual controlled feasibility from
the classical symbolic counting used to evaluate its cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Invariance entropy counts the controls needed to preserve a state constraint.
Its exact version requires membership in the constraint set at every time,
whereas its outer version first fixes a positive tolerance and then lets the
time horizon increase. These two requirements can have different asymptotic
costs. A joint limit, in which the tolerance decreases as the horizon grows,
asks a more quantitative question: how much of the exact feasibility
information remains necessary at a specified ambient resolution?

We answer this question for two classes of planar controlled systems. The
main result concerns two linear maps preserving a forward cone by an
appropriate choice of input. Both maps are contractions in a common norm,
but their contraction factors differ. For every compact initial set with
interior, the cost is determined by the largest symbolic entropy attainable
before contraction reaches the prescribed tolerance. In the same system,
compact initial sets with area arbitrarily close to that of the whole cone
can have a strictly smaller cost. Their exact invariance rate is nevertheless
the same, and their ordinary outer rate is still zero. Thus nearly full area
does not determine the joint precision--horizon cost.

The distinction is between worst-case feasible contraction along all symbolic
prefixes and the uniform typical contraction on a large compact subset.
Neither symbolic calculation alone gives a lower bound for the control
problem: an approximate control need not follow the exactly feasible branch.
We therefore estimate the actual states that an arbitrary control can cover
after its first incorrect branch choice. For initial sets with interior,
one control covers only linearly many witnesses from an appropriate stopped
tree. For large compact sets of uniformly typical points, the same first
deviation gives a sectional measure bound. These are the steps that connect
classical counting to the ambient constrained dynamics.

For comparison, we prove a different theorem with common radial dynamics and
a strict inward control margin. It allows nonlinear transverse terms and
gives the same cost for every positive-area compact initial set. Its
assumptions are not contained in those of the binary linear family, or
conversely. The comparison identifies two distinct mechanisms without
claiming a general dichotomy. A final example with one expanding branch
shows how to recover a matching lower bound when the stopped-interval
argument no longer applies.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

The control-catalogue formulation and the distinction between exact and
outer invariance are standard; see Kawan~\cite{Kawan2013}, especially
Sections 2.1 and 3.2. Volume growth and control selection already enter
invariance-entropy estimates. For hyperbolic control sets, Da Silva and
Kawan~\cite{DSK2016} express the cost through unstable volume growth under
their control-set hypotheses. Our forward cone does not satisfy those
hypotheses: a positive radial coordinate only decreases, so states cannot
approximately reach larger radii within the cone.

Chen and Zhong~\cite{CZ2024} study the finite-horizon outer invariance
complexity of subsets of a controlled invariant set. Their catalogue
already ranges over actual controls and uses an ambient neighborhood of
the constraint. Their Theorem 2.12 characterizes bounded complexity at
each fixed tolerance by finite outer equi-invariability. Our contracting
family has this boundedness property. The question here is quantitative:
how the catalogue grows when tolerance and horizon vary together, and
whether nearly full area forces the same rate as interior.

Precision requirements also occur in entropy for exponential stabilization
\cite{Colonius2012}, practical stabilization~\cite{CH2021}, and state
estimation~\cite{LM2018}. In stabilization the bound is imposed relative to
a target and changes with the current time. In state estimation it bounds
the discrepancy from a reference trajectory. Here the initial state is
never approximated: a selected control must keep its actual trajectory
within one tolerance of a fixed set throughout the entire horizon. Only
between horizons does that tolerance change.

Yao's preprint~\cite{Yao2026} gives a continuous-time construction with a
Cantor matching constraint, positive exact cost and zero approximate cost.
Its matching and fading estimates show why late control disagreements may
disappear at fixed ambient resolution. We determine the joint limit in a
positive-area planar constraint and prove its strict dependence on the
initial set, even among compact sets of nearly full area.

The symbolic tools have a separate, established history. Stopping at a
product scale and entropy-to-contraction ratios occur in self-similar
geometry~\cite{Hutchinson1981}; the binomial estimates used here are
elementary instances of the method of types~\cite{Csiszar1998}.
Dimension constructions for control entropy were developed by Huang and
Zhong~\cite{HZ2018}, and Bowen equations for invariance pressure on
partitions by Yang et al.~\cite{YCYZ2025}. Measure-theoretic invariance
entropy and variational principles are studied in
\cite{WHS2019,NWH2022}. Our large-set assertion keeps the statewise
covering requirement on one fixed compact set; it does not define a new
measure entropy. The pressure formulas in~\cite{YCYZ2025} are calculated
relative to invariant partitions. To use symbolic counting here, one
must first compare it with the infimum over all actual controls satisfying
an ambient tolerance. Proposition~\ref{prop:tree} supplies this
comparison with symbolic covers in the contracting binary family;
Section~\ref{sec:large} gives the separate estimate on nearly full-area
sets. The minimum-partial-sum rotation in the last example is a classical cyclic
argument; see~\cite{DZ1990}.

\section{The catalogue problem and the main result}\label{sec:main}

Let $U$ be a finite alphabet and let $f_i:\R^2\to\R^2$, $i\in U$, be
continuous maps. All sequences $u=(u_1,u_2,\ldots)\in U^{\N}$ are allowed.
Put $\varphi(0,x,u)=x$ and
$\varphi(k,x,u)=f_{u_k}\circ\cdots\circ f_{u_1}(x)$ for $k\ge1$.
Throughout, the constraint and metric are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone}
 Q=\{(s,z):0\le s\le1,\ |z|\le s\},\qquad \dist=\dist_2.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\area(Q)=1$. Write
$Q^\delta=\{x:\dist_2(x,Q)<\delta\}$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:count}
For compact nonempty $K\subset Q$, integer $n\ge1$, and $\delta>0$, let
$r(n,\delta;K)$ be the least cardinality of a finite set
$\mathcal C\subset U^{\N}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quantifiers}
 \forall x\in K\quad\exists u\in\mathcal C\quad
 \forall k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:\quad \varphi(k,x,u)\in Q^\delta.
\end{equation}
Let $r_E(n;K)$ be the corresponding exact count with $Q^\delta$ replaced
by $Q$. Set both counts equal to one at $n=0$, and set a count equal to
$+\infty$ if no finite catalogue exists.
The outer rate is
\[
 h_{\mathrm{out}}(K)=\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
          \frac1n\ln r(n,\delta;K).
\]
All logarithms are natural. We evaluate the joint limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:precision}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r(n,\delta_n;K),\qquad
 \delta_n>0,\quad\delta_n\to0,\quad
 -\frac1n\ln\delta_n\to\gamma\in[0,\infty].
\end{equation}
No monotonicity of $\delta_n$ is assumed.
\end{definition}

With the notation of Chen and Zhong~\cite[Section 2.2]{CZ2024}, our
count is exactly $r_{\mathrm{inv}}(n+1,\delta,K,Q)$; the shift accounts
for their time interval $[0,n)$ and our interval $\{0,\ldots,n\}$.
Only the first $n$ input symbols affect a horizon $n$. There are $n+1$
constrained states, including the actual initial state. A word meeting
\eqref{eq:quantifiers} may leave $Q$; it need not agree with any particular
exactly feasible coding.

Our main family is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary}
 F_i(s,z)=(\rho_i s,\rho_i(2z+v_i s)),\qquad
 v_0=1,\quad v_1=-1,\quad 0<\rho_0,\rho_1<\tfrac12.
\end{equation}
These are smooth linear diffeomorphisms. Define
\begin{gather*}
 \alpha=\ln2,\qquad c_i=-\ln\rho_i,\qquad
 \bar c=(c_0+c_1)/2,\qquad c_{\min}=\min(c_0,c_1),\quad
 c_{\max}=\max(c_0,c_1),\\
 H(p)=-p\ln p-(1-p)\ln(1-p),\qquad
 \chi(p)=pc_0+(1-p)c_1,\qquad 0\ln0=0,\\
 \mathcal F(\gamma)=\max_{0\le p\le1}
       H(p)\min\{1,\gamma/\chi(p)\},\qquad
 \mathcal G(\gamma)=\alpha\min\{1,\gamma/\bar c\}.
\end{gather*}
At $\gamma=\infty$ the minima are interpreted as one.

\begin{theorem}[Dependence on the initial set]\label{thm:main}
For the family \eqref{eq:binary}, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is controlled invariant. Every trajectory from a bounded set
converges to the origin uniformly over all input sequences. For every
compact $K\subset Q$ with positive area,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r_E(n;K)=\alpha,
 \qquad h_{\mathrm{out}}(K)=0.
\]
\item For every compact $K\subset Q$ with nonempty interior in $\R^2$,
and every tolerance sequence satisfying \eqref{eq:precision},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interiorlaw}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r(n,\delta_n;K)=\mathcal F(\gamma).
\end{equation}
\item For every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is a compact
$K_\varepsilon\subset Q$ with $\area(K_\varepsilon)>1-\varepsilon$
such that, for every tolerance sequence satisfying \eqref{eq:precision},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:largelaw}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r(n,\delta_n;K_\varepsilon)
       =\mathcal G(\gamma).
\end{equation}
The same $K_\varepsilon$ works for all $\gamma$ and all such sequences.
\item If $\rho_0\ne\rho_1$, then
$\mathcal F(\gamma)>\mathcal G(\gamma)$ for
$0<\gamma\le c_{\min}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This theorem does not classify arbitrary positive-area compact sets.
The sets in part (iii) have empty interior. They show that even arbitrarily
small loss of area can change the rate, while part (ii) ensures that every
set containing an open subset retains the full rate.

\begin{example}\label{ex:golden}
Take $\rho_0=1/4$, $\rho_1=1/16$, and $\delta_n=2^{-n}$.
Writing $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
\[
 \mathcal F(\ln2)=\tfrac12\ln\phi
       >\tfrac13\ln2=\mathcal G(\ln2).
\]
Both classes of initial sets have exact rate $\ln2$ and outer rate zero.
The computation and the strict inequality are justified in
Section~\ref{sec:gap}.
\end{example}

\section{Feasible words and arbitrary approximate controls}\label{sec:geometry}

For a finite word $w=w_1\cdots w_k$, put
\[
 A_w=\prod_{j=1}^k\rho_{w_j},\qquad S(w)=-\ln A_w,
 \qquad A_{\varnothing}=1.
\]
For $s>0$ use $y=z/s$ only as a calculation coordinate. Under $F_i$ the
ratio becomes $2y+v_i$, and the radius after $w$ is $sA_w$. The inverse
ratio branches
\[
 b_0(y)=(y-1)/2,\qquad b_1(y)=(y+1)/2
\]
map $[-1,1]$ onto its two halves. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinders}
 I_w=b_{w_1}\circ\cdots\circ b_{w_k}([-1,1]),\qquad |I_w|=2^{1-k},
\end{equation}
is precisely the set of ratios exactly feasible through the word $w$.
The intervals at each depth cover $[-1,1]$ with disjoint interiors.
Shared endpoints have no effect on any count or measure estimate below.
This proves controlled invariance, including at the fixed origin.

Choose $M\ge1$ so large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-norm}
 L=\rho_{\max}(2+1/M)<1,\qquad
 \norm{(s,z)}_M=\max\{M|s|,|z|\},\qquad
 \rho_{\max}=\max_i\rho_i.
\end{equation}
Each $F_i$ has operator norm at most $L$ in this norm, and
$\norm{x}_2\le\sqrt2\norm{x}_M$. This proves the uniform convergence
claim in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i). A length-$n$ exact word covers a cone
slice of area $\int_0^1s|I_w|\,ds=2^{-n}$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactbinary}
 \area(K)2^n\le r_E(n;K)\le2^n
\end{equation}
for positive-area $K\subset Q$. For fixed $\delta>0$, choose a fixed $m$
with $M\rho_{\max}^m<\delta/(2\sqrt2)$. Follow an exactly feasible prefix
of length $m$ and append any fixed tail. Every later state has Euclidean
norm less than $\delta/2$. The catalogue has at most $2^m$ elements for
all $n\ge m$, proving the outer assertion.

\subsection{Comparison with a stopped tree}

For $0<t<1$, stop every binary path at the first word $w$ satisfying
$A_w\le t$, or at depth $n$ if no earlier prefix meets this condition.
Let $\mathcal L(n,t)$ be the complete prefix-free leaf set and
$N(n,t)=|\mathcal L(n,t)|$. Write $w^-$ for the word obtained by deleting
the last symbol of $w$. For every leaf,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf-scale}
 A_{w^-}>t,\qquad A_w>\rho_{\min}t,\qquad
 \rho_{\min}=\min_i\rho_i.
\end{equation}
A leaf of depth less than $n$ also has $A_w\le t$.

\begin{proposition}[Actual controls versus stopped words]\label{prop:tree}
There are constants $B,C_0>0$, depending only on the maps, such that
for all $n\ge1$ and all sufficiently small $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:treecomparison}
 \frac{N(n,\delta)}{1+Bn}\le r(n,\delta;Q)
                \le N(n,\delta/C_0).
\end{equation}
If compact $K\subset Q$ has interior, there are a radius $s_*>0$ and a
fixed word $a$ of length $d$ such that, for $n>d$ and sufficiently small
$\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subtreecomparison}
 \frac{N(n-d,\delta/(s_*A_a))}{1+Bn}
       \le r(n,\delta;K)\le N(n,\delta/C_0).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $C_0=2\sqrt2M$. For each leaf of $\mathcal L(n,\delta/C_0)$,
use that word followed by a fixed tail. A leaf reaching $n$ is exactly
feasible for its cylinder throughout the horizon. At an earlier stopping
time the endpoint has $M$-norm at most $M\delta/C_0$; by
\eqref{eq:common-norm} its whole tail stays within Euclidean distance
$\delta/2$ of the origin. This proves both upper bounds.

For the lower bound fix $s_*\in(0,1]$, take $t=\delta/s_*<1$, and
associate to each leaf its midpoint witness $(s_*,s_*y_w)$,
where $y_w$ is the midpoint of $I_w$. A necessary condition for
$(r,z)\in Q^\delta$ with $r\ge0$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euclideannecessary}
 |z|<r+2\delta.
\end{equation}
Indeed a point $(r',z')\in Q$ within distance $\delta$ has
$|r-r'|<\delta$ and $|z-z'|<\delta$.

Consider any input word $u$ of length $n$ that covers some of these
witnesses in the sense of \eqref{eq:quantifiers}. At most one leaf is a
prefix of $u$. For any other covered witness let $j\le|w|$ be the first
position where $w_j\ne u_j$, and put $a'=u_1\cdots u_{j-1}$.
If $m_{a'}$ is the midpoint of $I_{a'}$, applying the wrong branch gives
\[
 |y_j|-1=2^j|y_w-m_{a'}|.
\]
Its actual radius is $s_*A_{a'}\rho_{u_j}$. Hence
\eqref{eq:euclideannecessary} confines $y_w$ to the opposite child in a
one-sided band of width at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
 b_j=\frac{2\delta}{s_*2^jA_{a'}\rho_{\min}}.
\end{equation}
Every leaf in that child has
\begin{align}
 |I_w|&=|I_{a'}|2^{-(|w|-|a'|)}
       \ge |I_{a'}|\frac{A_w}{A_{a'}}
       >\frac{2^{2-j}\rho_{\min}\delta}{s_*A_{a'}}=:\ell_j.
       \label{eq:leaflength}
\end{align}
The first inequality uses $\rho_i<1/2$, and the last one uses
\eqref{eq:leaf-scale}. Midpoints of disjoint-interior intervals of length
at least $\ell_j$ are separated by at least $\ell_j$. Thus the band
\eqref{eq:band} contains at most
$1+b_j/\ell_j\le1+(2\rho_{\min}^2)^{-1}$ such midpoints.
Summing over $j$ shows that one arbitrary control covers at most $1+Bn$
witnesses. Taking $s_*=1$ proves \eqref{eq:treecomparison}.

If $K$ has interior, a ball inside $K$ contains a fixed-radius segment
$\{(s_*,s_*y):y\in I_a\}$ for some dyadic interval $I_a$.
For $\delta/s_*<A_a$, the leaves below $a$ in
$\mathcal L(n,\delta/s_*)$ are $a$ concatenated with
$\mathcal L(n-d,\delta/(s_*A_a))$. Their witnesses lie in $K$.
The preceding bound still applies if $u$ first disagrees within $a$.
This proves \eqref{eq:subtreecomparison}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound uses an error at an intermediate time. It imposes no
condition on the approximate trajectory after that time, and in particular
does not presume that approximate controls follow the exact coding.

\section{Counting stopped words}\label{sec:count}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:types}
For integers $k\ge1$ and $0\le j\le k$,
\[
 \frac{e^{kH(j/k)}}{k+1}\le\binom{k}{j}\le e^{kH(j/k)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0<j<k$, set $p=j/k$. The probability of $j$ successes in a
binomial distribution with parameters $k,p$ is
$\binom{k}{j}e^{-kH(p)}$. It is at most one and is a mode, as the ratios
of adjacent probabilities show. A largest one of $k+1$ probabilities is
at least $1/(k+1)$. The endpoint cases are immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:stoppinglimit}
If $t_n\to0$ and $-n^{-1}\ln t_n\to\gamma\in[0,\infty]$, then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln N(n,t_n)=\mathcal F(\gamma).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $T_n=-\ln t_n$. A word of length $k$ with $j$ zeros has cost
$k\chi(j/k)$. An early leaf satisfies
\[
 T_n\le k\chi(j/k)<T_n+c_{\max};
\]
a terminal leaf of length $n$ satisfies $n\chi(j/n)<T_n+c_{\max}$.
Suppose first that $0<\gamma<\infty$. There are at most $(n+1)^2$
length--type pairs. Bound the number of leaves of each pair by
Lemma~\ref{lem:types}, allowing all words of that type. Along a
subsequence maximizing the exponential count, pass to limits
$k/n\to a$ and $j/k\to p$. For early leaves $a\chi(p)=\gamma$, and
the exponent is at most $aH(p)$. For terminal leaves $a=1$ and
$\chi(p)\le\gamma$, so it is at most $H(p)$. Both are bounded by
$\mathcal F(\gamma)$; the polynomial number of types does not change
the exponential rate.

Conversely fix $p\in[0,1]$ and
$0<a<\min\{1,\gamma/\chi(p)\}$. Choose $k_n=\lfloor an\rfloor$
and $j_n/k_n\to p$. All words of this type have cost less than $T_n$
for large $n$. Costs are positive, so no prefix has stopped. Distinct
words have disjoint descendant leaf sets, and therefore
$N(n,t_n)\ge\binom{k_n}{j_n}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:types} gives a lower
rate $aH(p)$. Increase $a$ to the indicated minimum and maximize over
$p$.

If $\gamma=0$, every path stops by depth
$\lceil T_n/c_{\min}\rceil+1$, unless it reaches the earlier horizon.
The number of leaves is bounded by $2^{\min(n,\lceil T_n/c_{\min}\rceil+1)}$,
which has zero rate. If $\gamma=\infty$, eventually
$T_n>nc_{\max}$, and every path reaches depth $n$; then $N(n,t_n)=2^n$.
\end{proof}

Fixed multiplicative changes in $t_n$ and fixed shifts of the horizon
do not alter this limit. Proposition~\ref{prop:tree} now proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii).

\section{Nearly full area with a smaller cost}\label{sec:large}

\subsection{A compact set of uniformly typical ratios}

For a non-dyadic $y\in(-1,1)$, let $i_1(y)i_2(y)\cdots$ be its unique
feasible coding. Under normalized Lebesgue measure on $[-1,1]$ these
digits are independent and uniformly distributed, since every
length-$k$ cylinder has measure $2^{-k}$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typical}
 \frac{S_k(y)}k:=\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kc_{i_j(y)}\longrightarrow\bar c
 \quad\text{for almost every }y.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the moment-generating function of the number of zeros,
together with $\cosh t\le e^{t^2/2}$, gives a probability at most
$2e^{-2k\eta^2}$ for a frequency deviation exceeding $\eta$.
Borel--Cantelli for $\eta=1/m$, $m\ge1$, proves \eqref{eq:typical}.

Egorov's theorem and inner regularity give compact sets
$E\subset(-1,1)$, excluding all dyadic points, of measure as close to
$2$ as desired, on which \eqref{eq:typical} is uniform. For every
$\zeta>0$ there is $C_\zeta\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformtypical}
 C_\zeta^{-1}e^{-(\bar c+\zeta)k}
 \le A_k(y)\le C_\zeta e^{-(\bar c-\zeta)k}
 \quad(y\in E,\ k\ge0).
\end{equation}
Indeed uniform convergence gives these bounds for large $k$, and a
single constant handles the finitely many earlier times.

Choose $s_0\in(0,1)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:largeK}
 K_E=\{(s,sy):s_0\le s\le1,\ y\in E\}.
\end{equation}
It is compact and has area $(1-s_0^2)|E|/2$, since the coordinate map
$(s,y)\mapsto(s,sy)$ has Jacobian $s$. Taking
$s_0^2<\varepsilon/2$ and $|E|>2(1-\varepsilon/2)$ gives
$\area(K_E)>1-\varepsilon$. The set has empty interior because every
ratio interval contains a dyadic point absent from $E$.

\subsection{Matching estimates for arbitrary controls}

Fix $0<\zeta<\bar c-\alpha$. Let $T=-\ln\delta$. For the upper bound
use the natural prefixes meeting $E$ at length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalupperlength}
 m=\min\left\{n,
 \left\lceil\frac{T+\ln(2\sqrt2MC_\zeta)}{\bar c-\zeta}\right\rceil
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
There are at most $2^m$ of them. If $m<n$, the endpoint has $M$-norm at
most $MC_\zeta e^{-(\bar c-\zeta)m}\le\delta/(2\sqrt2)$.
Append any fixed tail and use \eqref{eq:common-norm}. If $m=n$, use the
exact catalogue. Thus the upper rate is at most
$\alpha\min\{1,\gamma/(\bar c-\zeta)\}$, with the usual convention
at infinity.

For the lower bound consider any control $u$, any fixed $s\in[s_0,1]$,
and any $m\le n$. The ratios in $E$ that agree with $u$ through time
$m$ lie in one interval of length $2^{1-m}$. If the first disagreement
is at $j\le m$, the common prefix satisfies
\eqref{eq:uniformtypical}. The same first-deviation calculation as in
\eqref{eq:band} bounds the measure of those ratios by
\[
 C\delta\,2^{-j}e^{(\bar c+\zeta)(j-1)},
\]
where $C$ depends on $s_0,\zeta$ and the maps, but not on $u,s,j,n$
or $\delta$. Summing this geometric progression, using
$\bar c+\zeta>\alpha$, shows that the set of ratios covered by $u$
has measure at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalsection}
 C'\bigl(2^{-m}+\delta e^{(\bar c+\zeta-\alpha)m}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Fubini's theorem and the Jacobian $s\le1$ give the same bound, up to a
constant, for the area of points of $K_E$ covered by one control.

For sufficiently small $\delta$ choose
$m=\min\{n,\lfloor T/(\bar c+\zeta)\rfloor\}$. Since
$\delta e^{(\bar c+\zeta)m}\le1$, the second term in
\eqref{eq:typicalsection} is at most $2^{-m}$. Positive area then gives
$r(n,\delta;K_E)\ge b_\zeta2^m$ for some $b_\zeta>0$. Applied to
\eqref{eq:precision}, this gives the lower rate
$\alpha\min\{1,\gamma/(\bar c+\zeta)\}$. Letting $\zeta\downarrow0$
in both estimates proves \eqref{eq:largelaw}, including $\gamma=0$
and $\gamma=\infty$. The construction of $E$ preceded the choice of
$\zeta$ and of the tolerance sequence, so the required quantifier order
holds.

\subsection{Strict separation}\label{sec:gap}

Let $D>0$ be the unique solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimensionroot}
 \rho_0^D+\rho_1^D=1.
\end{equation}
Set $p_D=\rho_0^D$. The nonnegativity of relative entropy gives
\[
 H(p)-D\chi(p)
 =-p\ln\frac{p}{p_D}-(1-p)\ln\frac{1-p}{1-p_D}\le0,
\]
with equality at $p=p_D$. Consequently $\max_p H(p)/\chi(p)=D$,
and for $0\le\gamma\le c_{\min}$,
\[
 \mathcal F(\gamma)=D\gamma,\qquad
 \mathcal G(\gamma)=\frac{\alpha}{\bar c}\gamma.
\]
If $c_0\ne c_1$, strict arithmetic--geometric mean at
$D_0=\alpha/\bar c$ gives
$e^{-D_0c_0}+e^{-D_0c_1}>2e^{-D_0\bar c}=1$.
The left side decreases in $D$, so $D>D_0$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iv) and completes its proof.

In Example~\ref{ex:golden}, setting $x=4^{-D}$ in
\eqref{eq:dimensionroot} gives $x+x^2=1$, whence
$D=\ln\phi/(2\ln2)$ and $\bar c=3\ln2$.
The inequality $\phi^3=2\phi+1>4$ proves the claimed strict gap.
The equation \eqref{eq:dimensionroot} is classical; its role here is to
evaluate a rate whose comparison with actual controls has already been
proved.

\section{A common radial law for all positive-area sets}\label{sec:radial}

We now consider a separate class. Let $q>1$ and let $V\subset\R$ be a
finite nonempty set for which there exists $\eta>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
 \forall y\in[-1,1]\quad\exists v\in V:\quad |qy+v|\le1-2\eta.
\end{equation}
Let $R:\R\to\R$ be an increasing $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialR}
 R(0)=0,\qquad 0<R(s)<s\ (0<s\le1),\qquad
 \rho=R'(0)\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
For each $v\in V$ let $P_v:\R^2\to\R$ be smooth, with
$P_v(0)=0$ and $DP_v(0)=0$, and assume
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pbound}
 \kappa:=\max_{v\in V}\sup_{x\in\R^2}
 \max\{|P_v(x)|,\norm{DP_v(x)},\norm{D^2P_v(x)}\}
       <\min\{\eta,q/2\}.
\end{equation}
Derivative norms in this hypothesis are Euclidean operator norms. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialmaps}
 f_v(s,z)=\left(R(s),t(s)\,[qz+sv+P_v(s,z)]\right),\qquad
 t(s)=\begin{cases}R(s)/s,&s\ne0,\\ \rho,&s=0.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Use Definition~\ref{def:count} with these maps, the same $Q$, and all
sequences in $V^{\N}$ allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Common radial precision law]\label{thm:radial}
Under \eqref{eq:margin}--\eqref{eq:Pbound}, the maps
\eqref{eq:radialmaps} are smooth diffeomorphisms and $Q$ is controlled
invariant. For every compact positive-area $K\subset Q$ and every
tolerance sequence satisfying \eqref{eq:precision}, the limit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radiallaw}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r(n,\delta_n;K)
 =\Psi_{\alpha,c}(\gamma)
 :=\max_{0\le a\le1}\{a\alpha-\pos{ac-\gamma}\},
 \qquad \alpha=\ln q,\quad c=-\ln\rho.
\end{equation}
Here $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$. For finite $\gamma$, this equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialcases}
 \Psi_{\alpha,c}(\gamma)=
 \begin{cases}
 \alpha,&c=0,\\
 \min\{\alpha,\alpha-c+\gamma\},&0<c\le\alpha,\\
 \alpha\min\{1,\gamma/c\},&c>\alpha.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
At $\gamma=\infty$ it equals $\alpha$. Moreover
$\lim_n n^{-1}\ln r_E(n;K)=\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Viability and an exact prefix catalogue}

The identity $t(s)=\int_0^1 R'(\theta s)\,d\theta$ shows that $t$ is
smooth and positive. For fixed $s$, the vertical map is strictly
increasing, with derivative $t(s)(q+\partial_zP_v)>0$, and is proper
because $P_v$ is bounded. Together with invertibility of $R$, the inverse
function theorem proves that each $f_v$ is a global smooth diffeomorphism.

Taylor's formula and \eqref{eq:Pbound} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:TaylorP}
 |P_v(s,sy)|\le\kappa s^2\quad(|y|\le1),\qquad
 |\partial_zP_v(x)|\le\min\{\kappa,\kappa\norm{x}_2\}.
\end{equation}
For $s>0$ the ratio evolves as
\[
 y_{j+1}=qy_j+v_{j+1}+P_{v_{j+1}}(s_j,s_jy_j)/s_j,
 \qquad s_j=R^j(s).
\]
The last term has absolute value at most $\kappa s_j\le\kappa<\eta$
in $Q$. The inward margin \eqref{eq:margin} therefore proves viability
with margin at least $\eta$. The origin is fixed.

Since $R$ is increasing, $R^j(s)\le R^j(1)\to0$ uniformly on
$s\in[0,1]$: the decreasing limit for $s=1$ is a fixed point, and
\eqref{eq:radialR} excludes every positive fixed point in this interval.
For each $\xi\in(0,\rho)$ there is $C_\xi>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialupper}
 \frac{R^j(s)}s\le C_\xi(\rho+\xi)^j
 \quad(0<s\le1,\ j\ge0).
\end{equation}
For each $s_0>0$ there is also $d_{\xi,s_0}>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radiallower}
 \frac{R^j(s)}s\ge d_{\xi,s_0}(\rho-\xi)^j
 \quad(s_0\le s\le1,\ j\ge0).
\end{equation}
To see this, after a common finite time all radii are in a neighborhood
where $\rho-\xi\le R(s)/s\le\rho+\xi$. The finitely many earlier
products have a uniform upper bound, and a positive lower bound on
$[s_0,1]$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:exactradialupper}
For every $e>0$ there is $D_e\ge1$ such that
$r_E(m;Q)\le D_e e^{(\alpha+e)m}$ for every $m\ge0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block length $N$. Cover $[-1,1]$ by a mesh of at most
$C_\eta q^N$ centers, so that each ratio is within
$\eta q^{-N}/4$ of a center. For each center choose a length-$N$ word
for the unperturbed ratio system $y\mapsto qy+v$ that keeps every
subsequent ratio in $[-1+2\eta,1-2\eta]$; this is possible by
\eqref{eq:margin}.

Choose $a_N>0$ so small that
$\kappa a_N\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}q^j\le\eta/4$.
For any $0<s\le a_N$, apply the center's word to the actual system
starting from a nearby ratio. As long as the actual ratios lie in
$[-1,1]$, their discrepancy from the reference at each time $k\le N$
is bounded by
\[
 q^k\frac{\eta q^{-N}}4+
 \kappa a_N\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q^j\le\eta/2.
\]
Induction using the reference margin establishes the required membership
in $[-1,1]$ at every step, and hence justifies this estimate throughout
the block. This single block catalogue works for every $s\le a_N$.

A common finite time $m_0$ brings every feasible radius below $a_N$.
Use all length-$m_0$ words, which include a feasible choice for each
initial state, and thereafter concatenate the block catalogues. A
remaining block of length less than $N$ can use all words of that
length. For fixed $N$, this gives exact upper rate at most
$\ln q+N^{-1}\ln C_\eta$. Choose $N$ large enough for the extra term
to be less than $e$ and absorb the finite initial and remaining blocks
into $D_e$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sectional lower bound at every intermediate time}

Fix $s\in(0,1]$ and a word $u$. Write $Z_k(z)$ for the vertical
coordinate at time $k$ when the initial state is $(s,z)$. Each $Z_k$
is strictly increasing. The set $J=J(n,\delta,s,u)$ of
$z\in[-s,s]$ whose whole trajectory through time $n$ belongs to
$Q^\delta$ is an interval, possibly empty. Indeed, $Q^\delta$ is
convex; its section at the fixed radius $R^k(s)$ is an interval; and
its inverse under $Z_k$ is an interval. Intersect these intervals over
$k=0,\ldots,n$.

The chain rule gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:verticalderivative}
 Z_k'(z)=\frac{R^k(s)}s
 \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\bigl(q+\partial_zP_{u_{j+1}}(s_j,Z_j(z))\bigr).
\end{equation}
Given $\zeta\in(0,q)$, choose a small $d>0$ and a common time
$N_\zeta$ so that $s_j\le d$ for $j\ge N_\zeta$ and
$\kappa\sqrt{10}d\le\zeta$. If $\delta<d$ and $z\in J$, then
\eqref{eq:euclideannecessary} implies $|Z_j(z)|<s_j+2\delta\le3d$
at those times. Thus \eqref{eq:TaylorP} bounds each late derivative
factor below by $q-\zeta$. The early factors are at least
$q-\kappa>0$. After decreasing a constant to cover all early values
of $k$, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivlower}
 Z_k'(z)\ge A_\zeta\frac{R^k(s)}s(q-\zeta)^k
 \quad(z\in J,\ 0\le k\le n),
\end{equation}
uniformly in $n,u,s$ and sufficiently small $\delta$.

The interval $Z_k(J)$ has length at most $2R^k(s)+4\delta$.
Applying the mean value theorem on subintervals of $J$, then taking
their increasing union, gives from \eqref{eq:derivlower} and
\eqref{eq:radiallower}, for $s\in[s_0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialsection}
 |J|\le C(q-\zeta)^{-k}
           \bigl(1+C'\delta(\rho-\xi)^{-k}\bigr),
 \qquad 0\le k\le n.
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of the control and the horizon. This is a
bound for controls satisfying the entire time interval, tested at any
chosen time $k$.

If $K$ has positive area, choose $s_0>0$ so that
$K\cap\{s\ge s_0\}$ still has positive area. By Fubini, every
spanning catalogue satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialcountlower}
 r(n,\delta;K)\ge
 \frac{B(q-\zeta)^k}{1+C'\delta(\rho-\xi)^{-k}}
 \quad(0\le k\le n).
\end{equation}
For finite $\gamma$ in \eqref{eq:precision}, take $k=\lfloor an\rfloor$.
The logarithm of the denominator divided by $n$ tends to
$\pos{a[-\ln(\rho-\xi)]-\gamma}$. First let $n\to\infty$, then
$\zeta,\xi\downarrow0$, and finally maximize over $a\in[0,1]$.
This proves the lower bound in \eqref{eq:radiallaw}. When
$\gamma=\infty$, take $k=n$ to obtain the lower rate $\alpha$.
For the exact count the same argument with $\delta=0$ applies directly
to the exact feasible interval. It gives lower rate $\alpha$, matching
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactradialupper}.

\subsection{A feasible prefix followed by a full approximate tail}

Suppose first that $\rho<1$. Fix $a_r\in(\rho,1)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:radialupper} implies that the endpoint of any
exactly feasible prefix of length $m$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint}
 \norm{x_m}_M\le C a_r^m,
\end{equation}
where $\norm{(s,z)}_M=\max\{M|s|,|z|\}$ and $C$ is uniform in the
prefix and the initial state in $Q$. At the origin,
\[
 Df_v(0)=\begin{pmatrix}\rho&0\\ \rho v&\rho q\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For any $L>\rho q$, choosing $M$ sufficiently large makes the operator
norm of every such matrix less than $L$. Continuity and finiteness of
$V$ give a common convex neighborhood of the origin on which every
$f_v$ is $L$-Lipschitz in this norm. All maps fix the origin.

Append one fixed input tail after an exact prefix. Inductively, as long
as it stays in the neighborhood,
$\norm{x_k}_M\le Ca_r^m L^{k-m}$ for $m\le k\le n$.
For small enough $\delta$, making this bound at most
$\delta/(2\sqrt2)$ for every $k$ both proves that the induction remains
in the neighborhood and ensures membership in $Q^\delta$ throughout
the tail.

If $L>1$, it is enough to take an exact prefix of length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialtail1}
 m=\min\left\{n,\max\left\{m_0,
 \left\lceil\frac{n\ln L+\ln(2\sqrt2C/\delta)}
                         {\ln L-\ln a_r}\right\rceil\right\}\right\},
\end{equation}
where $m_0$ is any fixed initial time needed for the local estimate.
If the minimum selects $n$, the exact catalogue itself suffices.
Otherwise the displayed inequality proves the complete tail bound.
Lemma~\ref{lem:exactradialupper} gives the upper rate
\[
 (\alpha+e)\min\left\{1,
               \frac{\gamma+\ln L}{\ln L-\ln a_r}\right\}.
\]
If $L\le1$, the maximum along the tail occurs at $m$, and one can
instead take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialtail2}
 m=\min\left\{n,\max\left\{m_0,
       \left\lceil\frac{\ln(2\sqrt2C/\delta)}{-\ln a_r}\right\rceil
                      \right\}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The resulting upper rate is
$(\alpha+e)\min\{1,\gamma/(-\ln a_r)\}$.

Let $e\downarrow0$ and $a_r\downarrow\rho$. If $\rho q>1$, let
$L\downarrow\rho q$ in the first bound. It gives
$\min\{\alpha,\alpha-c+\gamma\}$. The same limit from $L>1$
handles $\rho q=1$. If $\rho q<1$, choose $L<1$ and use the second
bound to get $\alpha\min\{1,\gamma/c\}$. For $\rho=1$, the exact
catalogue already gives upper rate $\alpha$, which matches the lower
bound. The case $\gamma=\infty$ is likewise bounded by the exact
catalogue. A direct maximization over $a$ gives
\eqref{eq:radialcases}, completing Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}.

\begin{example}[Why the terminal time is insufficient]\label{ex:intermediate}
Let $q=2$, $V=\{-3/2,0,3/2\}$, $\eta=1/8$, and $R(s)=s/4$.
The margin \eqref{eq:margin} holds since every number in $[-2,2]$
is within $3/4$ of one of $-3/2,0,3/2$. Set
$P_v(s,z)=e_v\tanh s\tanh z$, with $|e_v|<1/64$.
These functions vanish to first order at the origin, and their function,
gradient and Hessian norms are bounded by $4|e_v|<1/16$.
For every positive-area compact $K\subset Q$, Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}
therefore gives rate $\min\{\ln2,\gamma/2\}$.
At $\delta_n=2^{-n}$, the rate is $\tfrac12\ln2$.
In \eqref{eq:radiallaw} the maximizing time fraction is $a=1/2$;
testing only $a=1$ would give zero. The whole time interval is essential.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[The two classes have different hypotheses]\label{rem:classes}
Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} has a strict inward margin and a common radial
map; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has two nonoverlapping ratio branches and
control-dependent radial factors. The latter alphabet has no strict
inward margin at the branch boundary. Thus neither theorem is stated
as an extension of the other. When $\rho_0\ne\rho_1$, their difference
also cannot be removed by a fixed $C^1$ state change taking origin to
origin and respecting labels. The eigenvalues of $DF_i(0)$ are
$\rho_i,2\rho_i$, so its spectral radius is $2\rho_i$; in
\eqref{eq:radialmaps} every derivative has the same spectral radius
$\rho q$. A $C^1$ conjugacy makes corresponding derivatives similar,
which is incompatible with two different spectral radii. No assertion
about an arbitrary topological change of coordinates is needed here.
\end{remark}

\section{One expanding branch at the critical tolerance}\label{sec:expanding}

The geometric inequality \eqref{eq:leaflength} uses
$\rho_i\le1/2$. We finish with a fixed system for which it fails:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expandingmaps}
 F_0(s,z)=\left(\frac{3s}4,\frac{3(2z+s)}4\right),\qquad
 F_1(s,z)=\left(\frac{s}4,\frac{2z-s}4\right).
\end{equation}
The exact ratio coding \eqref{eq:cylinders} remains valid. The map
$F_0$ has transverse eigenvalue $3/2$, whereas $F_1$ contracts in
the maximum norm with operator norm at most $3/4$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:expanding}
For \eqref{eq:expandingmaps}, $K=Q$, and $\delta_n=2^{-n}$, put
$p_*=\ln2/\ln3$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expandinglimit}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\ln r(n,2^{-n};Q)=H(p_*).
\end{equation}
More precisely, with $\lambda=\ln(p_* /(1-p_*))/\ln3>0$ and the
stopped-tree count for the factors $3/4,1/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expandingbounds}
 \frac{\binom{n}{\lceil p_*n\rceil}}{n(3n+1)}
 \le r(n,2^{-n};Q)
 \le N\left(n,\frac{2^{-n}}{2\sqrt2}\right)
 \le (8\sqrt2)^\lambda e^{nH(p_*)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the displayed stopped catalogue exceeds the minimal
actual catalogue by at most a factor $Cn^3$ for a fixed $C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, use each stopped prefix exactly, then append
$1^\infty$. At an early stopping time the endpoint has maximum norm
at most $2^{-n}/(2\sqrt2)$, and each tail step multiplies that norm by
at most $3/4$. Thus its Euclidean norm stays at most
$2^{-n}/2$. Terminal leaves are exactly feasible throughout.

To bound the leaves, write
$c_0=\ln(4/3)$, $c_1=\ln4$, and
$T=n\ln2+\ln(2\sqrt2)$. Every leaf $w$ of length $k\le n$ has
$S(w)<T+c_1$. Give symbol $0$ Bernoulli probability $p_*$.
The mass of its cylinder is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Bernleaf}
 \nu[w]=e^{-\beta k-\lambda S(w)},\qquad
 \beta=-\ln(1-p_*)-\lambda c_1.
\end{equation}
Here $1/2<p_*<3/4$. The function
$g(p)=c_0\ln(1-p)-c_1\ln p$ is strictly decreasing and
$g(3/4)=0$, so $\beta=g(p_*)/\ln3>0$.
The leaves form a complete prefix-free family whose masses sum to one.
By \eqref{eq:Bernleaf}, each mass is at least
$e^{-\beta n-\lambda(T+c_1)}$. It follows that
\[
 N\le e^{\beta n+\lambda(T+c_1)}
      =(8\sqrt2)^\lambda e^{nH(p_*)},
\]
because $p_*c_0+(1-p_*)c_1=\ln2$ and
$\beta+\lambda\ln2=H(p_*)$.

For the lower bound, let $j_n=\lceil p_*n\rceil$ and consider words
of length $n$ with $j_n$ zeros. For a word $w$ define
\[
 D_k(w)=\ln(2^kA_{w_1\cdots w_k})
       =N_0(w_1\cdots w_k)\ln3-k\ln2.
\]
Here $N_0(a)$ counts the zeros in $a$.
Their total satisfies $0\le D_n(w)<\ln3$. Call a word good if
$D_k(w)\ge0$ for every $0\le k\le n$. Every cyclic orbit of these
words contains a good one: start immediately after a minimum of
$D_0,\ldots,D_{n-1}$. Before wrapping, all new partial sums are
differences from that minimum; after wrapping they are such differences
plus the nonnegative total $D_n$. Each cyclic orbit has at most $n$
elements, so there are at least $n^{-1}\binom{n}{j_n}$ good words.
This is the elementary minimum-partial-sum rotation; no assertion about
an exact number of good rotations is required.

For every good word choose the actual state $(1,y_w)$, where $y_w$ is
the midpoint of $I_w$. All such midpoints belong to a grid of spacing
$2^{1-n}=2\delta_n$. Fix any length-$n$ input word $u$ covering some
of these states. One witness can have $w=u$. For any other one let
$j$ be the first disagreement and $a=u_1\cdots u_{j-1}$ the common
prefix. As in \eqref{eq:band}, the wrong branch and
\eqref{eq:euclideannecessary} imply
\[
 |y_w-m_a|<\frac{\delta_n}{\rho_{u_j}e^{D_{j-1}(w)}}
                         \le4\delta_n.
\]
For each fixed $j$, this is a one-sided band, so it contains at most
three points of the grid. Thus an arbitrary approximate control covers
at most $3n+1$ good witnesses. Dividing their count by $3n+1$ proves
the first inequality in \eqref{eq:expandingbounds}.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:types},
$\binom{n}{j_n}\ge e^{nH(j_n/n)}/(n+1)$.
Since $j_n/n=p_*+O(1/n)$ and $p_*\in(0,1)$,
$nH(j_n/n)=nH(p_*)+O(1)$. The two bounds therefore give
\eqref{eq:expandinglimit} and the stated polynomial ratio.
\end{proof}

Unlike \eqref{eq:binary}, this example does not have convergence for
every input sequence. In fact
\[
 F_0^k(1,1)=\bigl((3/4)^k,\ 2(3/2)^k-(3/4)^k\bigr),
\]
whose second coordinate diverges. Exactly feasible trajectories still
converge to the origin, since $s_k\le(3/4)^k$ and $|z_k|\le s_k$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:expanding} concerns only the specified tolerance
and initial set; the all-input convergence in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
does not hold here.

\section{Scope of the results}

The main distinction is visible within a single system: positive area
and even nearly full area need not substitute for interior when
contraction is tied to the feasible control branch. The common radial
theorem provides an independent regime where positive area does suffice.
The exact and outer rates alone do not record this distinction.

The proofs rely on one-dimensional ratio geometry. Classifying arbitrary
positive-area initial sets and treating nonlinear control-dependent
contraction remain outside their scope. The catalogues determine a
covering cost; an encoder--decoder implementation would require a
separate argument. Within the stated systems, the estimates apply to
arbitrary approximate controls and to the complete time horizon.
