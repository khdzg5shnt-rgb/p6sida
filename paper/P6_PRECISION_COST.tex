\begin{abstract}
We study the smallest control catalogue that keeps each actual initial
state within a prescribed Euclidean neighbourhood of a constraint set
throughout a finite horizon. For a class of smooth, globally invertible
planar systems, both the feasible angular branches and the radial
contraction depend on the state. We prove a sharp threshold
$\gamma_*=\beta[-a_0\log a_0-a_1\log a_1]$ for precision sequences
$\delta_n=\exp(-\gamma n+o(n))$. Below and at this threshold, every
fixed positive-area compact initial set has cost rate $\gamma/\beta$.
Above it, one compact set of arbitrarily nearly full area has rate
$-a_0\log a_0-a_1\log a_1$ simultaneously for every finite precision
exponent above the threshold, whereas the full constraint set has a strictly larger lower
rate. The comparison with actual approximate controls follows from
fibrewise first-deviation estimates and a stopping-witness argument
with only polynomial catalogue loss. The nonlinear systems admit no
simultaneous conjugacy to the matched linear model that preserves the
constraint set. A smooth noninjective example, identical to that linear
model near the common attracting origin, shows why local matching and
uniform convergence alone do not imply the threshold theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Invariance entropy measures the exponential growth of the number of
controls needed to keep an initial set inside a controlled invariant
set; see Kawan \cite{Kawan2013}. At a fixed positive tolerance, a
contracting system can have bounded outer invariance complexity.
This does not determine the cost when the tolerance decreases with the
horizon. The latter problem compares two scales: the information needed
to choose feasible branches and the contraction that eventually makes
further precision constraints inexpensive.

We consider a catalogue of open-loop inputs from which a control is
assigned to each actual initial state. Every time from $0$ through $n$
must satisfy the same tolerance $\delta_n$; approximate trajectories
may leave and reenter the constraint set. Our question is whether the
exponential catalogue cost is independent of the initial compact set
as long as that set has positive area. We identify the exact range of
precision exponents for which this independence holds in a class with
matched angular geometry and radial contraction. Beyond the threshold,
removing a set of arbitrarily small area changes the cost strictly.

The main result includes an explicit state-dependent, globally
invertible smooth family. Its feasible angular intervals vary with
radius, and its assigned digits are not assumed to be independent
under area measure. We derive uniform cylinder comparisons from the
maps, then establish estimates for \emph{arbitrary} approximate inputs.
These steps connect classical word counts to the optimal catalogue of
actual controls. They also show that the same nearly full-area compact
set works for all higher precision exponents and all precision
sequences with those exponents. A boundary-based obstruction rules out
a simultaneous change of coordinates preserving the constraint set.
A complementary example has the same local dynamics but a global
angular collapse, and loses initial-set independence at every positive
precision exponent.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

Our finite-horizon counting function is the outer invariance
complexity used by Chen and Zhong \cite[Section 2.2]{CZ2024}, with a
one-step change of time convention. Their bounded-complexity and
equi-invariability results concern each fixed tolerance. Entropies for
exponential and practical stabilization \cite{Colonius2012,CH2021}
also relate control information to accuracy, but impose stabilization
tasks different from the all-times Euclidean neighbourhood constraint
used here. These frameworks provide the context for the problem,
rather than the catalogue comparison proved below.

Measure-theoretic invariance entropies and variational principles have
been developed in \cite{WHS2019,NWH2022}, and invariance entropy
dimensions for zero-entropy systems are studied in \cite{CHZ2026}.
We evaluate outer complexity along a specified exponential Euclidean
precision scale and compare actual positive-area compact initial sets.
The measure in our proof is ordinary planar area, used to construct
typical subsets; it is not asserted to be an invariant measure of a
single controlled map.

Stopping at a product threshold, type counting, and typical-set
arguments are classical tools; see \cite{Hutchinson1981,Csiszar1998}.
Yang, Chen, Yang and Zhou \cite{YCYZ2025} establish induced-pressure
and Bowen-equation results on a given invariant partition. In
particular, their spanning/separated comparison is made for the
addresses of that partition. Such a comparison does not itself
identify a prescribed exact code with an optimal catalogue allowing
approximate controls. Our proof supplies the required controlled
geometric comparison: a wrong first branch confines the covered
initial states to a thin, radius-dependent band, and one arbitrary
input covers only $O(n)$ stopped midpoint witnesses. The resulting
threshold is specific to the matching structure stated below. We do
not claim a classification of general contracting control systems.

\section{The counting problem and the threshold theorem}

Write
\[
 Q=\{(s,z)\in\R^2:0\le s\le1,\ |z|\le s\},\qquad \area(Q)=1.
\]
For two maps $G_0,G_1:\R^2\to\R^2$, an input is
$u=(u_1,u_2,\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\N}$. Set
$G_u^0x=x$ and $G_u^kx=G_{u_k}\circ\cdots\circ G_{u_1}x$.
For $\delta>0$, let
$Q^\delta=\{x:\dist(x,Q)<\delta\}$, with Euclidean distance.

\begin{definition}\label{def:catalogue}
For a nonempty compact $K\subset Q$, let $r_G(n,\delta;K)$
be the least cardinality of a finite input catalogue $\mathcal U$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalogue}
 \forall x\in K\ \exists u\in\mathcal U\
 \forall k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:\quad G_u^kx\in Q^\delta .
\end{equation}
The assignment may depend on the actual state $x$. There is no
restriction on a trajectory between distinct integer times, and
leaving or reentering $Q$ at those times is allowed as long as
\eqref{eq:catalogue} holds. Only the first $n$ input symbols matter.
\end{definition}

All logarithms are natural. Fix parameters
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
\begin{gathered}
 a_0,a_1\in(0,1),\quad a_0+a_1=1,\quad a_0\ne a_1,\quad
 \beta>1,\\
 0\le\ee<\frac{a_{\min}}{4(\beta+1)},\qquad
 a_{\min}=\min(a_0,a_1).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
On the entire real line define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p}
 p_0(s)=a_0+\ee\frac{s}{1+s^2},\qquad
 p_1(s)=a_1-\ee\frac{s}{1+s^2}.
\end{equation}
Our maps are
\begin{align}
 G_0(s,z)&=\bigl(p_0(s)^\beta s,\
             p_0(s)^{\beta-1}[z+p_1(s)s]\bigr),\label{eq:G0}\\
 G_1(s,z)&=\bigl(p_1(s)^\beta s,\
             p_1(s)^{\beta-1}[z-p_0(s)s]\bigr).\label{eq:G1}
\end{align}
The value $\ee=0$ gives the matched linear maps $A_0,A_1$.
For $\ee>0$ the feasible branches and the radial factors depend on
$s$, while $DG_i(0)=A_i$ as matrices.

Use the functions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropies}
 H(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\quad
 \chi(t)=-t\log a_0-(1-t)\log a_1,\quad h=H(a_0),
\end{equation}
with $0\log0=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Common-cost threshold]\label{thm:main}
For every system \eqref{eq:parameters}--\eqref{eq:G1}, both maps
are $C^\infty$ diffeomorphisms of $\R^2$, $Q$ is controlled
invariant, and every input drives bounded sets uniformly and
exponentially to the origin.

For every fixed compact $K\subset Q$ with $\area(K)>0$, every
finite $\gamma\ge0$, and every positive sequence $\delta_n\to0$
with $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal}
 \min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}
 \le \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;K)}n
 \le \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;K)}n
 \le \min\{\log2,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $0\le\gamma\le\beta h$ the limit exists and
equals $\gamma/\beta$ for every such $K$.

For each $\zeta\in(0,1)$ there is one compact set $K_\zeta\subset Q$
with $\area(K_\zeta)>1-\zeta$, chosen independently of $\gamma$
and of the precision sequence, such that for every finite
$\gamma>\beta h$ and every sequence as above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)}n
 =h
 <\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;Q)}n .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Thus $\beta h$ is the exact boundary for the property that
\emph{all} positive-area compact initial sets share a cost.
The assertion above the boundary is a strict separation, not a
two-value classification of initial sets. The theorem does not
give the general high-precision exact rate or limit for $Q$.

The matching relation is instantaneous: on a fibre with radius $s$,
the inverse angular branches have lengths $p_i(s)$ and the radial
factor is $p_i(s)^\beta$. It is stronger than a first-order condition
at the attracting origin. Sections \ref{sec:conjugacy} and
\ref{sec:local} respectively distinguish the state-dependent theorem
from a constraint-preserving coordinate reduction and from a
statement based solely on local matching.

\section{Smooth dynamics and complete feasible cylinders}

Set $a_{\max}=\max(a_0,a_1)$ and
\[
 \underline p=a_{\min}-\ee/2>0,\qquad
 \overline p=a_{\max}+\ee/2<1,\qquad r=\overline p^\beta<1.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:dynamics}
The maps in \eqref{eq:G0}--\eqref{eq:G1} are global smooth
diffeomorphisms. There are $M\ge1$ and $L<1$ such that the norm
$\|(s,z)\|_*=\max\{M|s|,|z|\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 \|G_i(s,z)\|_*\le L\|(s,z)\|_*
 \quad(i=0,1,\ (s,z)\in\R^2).
\end{equation}
For $s>0$, writing $y=z/s$ and $\xi(s)=p_0(s)-p_1(s)$,
the complete one-step feasible intervals are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branches}
 I_0(s)=[-1,\xi(s)],\qquad I_1(s)=[\xi(s),1].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $f(s)=s/(1+s^2)$ one has $|f|\le1/2$, $|f'|\le1$,
and $|sf'|\le1/2$. The radial maps $g_i(s)=s p_i(s)^\beta$
therefore satisfy
\[
 g_i'(s)=p_i(s)^{\beta-1}\bigl[p_i(s)+\beta s p_i'(s)\bigr]>0,
\]
because the bracket is at least
$a_{\min}-(\beta+1)\ee/2>0$. Moreover it is bounded above by
$a_{\max}+(\beta+1)\ee/2<1$.
Since $p_i(s)\to a_i$ as $|s|\to\infty$, $g_i$ maps $\R$
onto $\R$. Recovering $s$ from the first coordinate and then $z$
from its affine second coordinate gives a smooth global inverse.
The Jacobian is
\[
 \det DG_i(s,z)=p_i(s)^{2\beta-2}
                 [p_i(s)+\beta s p_i'(s)]>0.
\]
Choose $M\ge1$ so large that
$\overline p^{\beta-1}+\overline p^\beta/M<1$.
The first coordinate in the adapted norm has factor at most
$\overline p^\beta$, and the second satisfies
\[
 |z'|\le\overline p^{\beta-1}|z|+\overline p^\beta|s|.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:norm}, uniformly on the whole plane.

For positive $s$, the angular maps and their inverses are
\begin{align}
 T_{0,s}(y)&=(y+p_1(s))/p_0(s),&
 B_{0,s}(y)&=p_0(s)y-p_1(s),\label{eq:T}\\
 T_{1,s}(y)&=(y-p_0(s))/p_1(s),&
 B_{1,s}(y)&=p_1(s)y+p_0(s).\nonumber
\end{align}
Their inverse images of $[-1,1]$ are precisely
\eqref{eq:branches}, and their union is $[-1,1]$.
The radial coordinate remains in $[0,1]$ from $Q$; the origin is
fixed. Thus every state has an exact feasible continuation in $Q$.
\end{proof}

For a word $w=w_1\cdots w_l$ and initial radius $s\in[0,1]$,
write $s_0=s$, $s_j=g_{w_j}(s_{j-1})$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:products}
 P_w(s)=\prod_{j=1}^l p_{w_j}(s_{j-1}),\qquad
 \lambda_w=\prod_{j=1}^l a_{w_j}.
\end{equation}
Empty products are one. The radius depends only on $s$ and
the word, not on the initial angle.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform product comparison]\label{lem:products}
There is a constant $D\ge1$, depending only on the system, such that
for all words and all $s\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:productcomparison}
 D^{-1}\lambda_w\le P_w(s)\le D\lambda_w,\qquad
 s_l=sP_w(s)^\beta\le r^l s .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The radial identity and bound follow directly from the definitions.
For $s_j\in[0,1]$,
\[
 |\log p_i(s_j)-\log a_i|
 \le\frac{\ee s_j}{\underline p}
 \le\frac{\ee r^j}{\underline p}.
\]
Summation gives
$|\log P_w(s)-\log\lambda_w|
 \le\ee/[\underline p(1-r)]$.
Take $D$ to be the exponential of this upper bound.
Thus bounded cumulative distortion is derived from the maps,
rather than imposed as an assumption.
\end{proof}

For each word let $I_w(s)$ be the closed interval of initial
angles for which all its intermediate states belong to $Q$.
Set $I_\varnothing(s)=[-1,1]$. Directly from \eqref{eq:T},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinderrecursion}
 I_{iv}(s)=B_{i,s}\bigl(I_v(g_i(s))\bigr),\qquad
 |I_w(s)|=2P_w(s).
\end{equation}
The first formula includes every intermediate constraint, since
$B_{i,s}([-1,1])$ is the full feasible first-branch interval.
At each depth the intervals cover $[-1,1]$ with disjoint interiors.
The same holds for any finite complete prefix-free tree of words.
Shared endpoints are retained in both closed intervals.

The actual feasible region of $w$ is the curved strip
\[
 Q_w=\{(s,sy):0<s\le1,\ y\in I_w(s)\}\cup\{(0,0)\}.
\]
The change $(s,y)\mapsto(s,sy)$ has Jacobian $s$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinderarea}
 \area(Q_w)=\int_0^1 2sP_w(s)\,ds,\qquad
 D^{-1}\lambda_w\le\area(Q_w)\le D\lambda_w.
\end{equation}
For each finite word its endpoint curves are continuous, and
have area zero by fibrewise integration. Their countable union
and the vertex are a null set, but remain part of every catalogue
used to cover $Q$.

\section{Arbitrary approximate controls and stopping witnesses}
\label{sec:comparison}

Assign a natural exact code by taking branch $0$ at
$y\le\xi(s)$ and branch $1$ at $y>\xi(s)$, and iterating this
rule. Denote its length-$k$ prefix at $x$ by $c_k(x)$.
This convention fixes boundary itineraries. It is a tool for
comparison, not a restriction on the catalogue in
Definition \ref{def:catalogue}.

For an arbitrary word $u$ of length $n$, let
\[
 V(n,\delta,u)=
 \{x\in Q:G_u^kx\in Q^\delta\text{ for every }0\le k\le n\}.
\]
If a state $x=(s,sy)$ first disagrees with $u$ at time $j$,
let $a=u_1\cdots u_{j-1}$ and $i=u_j$. The common angular
composition $T_{a,s}$ is increasing and affine, with slope
$P_a(s)^{-1}$. Put
\[
 b_a(s)=T_{a,s}^{-1}(\xi(s_{j-1})).
\]
At the wrong branch one has the exact identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstidentity}
 |y_j|-1=\frac{|y-b_a(s)|}{P_{ai}(s)},\qquad
 s_j=sP_{ai}(s)^\beta .
\end{equation}
At a branch tie the excess is zero; the formula still applies.

\begin{lemma}[First-deviation band]\label{lem:band}
The states of $V(n,\delta,u)$ with first disagreement at $j$
lie in a one-sided fibrewise band
\begin{equation}\label{eq:band}
 |y-b_a(s)|<
 \frac{2\delta}{sP_{ai}(s)^{\beta-1}}
 \le\frac{C_b\delta\lambda_a^{1-\beta}}s,\qquad
 C_b=2D^{\beta-1}a_{\min}^{1-\beta}.
\end{equation}
Its area is at most $C_b\delta\lambda_a^{1-\beta}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $(s_j,z_j)\in Q^\delta$, the Lipschitz function
$(s,z)\mapsto |z|-s$ gives $|z_j|-s_j<\sqrt2\delta<2\delta$.
Combine this necessary condition with
\eqref{eq:firstidentity} and \eqref{eq:productcomparison}.
The side of the band is fixed by the chosen wrong branch.
Multiplying its angle width by the physical Jacobian $s$ and
integrating over $0<s\le1$ gives the claimed area bound.
The centre may depend on $s$; the fibrewise estimate remains
valid. No lower bound on the initial radius is needed.
Any later reentry into $Q$ still has to satisfy this first
deviation constraint.
\end{proof}

For $0<t<1$, let $\mathcal L(n,t)$ be the finite complete
prefix-free family obtained by stopping at the first word
with $\lambda_w\le t$, or at depth $n$ if no earlier crossing
has occurred. Put $N(n,t)=\#\mathcal L(n,t)$. Every leaf has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leafbounds}
 \lambda_{w^-}>t,\qquad \lambda_w>a_{\min}t,\qquad
 \sum_{w\in\mathcal L(n,t)}\lambda_w=1 .
\end{equation}
Here $w^-$ is its parent; the parent of a one-letter word is
the empty word. The last identity follows from $a_0+a_1=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Comparison with the actual catalogue]\label{lem:catalogue}
With
\[
 C_0=2\sqrt2\,MD^\beta,\qquad
 B_0=\left\lceil1+D^\beta a_{\min}^{-\beta}\right\rceil,
\]
for every $n\ge1$ and $0<\delta<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cataloguecomparison}
 \frac{N(n,\delta^{1/\beta})}{1+B_0n}
 \le r_G(n,\delta;Q)
 \le N\bigl(n,(\delta/C_0)^{1/\beta}\bigr)
 \le C_U\delta^{-1/\beta},
 \quad C_U=a_{\min}^{-1}C_0^{1/\beta}.
\end{equation}
Also $r_G(n,\delta;Q)\le2^n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, use one input with prefix $w$ for each
leaf at $t=(\delta/C_0)^{1/\beta}$, followed by a fixed
infinite tail. At every initial radius, the intervals
$I_w(s)$ form a complete cover of all angles, including their
endpoints. A depth-$n$ leaf is exactly feasible throughout
the required horizon. At an earlier leaf, the endpoint obeys
$s_l\le D^\beta t^\beta$ and $|z_l|\le s_l$, so
\[
 \|(s_l,z_l)\|_*\le MD^\beta t^\beta=\delta/(2\sqrt2).
\]
By \eqref{eq:norm}, all later states under the fixed tail
have Euclidean norm at most $\delta/2$. They therefore lie in
$Q^\delta$ at every remaining time. The vertex is fixed.
Equation \eqref{eq:leafbounds} gives
$N(n,t)\le(a_{\min}t)^{-1}$.
All length-$n$ words give the additional $2^n$ upper bound.

For the lower bound set $t=\delta^{1/\beta}$.
For each leaf choose the actual state $(1,y_w)$ with $y_w$
the midpoint of $I_w(1)$. By \eqref{eq:cylinderrecursion},
\eqref{eq:productcomparison} and \eqref{eq:leafbounds},
every such interval has length greater than
$2D^{-1}a_{\min}t$. Consecutive midpoints have at least this
spacing because the intervals have disjoint interiors.

Fix any arbitrary length-$n$ input $u$. At most one leaf is
a prefix of $u$. Every other covered midpoint witness first
disagrees at a time $j\le |w|$, with common prefix $a=u|_{j-1}$.
Since this occurs before stopping, $\lambda_a>t$.
At radius one, Lemma \ref{lem:band} restricts its initial
angle to one band of length at most $C_b t$. Its centre
and side depend only on $u$ and $j$. The midpoint spacing
bounds the number of witnesses in that band by
$1+D^\beta a_{\min}^{-\beta}\le B_0$.
Summing over $j\le n$, one arbitrary input covers at most
$1+B_0n$ witnesses. A catalogue spanning all of $Q$ must
cover all these actual states, proving the lower bound.
\end{proof}

The lower estimate allows every approximate input and every
later reentry. It does not assume that the natural stopping
tree is an optimal catalogue. The loss is polynomial in the
horizon and hence does not affect its exponential rate.

\section{Area-typical initial states and proof of the threshold}
\label{sec:threshold}

\begin{lemma}[Physical typical sets]\label{lem:typical}
For area-almost every $x\in Q$, the frequency of zero symbols
in $c_k(x)$ tends to $a_0$.
Every compact $K\subset Q$ of positive area contains a compact
positive-area set $T$ on which this convergence is uniform.
For every $0<\eta<h$, there is $D_\eta\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalproducts}
 D_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)k}
 \le\lambda_{c_k(x)}
 \le D_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)k}
 \quad(x\in T,\ k\ge0).
\end{equation}
For each $\zeta\in(0,1)$ such a compact $T$ can be chosen with
$\area(T)>1-\zeta$ inside $Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Normalize area on $Q$, whose area is one.
For every assigned word $w$, \eqref{eq:cylinderarea} bounds
its probability by $D\lambda_w$. Thus the area of states
whose length-$k$ prefix has zero frequency at distance at
least $\eta$ from $a_0$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typeprob}
 D(k+1)e^{-2\eta^2 k}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, for $t=j/k$,
put $D_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)=t\log(t/a_0)
+(1-t)\log((1-t)/a_1)$. Then
\[
 \binom{k}{j}a_0^j a_1^{k-j}
 \le e^{-kD_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)},\qquad
 D_{\rm KL}(t\Vert a_0)\ge2(t-a_0)^2.
\]
The second inequality follows from
$D_{\rm KL}''(t)=1/[t(1-t)]\ge4$, with its value and first
derivative zero at $a_0$. Summing over types gives
\eqref{eq:typeprob}. Borel--Cantelli, followed by a countable
sequence of positive $\eta$ tending to zero, proves almost
everywhere convergence. No independence of the state-dependent
digits has been used.

Remove the null set of finite-word boundary curves and the
vertex. Egorov's theorem and inner regularity of area give
a compact subset of any positive-area $K$ with positive area
and uniform frequency convergence. On $Q$ they give a
compact set of area greater than $1-\zeta$.
Since $-\log\lambda_{c_k(x)}/k$ is $\chi$ of the zero
frequency, it converges uniformly to $\chi(a_0)=h$ on each
chosen set. Absorb the finitely many early times into
$D_\eta$ to obtain \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}.
All sets are chosen before any precision exponent or sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Area covered by one arbitrary input]\label{lem:typicalarea}
If $T$ satisfies \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}, then for each
$0<\eta<h$ there is a constant $C_\eta$ such that, uniformly
in $u,n,\delta$ and $0\le m\le n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalarea}
 \area(T\cap V(n,\delta,u))
 \le C_\eta\left[e^{-(h-\eta)m}
       +\delta e^{(\beta-1)(h+\eta)m}\right].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Agreement through $m$ confines the state to $Q_{u|_m}$.
If this agreement class meets $T$, its reference product is
at most $D_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}$, and
\eqref{eq:cylinderarea} gives the first term.

For a first disagreement at $j\le m$, a nonempty class in
$T$ has common prefix $a=u|_{j-1}=c_{j-1}(x)$ for some $x\in T$.
Equation \eqref{eq:typicalproducts} bounds its product from
below by $D_\eta^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)(j-1)}$.
Lemma \ref{lem:band} bounds its area by
$C_bD_\eta^{\beta-1}\delta
 e^{(\beta-1)(h+\eta)(j-1)}$.
The sum over $j\le m$ is a geometric sum with ratio
greater than one, proving the second term. Empty classes
give no contribution. Later disagreement times are already
included in the agreement class through $m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}]
The dynamical assertions follow from Lemma \ref{lem:dynamics}.
For a given positive-area compact $K$, choose $T\subset K$
as in Lemma \ref{lem:typical}. For small $\delta$ set
\[
 m=\min\left\{n,\left\lfloor
       \frac{-\log\delta}{\beta(h+\eta)}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Then $\delta e^{\beta(h+\eta)m}\le1$, and
Lemma \ref{lem:typicalarea} gives
\[
 \area(T\cap V(n,\delta,u))\le
             2C_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}.
\]
Every catalogue for $K$ must cover $T$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowerK}
 r_G(n,\delta;K)\ge
       \frac{\area(T)}{2C_\eta}e^{(h-\eta)m}.
\end{equation}
For $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$, passage to rates and then
$\eta\downarrow0$ yields the lower bound
$\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}$. Lemma \ref{lem:catalogue} and
$K\subset Q$ give both upper bounds in \eqref{eq:universal}.
They coincide with the lower bound when $\gamma\le\beta h$.
The argument includes $\gamma=0$ and equality at the
threshold, and needs no monotonicity of $\delta_n$.

For the high side choose once a compact $T_\zeta$ from
Lemma \ref{lem:typical} with area greater than $1-\zeta$,
and set $K_\zeta=T_\zeta\cup\{(0,0)\}$.
For every $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, all
natural prefixes on $T_\zeta$ have zero frequency within
$\eta$ of $a_0$. The number of these prefixes is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalcount}
 (n+1)\exp\left[n
    \max_{\substack{0\le t\le1\\|t-a_0|\le\eta}}H(t)\right].
\end{equation}
Use each as an exact length-$n$ prefix with any infinite
extension, and one extra input for the vertex. This covers
$K_\zeta$ exactly for all required times, for every
$\delta>0$. Continuity of $H$ gives upper rate $h$.
For every finite $\gamma>\beta h$, \eqref{eq:universal}
gives lower rate $h$ on this same set. Its rate therefore
exists and equals $h$ simultaneously for all those
exponents and all their precision sequences.

It remains to obtain a strictly larger lower rate on $Q$.
Since $a_0\ne a_1$,
\[
 \Delta=\chi(1/2)-h
       =(a_0-1/2)\log(a_0/a_1)>0 .
\]
For a given $\gamma>\beta h$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pgamma}
 \theta_\gamma=\min\left\{\frac12,
             \frac{\gamma/\beta-h}{2\Delta}\right\},
 \qquad t_\gamma=(1-\theta_\gamma)a_0+\theta_\gamma/2 .
\end{equation}
By affinity of $\chi$ and strict concavity of $H$,
$\chi(t_\gamma)<\gamma/\beta$ and $H(t_\gamma)>h$.
Choose integers $j_n$ with $j_n/n\to t_\gamma$.
For all sufficiently large $n$, every word of length $n$
with exactly $j_n$ zeros has
\[
 -\log\lambda_w=n\chi(j_n/n)<-\log\delta_n/\beta.
\]
Every proper prefix has still smaller cost. All these words
are therefore terminal leaves of
$\mathcal L(n,\delta_n^{1/\beta})$.
The elementary type bound
$\binom{n}{j}\ge e^{nH(j/n)}/(n+1)$ and
Lemma \ref{lem:catalogue} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:highlower}
 r_G(n,\delta_n;Q)\ge
   \frac{\exp[nH(j_n/n)]}{(n+1)(1+B_0n)}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, the type bound follows since $j$ is a
mode of the binomial law with parameter $j/n$, so its
mass is at least $1/(n+1)$; endpoint types are immediate.
Taking lower rates in \eqref{eq:highlower} yields
$H(t_\gamma)>h$, proving \eqref{eq:high}.
The strict gap may tend to zero as $\gamma\downarrow\beta h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched linear systems]\label{cor:linear}
For
\[
 A_0(s,z)=(a_0^\beta s,a_0^{\beta-1}(z+a_1s)),\qquad
 A_1(s,z)=(a_1^\beta s,a_1^{\beta-1}(z-a_0s)),
\]
the conclusions of Theorem \ref{thm:main} hold with the
same threshold $\beta h$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set $\ee=0$ in the theorem. Then $P_w(s)=\lambda_w$,
so the product comparison constant is $D=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For example, $a_0=1/3$, $a_1=2/3$, $\beta=2$ gives
$A_0(s,z)=(s/9,z/3+2s/9)$ and
$A_1(s,z)=(4s/9,2z/3-2s/9)$.
Their common-cost threshold is
$2[-\tfrac13\log\tfrac13-\tfrac23\log\tfrac23]$.
Every admissible positive $\ee$ produces a reversible
state-dependent system with that same threshold.
This is a consequence of the controlled comparison, not
merely of the common derivatives at the origin.
\end{remark}

\section{No simultaneous coordinate reduction preserving the constraint}
\label{sec:conjugacy}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:conjugacy}
If $\ee>0$, there is no ambient homeomorphism $J$ taking
$Q$ onto $Q$ and simultaneously conjugating $G_0,G_1$
to $A_0,A_1$, even after permuting the two input labels.
In particular, no such $C^1$ or bi-Lipschitz coordinate
change exists. The assertion concerns preservation of
$Q$; it does not exclude conjugacies that change the
constraint set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The states that remain in $Q$ under constant input $0$
forever are exactly the lower ray
$S_0=\{(s,-s):0\le s\le1\}$.
Indeed, under this input $y+1$ is multiplied at each step
by $p_0(s_k)^{-1}\ge\overline p^{-1}>1$.
Every $y>-1$ eventually exits the feasible region.
Similarly constant input $1$ selects exactly the upper
ray $S_1=\{(s,s):0\le s\le1\}$.
The same statements hold for $A_i$.
A conjugacy preserving $Q$ must send these rays to the
corresponding target rays, with the possible label
permutation, and fix their common vertex.

Let $f_-,f_+:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ be the radial homeomorphisms
on the images of the actual lower and upper rays.
Write $\tau_0,\tau_1$ for the target radial factors
according to the label permutation. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rayconjugacy}
 f_-\circ g_0=\tau_0 f_-,\qquad
 f_+\circ g_1=\tau_1 f_+.
\end{equation}
The dividing state $x_s=(s,s\xi(s))$ admits both controls,
and its images are the upper-ray point of radius $g_0(s)$
and the lower-ray point of radius $g_1(s)$.
The conjugacy sends the intersection of the two feasible
domains to the target dividing ray. If its radius there is
$R(s)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dividingconjugacy}
 f_+(g_0(s))=\tau_0R(s),\qquad
 f_-(g_1(s))=\tau_1R(s).
\end{equation}
These relations hold with either permutation.
Set $L_*=g_1\circ g_0^{-1}$ near zero.
Equation \eqref{eq:dividingconjugacy} gives
$f_+=(\tau_0/\tau_1)f_-\circ L_*$ near zero.
Substitution into the second relation of
\eqref{eq:rayconjugacy} yields
\[
 f_-\circ h_1=\tau_1 f_-,\qquad
 h_1=L_*\circ g_1\circ L_*^{-1}.
\]
Thus the injective $f_-$ conjugates $g_0$ and $h_1$
to two scalar contractions. Those commute, so $g_0$
and $h_1$ must commute near zero. This implication
uses no differentiability of the proposed conjugacy.

We disprove commutation directly. Write $\rho_i=a_i^\beta$.
The explicit maps have expansions
\[
 g_i(s)=\rho_i s+b_i s^2+O(s^3),\quad
 b_0=\beta\ee a_0^{\beta-1}>0,\quad
 b_1=-\beta\ee a_1^{\beta-1}<0.
\]
Inverting $g_0$ and composing gives
\[
 L_*(s)=\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_0}s+
       \frac{b_1\rho_0-\rho_1b_0}{\rho_0^3}s^2+O(s^3).
\]
For a map $L(s)=As+Bs^2+O(s^3)$ and
$g(s)=\rho s+bs^2+O(s^3)$, the quadratic coefficient of
$L\circ g\circ L^{-1}$ is
$b/A+B\rho(\rho-1)/A^2$.
Applying this formula gives
\[
 h_1(s)=\rho_1s+\widetilde b_1s^2+O(s^3),\qquad
 \widetilde b_1=
 \frac{b_1(\rho_0+\rho_1-1)}{\rho_1}
       +\frac{b_0(1-\rho_1)}{\rho_0}.
\]
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 (g_0\circ h_1-h_1\circ g_0)(s)&=Cs^2+O(s^3),\\
 C&=(\rho_0+\rho_1-1)
       \left[\frac{\rho_0(1-\rho_0)b_1}{\rho_1}
                   -b_0(1-\rho_1)\right]>0.
\end{align*}
Here $\rho_0+\rho_1<1$ because $\beta>1$, and the
bracket is strictly negative. This contradiction proves
the proposition. The boundary argument also applies to
a locally defined ambient conjugacy whenever it and its
inverse are defined on all required one-step images of
$Q$, so feasibility in both directions is preserved.
\end{proof}

\section{Local matching does not suffice}
\label{sec:local}

The following example keeps the common attracting origin
and even agrees with the linear maps on a neighbourhood
of it. It violates global injectivity by collapsing
positive-area regions onto an invariant ray.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:collapse}
For every choice of $a_0,a_1,\beta$ in
\eqref{eq:parameters} and every $0<\sigma<1/2$, there are
$C^\infty$ maps $S_0,S_1:\R^2\to\R^2$ with these properties:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item They equal $A_0,A_1$ on $\{s\le\sigma\}$, so
       all derivatives at the origin agree.
 \item All inputs drive bounded sets uniformly
       exponentially to the origin, and $Q$ is
       controlled invariant.
 \item The compact set
       $K_\sigma=Q\cap\{s\ge2\sigma\}$ has area
       $1-4\sigma^2$ and $r_S(n,\delta;K_\sigma)=1$
       for every $n$ and every $\delta>0$.
 \item For every finite $\gamma\ge0$ and every sequence
       $\delta_n\to0$ with $-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$,
       the full-set cost satisfies
       \[
       \min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}
       \le\liminf\frac{\log r_S(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
       \le\limsup\frac{\log r_S(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
       \le\min\{\log2,\gamma/\beta\}.
       \]
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for $0<\gamma\le\beta h$ the full-set rate
is $\gamma/\beta$, but the positive-area set $K_\sigma$
has rate zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $e(t)=0$ for $t\le0$ and $e(t)=\exp(-1/t)$ for $t>0$,
and define
\[
 \psi(s)=\frac{e(s-\sigma)}
               {e(s-\sigma)+e(2\sigma-s)}.
\]
The denominator is positive everywhere. Thus $\psi$ is
smooth, zero for $s\le\sigma$, and one for $s\ge2\sigma$.
Put $\rho_i=a_i^\beta$ and define
\begin{align}
 S_0(s,z)&=\bigl(\rho_0s,\
 (1-\psi(s))a_0^{\beta-1}(z+a_1s)-\psi(s)\rho_0s\bigr),
       \label{eq:S0}\\
 S_1(s,z)&=\bigl(\rho_1s,\
 (1-\psi(s))a_1^{\beta-1}(z-a_0s)+\psi(s)\rho_1s\bigr).
       \label{eq:S1}
\end{align}
They have the claimed local identity.
Each $S_i(s,z)$ is a convex combination of $A_i(s,z)$
and $R_i(s,z)$, where
$R_0=(\rho_0s,-\rho_0s)$ and
$R_1=(\rho_1s,\rho_1s)$.
All four linear maps contract toward zero in one adapted
norm $\max\{M|s|,|z|\}$ for sufficiently large $M$:
the second-coordinate factor for $A_i$ is at most
$a_i^{\beta-1}+a_i^{\beta-1}a_{1-i}/M<1$, and
$R_i$ has factor at most $\rho_i$.
Convexity of the norm gives the same contraction bound
for $S_i$ on all of $\R^2$.

For controlled feasibility let $t=\psi(s)$, $y=z/s$,
and $\xi=a_0-a_1$. If $t<1$ the full feasible intervals
are
\begin{align*}
 J_0(s)&=\left[-1,\min\left\{1,
                         \xi+\frac{2a_0t}{1-t}\right\}\right],\\
 J_1(s)&=\left[\max\left\{-1,
                         \xi-\frac{2a_1t}{1-t}\right\},1\right].
\end{align*}
They follow by imposing $-1\le z'/s'\le1$ in
\eqref{eq:S0}--\eqref{eq:S1}, and their union is
$[-1,1]$. For $t=1$ both branches are feasible on the
whole fibre. The radius always decreases, so $Q$ is
controlled invariant.

On $K_\sigma$, one application of $S_0$ sends every
state to the lower ray. At every radius
$S_0(s,-s)=(\rho_0s,-\rho_0s)$, so input $0^\infty$
keeps the whole set exactly in $Q$. Its area is
$\int_{2\sigma}^1 2s\,ds=1-4\sigma^2$.

To obtain the full-set lower bound, use
$Q_c=Q\cap\{s\le c\}$ with $c=\sigma/2$.
For every input, the radius of a state in $Q_c$ stays
at most $c$. Thus $\psi=0$ forever along that trajectory,
even when its angle leaves $[-1,1]$, and its entire
trajectory is exactly that of $A_i$.
Consequently
$r_S(n,\delta;Q)\ge r_A(n,\delta;Q_c)$.
Corollary \ref{cor:linear} and \eqref{eq:universal}
give the lower bound.

For the upper bound choose an integer $t_0$ such that
$(\max_i\rho_i)^{t_0}\le\sigma$.
Every initial state admits an exact feasible prefix of
length $t_0$, ending in the region $s\le\sigma$.
All subsequent trajectories there coincide with the
linear system, for all inputs, since the radius continues
to decrease independently of the angle.
Concatenate all $2^{t_0}$ possible prefixes with a
catalogue for the linear full set over the remaining
horizon. For $n\ge t_0$ this gives
\[
 r_S(n,\delta;Q)\le
       2^{t_0}r_A(n-t_0,\delta;Q)
       \le 2^{t_0}C_U\delta^{-1/\beta}.
\]
The $2^n$ exact-prefix bound also holds.
These yield the upper rates for every stated precision
sequence. Finally
$\det DS_i=(1-\psi(s))a_i^{2\beta-1}$ vanishes on the
saturated region, identifying the noninjective mechanism.
\end{proof}

For fixed $\sigma$ this proposition gives one large
zero-cost initial set in one fixed system. Making its
area arbitrarily close to one changes $\sigma$ and the
system. This differs from Theorem \ref{thm:main}, where
all $K_\zeta$ are chosen inside a single fixed system.
The collapse example is a counterexample to sufficiency
of local matching and uniform convergence; it is not a
counterexample to the reversible theorem.

\section{Scope and consequences}

The low-precision law uses positive area without requiring
interior. Its proof remains valid near the vertex because
the singular factor $1/s$ in the first-deviation band is
cancelled by the physical area element. Above the threshold,
typical compact sets have rate $h$, while atypical terminal
words force a larger full-set lower rate. The threshold
therefore concerns the quantifier over \emph{all} positive-area
compact initial sets, rather than uniform convergence
of the controlled states.

The state-dependent result preserves the exact relation
between angular branch length and radial contraction,
but removes the requirement that branches be fixed linear
intervals. Uniform distortion alone is not used as a
substitute for actual control feasibility: the comparison
of arbitrary inputs with stopped physical witnesses is
what permits counting estimates to govern the true
catalogue. Proposition \ref{prop:conjugacy} distinguishes
this extension from a change of coordinates preserving
the original constraint, and Proposition \ref{prop:collapse}
shows a concrete obstruction outside the invertible class.

Several limitations remain. The systems are triangular,
the instantaneous matching is exact, and the perturbation
has a specific summable state dependence. We prove no
general nonlinear perturbation theorem based solely on
invertibility or the derivative at the origin. For the
full set above the threshold, the strict lower bound
\eqref{eq:highlower} need not match the upper bounds in
\eqref{eq:universal}; a general exact spectrum is not
claimed. The main theorem instead completely resolves
the common-cost boundary and its simultaneous initial-set
quantifiers in the stated family.
