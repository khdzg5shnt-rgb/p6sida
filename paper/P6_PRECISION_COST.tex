\begin{abstract}
Even when every input drives the state to zero, keeping a trajectory
near a prescribed set can require an increasing number of control
choices as the tolerance shrinks. We study this cost for two $C^2$
planar maps and a triangular constraint. A controller observes the
initial state and selects one input sequence from a finite catalogue.
The triangle is controlled invariant, both maps contract toward zero,
and their critical sets have area zero. Their prescribed linear parts
have radial factors equal to a common power $\beta>1$ of the
contraction factors of the inverse angular maps.
For tolerances $\delta_n=\exp(-\gamma n+o(n))\to0$, we compute
the exponential catalogue growth rate $S(\gamma)$ for full coverage.
One compact set obtained by removing an arbitrarily small fixed area
has a lower saturation rate. If neither map has a critical point in
the triangle, allowing failure probability $\exp(-\alpha n+o(n))$,
$\alpha>0$, gives the rate $\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}$.
Here $L_a(\alpha)$ is the growth rate of the number of exactly
feasible words needed in the linear model with that failure allowance.
The proof bounds the area served by one
input and constructs regions that force different control prefixes.
Finally, an injective example preserves every finite-horizon
full-cover count of the linear model but has a reliability lower
rate strictly above the linear rate. Thus critical sets of area zero do not
suffice for the probability formula.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Suppose that a controller observes an initial state, chooses one
input sequence from a finite list, and then applies that sequence
without further observations. How long must the list be to keep
every trajectory near a prescribed set? This is the catalogue
problem underlying invariance entropy \cite{CK2009,Kawan2013}.
Here we ask how the answer depends jointly on the allowed distance
from the set and the probability that no input in the list succeeds.
The only randomness is in the initial state.

Every input drives the state toward the origin, which belongs to
the constraint set.
At a fixed positive tolerance, only finitely many early choices
matter. If the tolerance decreases with the horizon, however,
contraction takes longer to make those choices irrelevant.
The following linear model makes this dependence explicit.

\subsection{What one input does}
Let
\[
Q=\{(s,z):0\le s\le1,\ |z|\le s\}.
\]
On a fibre with $s>0$, use $y=z/s\in[-1,1]$ to record the
slope of the ray through the state. We call $s$ the radius and
$y$ the angular coordinate; neither is a Euclidean polar coordinate.
Choose unequal $a_0,a_1\in(0,1)$ with $a_0+a_1=1$, and
$\beta>1$. The two controls act by
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{control}&\text{feasible initial }y&s\text{ after one step}
                                      &y\text{ after one step}\\ \hline
0&[-1,a_0-a_1]&a_0^\beta s&(y+a_1)/a_0\\
1&[a_0-a_1,1]&a_1^\beta s&(y-a_0)/a_1
\end{array}
\]
Thus each control is feasible on part of the triangle. It expands
that part's angular interval onto $[-1,1]$ and contracts its radius.
The intervals meet at their common endpoint, so every state has
at least one feasible next control.

For a word $w=w_1\cdots w_m$, let
$\lambda_w=\prod_{j=1}^m a_{w_j}$. The initial angles kept
in $Q$ through all $m$ steps form an interval of length
$2\lambda_w$; their final radius is $s\lambda_w^\beta$.
For example, when $a_0=1/3$, $a_1=2/3$ and $\beta=2$,
the word $01$ serves precisely $-7/9\le y\le-1/3$ on each
fibre. After two steps these states fill the angular range again,
at radius $4s/81$. The word is one catalogue entry serving a whole
region of initial states, not a trajectory selected separately
for each state.

Longer words serve narrower regions. At the same time, the states
they serve move closer to zero. Once the radius is a sufficiently
small multiple of the tolerance, the controller can use the same
continuation for all of them. Counting inputs therefore amounts
to following a binary tree only until each branch becomes small
enough, or the horizon is reached.

Uniform area on $Q$ is a probability measure, since $\area(Q)=1$.
The change of variables $z=sy$ has Jacobian $s$, so the region
served by $w$ has probability $\lambda_w$. In particular, words
of equal length can have very different probabilities. Allowing
a small failure probability may remove many rare words from the
catalogue even while they remain relevant to full coverage.

\subsection{The two budgets and the nonlinear problem}
An input succeeds through time $n$ when its state stays within
Euclidean distance $\delta_n$ of $Q$ at every time $0,\ldots,n$.
We consider
\[
\delta_n\to0,\qquad -\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma\ge0,
\qquad e_n\to0,\qquad -\log e_n/n\to\alpha>0.
\]
The precision budget $\delta_n$ bounds each successful trajectory's
departure from $Q$. The failure budget $e_n$ bounds the area of
initial states that have no successful catalogue entry. A trajectory
may leave and reenter $Q$, but it must satisfy the tolerance at
every intermediate time.

We replace the linear controls by $C^2$ maps with the same
derivatives at zero, retaining controlled invariance of $Q$ and
contraction toward zero under every input. The full-cover rate is
still an explicit function $S(\gamma)$: it starts as $\gamma/\beta$,
then follows a binary entropy curve, and eventually reaches $\log2$.
Critical sets of area zero suffice for this conclusion
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). The same theorem identifies a compact
set of area greater than $1-\zeta$, chosen once for any given
$\zeta>0$, whose rate is only $\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}$, where
$h=-a_0\log a_0-a_1\log a_1$. Hence deleting a fixed small area
and allowing an exponentially small failure are different problems.

When neither control has a critical point on $Q$, the rate with
both budgets is
\[
\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}
\qquad\text{(Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability}).}
\]
Here $L_a$ is the number-of-words exponent for a binary source
with probabilities $a_0,a_1$ and exponentially small error; its
definition appears in \eqref{eq:reliabilityfunction}. The two upper
bounds have simple constructions: cover all states at the requested
precision, or keep exactly feasible words carrying enough area.
The lower bound must deal with any catalogue, including inputs
that make small excursions outside $Q$.

Two features of nonlinear controls prevent a direct word count.
Their feasible regions can overlap, so different word labels need
not require different controls. Also, the initial steps can distort
area before the trajectory reaches the neighbourhood where the
linear parts dominate. We handle the first issue by estimating
the area served by one input at its first actual exit and by
constructing regions on which a prescribed prefix is forced.
For the second, nonsingularity supplies an area bound uniform over
all initial states. A bound obtained only after removing a fixed
exceptional set would not suffice when $e_n\to0$.

Theorem~\ref{thm:counter} shows what can go wrong with this area
comparison. An injective $C^2$ change in the first step compresses
relatively large initial regions onto rare angular words. It
preserves every finite-horizon full-cover count, yet gives a
reliability lower rate strictly larger than the displayed formula.
The critical sets are null. The obstruction is therefore a
quantitative distortion of area, even without positive-area collapse.

The original motivation was the relation between control entropy
and state chaos. In the class studied here, convergence of all
states to zero already excludes persistent separation of states.
The question that remains is how rapidly the necessary control
choices grow as precision and reliability increase.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}
The full-cover count is the outer invariance complexity of Chen
and Zhong \cite[Section 2.2]{CZ2024}, with a one-step change of
horizon convention. Their Section 3 treats measure-dependent
catalogue complexity. Wang, Huang and Sun \cite{WHS2019} use
invariant partitions and cylinder probabilities; their Theorem 4.8
transports the initial measure under a control conjugacy.
Colonius \cite{Colonius2018} develops an entropy based on
quasi-stationary measures. The semiconjugacy comparison in his
author version, Theorem 2.13, keeps the transported measure. Our
counterexample instead compares two systems each equipped with
uniform initial area; those measures do not correspond under the
map relating their full covers.

Estimating how much volume one control can serve is a standard
route to lower bounds for invariance entropy and pressure
\cite{Kawan2011,DK2016,CCS2020}. For example, the proof in
\cite[Theorem 4.8, author version]{DK2016} combines a volume estimate with a
bounded initial-time pullback. Here the local geometry is a
contracting radius and an expanding angular coordinate, and the
tolerance decreases with the horizon. The proof must retain its
dependence on that tolerance and on the area discarded during
the initial steps. Once these geometric comparisons are established,
the optimisation over word frequencies uses the classical method
of types \cite{Csiszar1998}; that optimisation is not a new
source-coding principle.

Other nearby formulations ask different measure or limit questions.
Nie, Wang and Huang \cite{NWH2022} use convex duality for
invariance pressure and measures on the control region. Chen,
Huang and Zhong \cite{CHZ2026} study entropy dimension using
invariant-partition cylinders and time-power normalisation; their
high-probability coverings take the failure allowance to zero
after the time limit. Cost-threshold pressure formulas in
\cite{YCYZ2025} give another counting framework. Wang and Huang
\cite{WH2022} study spanning-based entropies and inverse variational
principles. Our results concern the catalogue rates for the specified
planar class when the spatial tolerance and the failure allowance
decrease simultaneously with the horizon.

Section~\ref{sec:planar} gives the definitions and statements.
We first prove the full-cover formula using local feasible regions
and forced prefixes (Sections~\ref{sec:geometry}--\ref{sec:rates}).
Area estimates then yield the fixed-initial-set and probability
results (Sections~\ref{sec:area}--\ref{sec:reliability}), followed
by the counterexample. Section~\ref{sec:examples} gives a nonlinear
example for which the formulas apply. The appendices contain a
finite-horizon linear comparison, the unequal-power criterion,
further tests of the hypotheses, the angular interpolation, and
the local derivative bounds.

\section{Control catalogues and main results}\label{sec:planar}

\subsection{What a catalogue covers}
We keep the triangle
\[
Q=\{(s,z)\in\R^2:0\le s\le1,\ |z|\le s\},\qquad \area(Q)=1.
\]
For an infinite binary input $u=(u_1,u_2,\ldots)$ set
$F_u^0=\operatorname{id}$ and
$F_u^k=F_{u_k}\circ\cdots\circ F_{u_1}$.
All binary inputs are
allowed. Distances are Euclidean, and all logarithms are natural.
For a nonempty compact $K\subset Q$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:catalogue}
A_u(n,\delta;K)=\{x\in K:\dist(F_u^kx,Q)<\delta
                    \text{ for every }0\le k\le n\}.
\end{equation}
Let $r_F(n,\delta;K)$ be the smallest number of infinite inputs
whose sets \eqref{eq:catalogue} cover $K$. The list is selected
before the initial state; the assigned input
may depend on that state. We call a prefix \emph{exactly feasible}
from $x$ if all its intermediate states belong to $Q$.
Only the first $n$ letters matter. Under the controlled-invariance
assumption below, the $2^n$ words already cover $Q$, so the minimum
is finite.

A precision sequence means $\delta_n>0$, $\delta_n\to0$ and
$-\log\delta_n/n\to\gamma$ for a finite $\gamma\ge0$.
It need not be monotone. With the convention $[0,N)$ used in
\cite[Section 2.2]{CZ2024}, the exact correspondence is
$r_F(n,\delta;K)=r_{\rm inv}(n+1,\delta,K,Q)$.

\subsection{The class of systems}
Fix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
a_0,a_1\in(0,1),\quad a_0+a_1=1,\quad a_0\ne a_1,\quad
\beta_0,\beta_1>1,
\end{equation}
and set $r_i=a_i^{\beta_i}$, $t_i=r_i/a_i<1$,
$a_* =\min_i a_i$, $a^*=\max_i a_i$ and $h=-\sum_i a_i\log a_i$.
Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jets}
\mathsf A_0=\begin{pmatrix}r_0&0\\t_0a_1&t_0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathsf A_1=\begin{pmatrix}r_1&0\\-t_1a_0&t_1\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The linear model in the introduction has $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$.
We call this the \emph{matched} case. Allowing the two powers to differ
will let us identify its role in Appendix~\ref{sec:mismatch}; the
main spectrum and reliability formulas use matched powers.
Throughout the paper, except where a separate example is specified,
we impose the following assumptions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F_0,F_1:\R^2\to\R^2$ are $C^2$ maps,
$F_i(0)=0$ and $DF_i(0)=\mathsf A_i$.
\item $Q\subset F_0^{-1}Q\cup F_1^{-1}Q$.
\item For some $\Lambda\ge1$ and $0<\theta<1$, the norm
$V(s,z)=\max\{\Lambda|s|,|z|\}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contract}
V(F_i x)\le\theta V(x)\qquad(x\in\R^2,\ i=0,1).
\end{equation}
\item Each critical set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical}
\mathcal C_i=\{x\in Q:\det DF_i(x)=0\}
\end{equation}
has area zero.
\end{enumerate}
The second assumption is one-step controlled invariance; unrestricted
concatenation yields an exactly feasible infinite input from each
point of $Q$. The third concerns contraction toward the origin,
not distances between two arbitrary trajectories. In particular it
controls appended tails even outside $Q$. The fourth ensures that
an exactly feasible finite sequence cannot
send a positive-area set to a null set (Lemma~\ref{lem:nullpullback}).
It still allows folds. In the reliability theorem it will be
strengthened to exclude critical points altogether. Conditions on
critical points outside $Q$ are unnecessary: the derivative factors
used before a first exit are evaluated inside $Q$.

For comparison, a fixed tolerance has zero exponential cost.
Indeed $\|x\|_2\le\sqrt2 V(x)$, and \eqref{eq:contract} gives a
fixed $J_\delta$ such that every input sends $Q$ into the
$\delta$-ball about zero after $J_\delta$ steps and keeps it there.
Choose an exactly feasible prefix of that length for each initial
state and then append an arbitrary tail. The at most
$2^{J_\delta}$ prefixes cover $Q$ for every longer horizon.
Shrinking precision is what prevents this finite-transient
argument from making the asymptotic question trivial.

Both matched and mismatched subfamilies are nonempty. For the linear
maps $\mathsf A_i$, the angular domains
$[-1,a_0-a_1]$ and $[a_0-a_1,1]$ cover every fibre.
Put $t=\max_i t_i<1$ and choose $\Lambda=1+4t/(1-t)$.
Then $V(\mathsf A_i x)\le
\max\{\max_i r_i,t(1+1/\Lambda)\}V(x)$ globally, with factor
less than one, and $\det\mathsf A_i=r_i^2/a_i>0$.

\subsection{Full coverage and a fixed large initial set}
Assume for now that $r_i=a_i^\beta$. The formula for the spectrum
uses the frequency $p$ of zero in a word. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropycost}
H_b(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p),\qquad
\chi(p)=-p\log a_0-(1-p)\log a_1,
\end{equation}
with $0\log0=0$. Among length-$m$ words with about $pm$ zeros,
the count is $\exp(mH_b(p)+o(m))$, and each has angular weight
$\exp(-m\chi(p)+o(m))$. Its radial scale is therefore
$\exp(-\beta m\chi(p)+o(m))$. At precision $\exp(-\gamma n)$,
one follows such words for a fraction
$\min\{1,\gamma/(\beta\chi(p))\}$ of the horizon. This explains
the candidate full-cover rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variational}
S(\gamma)=\max_{p\in[0,1]}H_b(p)
                  \min\{1,\gamma/[\beta\chi(p)]\}.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proves this formula for the nonlinear
systems as well. It also compares full coverage with coverage of
a compact initial set fixed in advance. Recall
$H_b(a_0)=\chi(a_0)=h$.

\begin{theorem}[Precision spectrum and initial-set dependence]\label{thm:main}
Suppose $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$ in the standing assumptions.
For every fixed system,
every finite $\gamma\ge0$ and every precision sequence of exponent
$\gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
\lim_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)}n=S(\gamma).
\end{equation}
Every fixed positive-area compact $K\subset Q$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal}
\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}\le
\liminf_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K)}n\le
\limsup_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K)}n\le
S(\gamma).
\end{equation}
Thus the rate exists and is $\gamma/\beta$ for $0\le\gamma\le\beta h$.
For each $\zeta\in(0,1)$ there is one fixed compact
$K_\zeta\subset Q$ with $\area(K_\zeta)>1-\zeta$ such that,
simultaneously for every finite $\gamma\ge0$ and every precision
sequence of that exponent,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high}
\lim_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)}n
                         =\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Closed spectrum and two thresholds]\label{cor:thresholds}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, put
$a=a_*\in(0,1/2)$,
$c_b=-\log(1-a)$ and $d_b=\log((1-a)/a)>0$.
The first threshold and the saturation threshold are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thresholds}
\gamma_*=\beta h,\qquad
\gamma_{\rm sat}=-\frac\beta2\log(a_0a_1)
                  =\beta(c_b+d_b/2)>\gamma_*.
\end{equation}
The full-set spectrum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed}
S(\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
\gamma/\beta,&0\le\gamma\le\gamma_*,\\[2pt]
H_b\!\left((\gamma/\beta-c_b)/d_b\right),
      &\gamma_*<\gamma<\gamma_{\rm sat},\\[2pt]
\log2,&\gamma\ge\gamma_{\rm sat}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For the set $K_\zeta$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the rate gap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
\Delta(\gamma)=S(\gamma)-\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
It is zero up to $\gamma_*$, strictly positive above $\gamma_*$,
and equal to $\log2-h$ from $\gamma_{\rm sat}$ onwards.
The full-set rate equals $\log2$ exactly when
$\gamma\ge\gamma_{\rm sat}$.
\end{corollary}

The first threshold concerns equality across all positive-area
compact initial sets; the second concerns the full-set catalogue.
The rate of an arbitrary compact initial set above the first
threshold is constrained by \eqref{eq:universal}, but is not
determined by the theorem.

Appendix~\ref{sec:mismatch} shows why the two powers must match
for a common low-precision rate on all positive-area compact sets:
unequal powers give different full-state and near-full-set rates
throughout an interval adjacent to zero.

\subsection{Allowing an exponentially small failure probability}
Let $\mu=\area|_Q$, which is a probability since $\area(Q)=1$.
For $0<e<1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chancecatalogue}
r_e(n,\delta;Q)=\min\left\{\#\mathcal U:
\mu\left(\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal U}A_u(n,\delta;Q)\right)\ge1-e\right\}.
\end{equation}
The minimum is over finite catalogues of unrestricted binary inputs.
It exists because the full-cover count is finite.
A failure sequence has $0<e_n<1$, $e_n\to0$ and
$-\log e_n/n\to\alpha>0$. Both sequences may be nonmonotone.
The covered subset in \eqref{eq:chancecatalogue} may
change with $n$; it is not a fixed compact initial set.


For a family of words with zero frequency $p$, its total reference
probability has exponent $\chi(p)-H_b(p)$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliabilityfunction}
I_a(p)=\chi(p)-H_b(p),\qquad
L_a(\alpha)=\max_{I_a(p)\le\alpha}H_b(p).
\end{equation}
Here $I_a(p)=p\log(p/a_0)+(1-p)\log((1-p)/a_1)$ is the
relative entropy of $(p,1-p)$ with respect to $(a_0,a_1)$.
Frequencies with probability exponent greater than $\alpha$ can
be discarded at the exponential scale. Counting the remaining
words gives $L_a(\alpha)$, the source-coding rate at error exponent
$\alpha$. For nonlinear controls, the word weights need not equal
initial-state probabilities. The next theorem supplies the required
comparison under a stronger derivative assumption.

\begin{theorem}[Catalogue rate with both budgets]\label{thm:reliability}
Assume the standing hypotheses with $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$,
and strengthen the critical-set condition to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noncritical}
\det DF_i(x)\ne0\qquad(x\in Q,\ i=0,1).
\end{equation}
For every fixed such system, every finite $\gamma\ge0$, every
$\alpha>0$, and every precision and failure sequence of those
exponents,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointcost}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)}{n}
                   =\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

For a finite catalogue, enumerate its inputs and assign a successful
state to its first successful input. The sets are Borel, so this is a
measurable initial-state encoder. A fixed-length index sent once at
time zero has minimum bit length $\lceil\log_2 r_e\rceil$; the
asymptotic bit rate is \eqref{eq:jointcost} divided by $\log2$.
There are no later messages: this is the cost of one initial index.

\begin{theorem}[A null critical set can change reliability]
\label{thm:counter}
For $a_0=2^{-10}$, $a_1=1-2^{-10}$ and $\beta=4096$,
there is a fixed injective $C^2$ pair satisfying the standing
matched hypotheses, with critical sets of area zero, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counterfull}
r_F(n,\delta;Q)=r_{\mathsf A}(n,\delta;Q)
                 \qquad(n\ge1,\ \delta>0).
\end{equation}
It maps no positive-area subset of $Q$ into an area-zero set.
Nevertheless, for $\gamma=-\beta\log(a_0a_1)$ and $\alpha=1/8$,
every pair of sequences with these exponents satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counterrate}
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)
\ge\tfrac12\log2>\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The two theorems distinguish nonsingularity from null critical
sets, rather than classify every system with critical points.
Global injectivity is not needed for Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability}:
its proof sums over finitely many local inverse branches.
The injectivity in Theorem~\ref{thm:counter} isolates the possible
failure of area bounds from folding.

\section{Feasible regions near the origin}\label{sec:geometry}
In the linear model, one word serves a narrow angular interval
and contracts its radius. We need estimates for both effects
that remain uniform as the word length grows. There is no need
for the nonlinear interval to have the full angular range as its
image; an upper bound for its size will suffice.

For a word $w$, write $w|k$ for its first $k$ letters and
$F_w=F_{w_{|w|}}\circ\cdots\circ F_{w_1}$. The reference scales are
\[
\lambda_w=\prod_{j=1}^{|w|}a_{w_j},\qquad
P_w=\prod_{j=1}^{|w|}r_{w_j},
\]
with empty products one. We keep the angular and radial products
separate so that the estimates also apply to unequal powers in
Appendix~\ref{sec:mismatch}. They obey $P_w=\lambda_w^\beta$
in the matched case.

\subsection{One step near the origin}
Use \((s,y)\) with \(z=sy\) for \(s>0\). All local estimates
below are on \(0<s\le\rho\), \(|y|\le1\), for a small radius
$\rho$ chosen below. Write $F_i=(F_i^s,F_i^z)$ and define
\[
 R_i(s,y)=F_i^s(s,sy)/s,\quad
 N_i(s,y)=F_i^z(s,sy)/s,\quad T_i=N_i/R_i.
\]
The integral formula
\(F_i(s,sy)/s=\int_0^1DF_i(ts,tsy)(1,y)\,dt\)
extends \(R_i,N_i\) as \(C^1\) functions to \(s=0\).
At zero
\begin{equation}
R_i(0,y)=r_i,\quad
 L_0(y):=T_0(0,y)=(y+a_1)/a_0,\quad
 L_1(y):=T_1(0,y)=(y-a_0)/a_1.                                \label{eq:limangular}
\end{equation}

There are constants $B\ge1$ and $\rho>0$, depending only on the
fixed maps, for which the following estimates hold on
$0\le s\le\rho$, $|y|\le1$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 &|R_i-r_i|\le Bs,\qquad |\log(R_i/r_i)|\le Bs,\\
 &|\partial_s\log R_i|\le B,
          \quad |\partial_y\log R_i|\le Bs,\\
 &|T_i-L_i|\le Bs,\qquad |\partial_sT_i|\le B,
          \quad |\partial_yT_i-1/a_i|\le Bs .                 \label{eq:local}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
We choose $\rho$ small enough to satisfy \eqref{eq:rho} in
Appendix~\ref{app:localconstants}, where the Taylor and quotient
calculations giving these constants are recorded. In particular,
put $r_* =\min_i r_i$ and $r^*=\max_i r_i$. All constants in
this section are fixed before choosing a word. The errors in
\eqref{eq:local} are of order $s$, and radial contraction makes
their sum finite along a feasible trajectory.

Set \(\bar r=r^*+B\rho<1\) and
\(\widehat r=r^*+2B\rho<1\). On an exactly feasible local orbit,
\(s_k\le\bar r^ks_0\). The bounds in \eqref{eq:rho} also give
\(T_1(s,-1)<-1\) and \(T_0(s,1)>1\). Controlled invariance therefore
forces
\begin{equation}
T_0(s,-1)\ge-1,\qquad T_1(s,1)\le1.       \label{eq:edges}
\end{equation}
Equality is not required in \eqref{eq:edges}.
The increasing functions \(T_i(s,\cdot)\) have unique cut points
\(\vartheta_0(s)\) with \(T_0=1\) and \(\vartheta_1(s)\) with
\(T_1=-1\). They equal \(a_0-a_1+O(s)\).
Their feasible intervals are respectively
\([-1,\vartheta_0(s)]\) and \([\vartheta_1(s),1]\).
Controlled invariance gives \(\vartheta_1\le\vartheta_0\).
Thus any local overlap has angular width \(O(s)\) and physical
width \(O(s^2)\). Positivity of the overlap is not assumed.

\subsection{Following an interval through several controls}
Start with a fibre $s=s_0$ and vary its angle. Its image need
not be another fibre: the output radius can depend on the angle.
Represent that image as $s=S(y)$. A bound on $S'/S$ controls
the extra term in its angular derivative, and will survive
iteration because the inverse angular branches contract.

Consider a radial graph \(s=S(y)\), on any closed subinterval of
\([-1,1]\), with \(|S'/S|\le1\). Put \(k=S'/S\).
For either input its terminal angular derivative along the graph is
\[
 J_i=\partial_yT_i+\partial_sT_i S'
           =1/a_i+O(BS)>0.
\]
The logarithmic derivative of the image radius
$S(y)R_i(S(y),y)$ with respect to $y$ is the numerator below.
Dividing by $J_i$ expresses that derivative in the new angular
coordinate:
\begin{equation}
k_{\rm new}
 =\frac{k+(\partial_s\log R_i)S'+\partial_y\log R_i}{J_i}.
                                                                  \label{eq:coneupdate}
\end{equation}
Thus the new logarithmic slope is again at most one in absolute
value: its numerator is at most \(1+2B\rho\) in absolute value and its
denominator at least \(1/a^*-2B\rho\). By \eqref{eq:rho}, \(|k_{\rm new}|\le1\).
Piecewise \(C^1\) graphs obey the same assertion almost everywhere.

For a fixed initial radius \(0<s_0\le\rho\), start with the horizontal
graph \(S\equiv s_0\). Let \(I_w(s_0)\) be the set of initial angles
whose trajectory under a finite word \(w\) stays in $Q$ at every
intermediate time. We claim that it is a closed interval, possibly empty or a point.
The claim follows by induction, starting with $[-1,1]$ for the
empty word. Suppose the current angular image is an interval
whose radial graph satisfies \eqref{eq:coneupdate}. Extend that graph
constantly to the two sides of its domain. The extension is positive,
bounded by \(\rho\), and obeys the logarithmic-slope bound almost
everywhere. Both \(T_i(S(y),y)\) are increasing. By \eqref{eq:edges}, control 0
cannot violate the lower angular boundary, and control 1 cannot
violate the upper one. The other boundary imposes one cut. Pulling
that cut back through the increasing preceding angular map leaves
an interval. Its image is a subinterval of $[-1,1]$.
Controlled invariance ensures that the two children together cover
the parent, including endpoints; one child may be empty.

Let $Y_w$ denote the final angular coordinate as a function of the
initial angle on $I_w(s_0)$. Each step changes the linear radial
factor and angular derivative by a relative error of order $s_j$.
Since $\sum_j s_j\le\rho/(1-\bar r)$, multiplying over a word
produces a bounded distortion, independent of its length:

\begin{equation}
C_R^{-1}s_0P_w\le s_{|w|}
                  \le C_Rs_0P_w,\qquad
 C_A^{-1}\lambda_w^{-1}\le \frac{dY_w}{dy_0}
                  \le C_A\lambda_w^{-1}.                    \label{eq:products}
\end{equation}
For instance one may take
\(C_R=\exp(B\rho/(1-\bar r))\),
\(C_A=\exp(4B\rho/(1-\bar r))\):
\(|a_iJ_i-1|\le2Bs_j\le1/8\), so its logarithm has modulus at
most \(4Bs_j\). The first bound sums $\log(R_i/r_i)$; the second sums the
logarithms of the angular derivatives. Since the terminal angular
interval has length at most two, the derivative lower bound gives
$|I_w(s_0)|\le2C_A\lambda_w$. Integration over initial radii,
with area element $s_0\,ds_0\,dy_0$, yields
\begin{equation}
|I_w(s_0)|\le2C_A\lambda_w,
 \quad |\mathcal I_w|\le C_A\rho^2\lambda_w,
 \quad \mathcal I_w=\{(s,sy):0<s\le\rho,\ y\in I_w(s)\}.      \label{eq:striparea}
\end{equation}
There is no corresponding positive lower bound for
$|I_w(s_0)|$: the example in Section~\ref{sec:inward} has empty
sections at some positive radii.

Endpoints are included by continuity. A degenerate interval must
still be retained in a full cover, although it contributes no area.
\subsection{The first true exit}
We now fix an input and allow a tolerance $\delta>0$.
A state it serves either remains in $Q$ or has a first exit.
At that exit the preceding graph estimates still apply, because
all earlier states lie in $Q$. The tolerance bounds the angular
overshoot by $\delta$ divided by the current radius. This gives
the additional area served outside the exact feasible region.

Suppose the prefix \(w\) of length \(j-1\) is exactly feasible and
the next prescribed input \(i\) is the first one to leave $Q$.
On the current graph the map \(T_i\) is increasing with derivative
at least \(1/(2a_i)\). The radial output is at least
\(c s_0P_w\), by \eqref{eq:local},\eqref{eq:products}, with \(c>0\) fixed.
If this wrong step nonetheless has distance less than \(\delta\)
from $Q$, then
\begin{equation}
(|z_j|-s_j)_+<2\delta.                    \label{eq:exitnecessary}
\end{equation}
This follows from the Euclidean Lipschitz linear functionals
\(z-s\) and \(-z-s\). The output radius is positive and at most
\(\rho\), so the violated constraint is angular, not the upper radius.
Fix $s_0$ and regard the current state as a point on the image
graph after $w$. On the side where input $i$ violates the constraint,
\eqref{eq:exitnecessary} and the radial lower bound give
\[
0<|T_i(S(y),y)|-1<\frac{2\delta}{c s_0P_w}.
\]
Only one angular boundary can be crossed, by \eqref{eq:edges}.
The derivative lower bound for $T_i$ along this graph therefore
limits this set of current angles to length
$C\delta/(s_0P_w)$. Pulling it back by the preceding angular map
in \eqref{eq:products} gives initial-angle length at most
\[
                         C\delta\lambda_w/(s_0P_w).
\]
Integration against \(s_0\,ds_0\) then bounds its physical area by
\begin{equation}
C\delta\lambda_w/P_w.     \label{eq:exitarea}
\end{equation}
The monotonicity argument bounds the overshoot set even if the
cut lies outside the terminal image or that image is a point.
An exit from $Q$ may still respect the tolerance. Estimate~\eqref{eq:exitarea}
bounds all states that do so at this first exit; imposing the later
constraints, with or without reentry, can only reduce their area.
\section{States that force a control prefix}\label{sec:shadow}
Counting feasible word labels does not give a catalogue lower
bound, because their regions may overlap. We instead
construct states at which the wrong next control violates the
constraint by a definite angular amount. A catalogue that serves
such a state must follow its chosen prefix until radial contraction
makes that violation smaller than the tolerance.

For the linear branches this is achieved by avoiding long strings
of equal digits. We impose a fixed bound $L$ on each such run.
The resulting reference angles stay away from $-1$ and $1$,
leaving room to solve the nonlinear orbit equations and to keep
the alternative control infeasible.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-run realisation]\label{lem:shadow}
For each integer \(L\ge2\), there is a positive radius \(s_L\)
and \(d_L'>0\) such that, for every \(0<s\le s_L\), every
infinite binary sequence whose successive runs have length at most
\(L\) is realised by an actual exactly feasible orbit starting at
\((s,sy_0)\).
At every step of that orbit the alternative control is genuinely
infeasible, with an angular excess at least \(d_L'\).
The initial angle can be chosen Lipschitz in $s$. The radius
bound, the margin and the Lipschitz constant depend only on $L$
and the fixed system.

\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{A reference orbit away from the boundary.}
The angular maps expand forward, so it is convenient to construct
the orbit using their contracting inverses. These are
\(f_0(y)=a_0y-a_1\), \(f_1(y)=a_1y+a_0\).
For any fixed infinite input \(v\), successive inverse compositions
give a unique reference bounded orbit \(y_k^0\in[-1,1]\).
If runs have length at most \(L\), the next opposite digit is seen
within \(L+1\) digits. Since
\(f_0(y)+1=a_0(y+1)\), \(1-f_1(y)=a_1(1-y)\), and each opposite
digit inserts an endpoint distance at least \(2a_*\),
\begin{equation}
|y_k^0|\le1-d_L,
              \qquad d_L=2a_*^{L+1}>0.                     \label{eq:refmargin}
\end{equation}

\smallskip
\emph{Solving for the nonlinear orbit.}
The nonlinear radius and angle equations are coupled. Given a
candidate angle sequence, first solve its forward radial recursion.
Substitute those radii into the inverse angular equations. The
dependence of the radii on the candidate has size $O(s_*^2)$,
which will make this combined operation a contraction when the
initial radius $s_*$ is small.
Let \(b=d_L/4\) and consider the complete closed ball of sequences
\(\|y-y^0\|_\infty\le b\) in \(\ell^\infty\).
For each such sequence and a positive fixed initial radius \(s_*\),
recursively define \(s_{k+1}=s_kR_{v_{k+1}}(s_k,y_k)\).
The bound $|y_k|<1$ places each candidate in the rectangle where
\eqref{eq:local} holds. Hence these radii stay below
$\bar r^ks_*$, even before the angular equation is solved.
The scalar map \(s\mapsto sR_i(s,y)\) has derivative bounded in
absolute value by \(\widehat r<1\), and its \(y\) derivative is
bounded by \(B s^2\), since \(R_i\le\bar r<1\) and \eqref{eq:local} bounds
its logarithmic angular derivative. Thus two candidate sequences give
\begin{equation}
\sup_k|s_k(y)-s_k(\widetilde y)|
       \le C_s s_*^2\|y-\widetilde y\|_\infty,
       \qquad C_s=B/(1-\widehat r).                         \label{eq:radialdependence}
\end{equation}
Write \(e_i(s,y)=T_i(s,y)-L_i(y)\). By \eqref{eq:local},
\(|e_i|\le Bs\), \(|\partial_s e_i|\le B\), and
\(|\partial_y e_i|\le Bs\). Define
\begin{equation}
(\mathcal By)_k=f_{v_{k+1}}(y_{k+1})
         -a_{v_{k+1}}e_{v_{k+1}}(s_k(y),y_k).               \label{eq:shadowoperator}
\end{equation}
Choose \(s_*\le\rho\) so small that
\begin{equation}
a^*Bs_*\le(1-a^*)b/2,
 \qquad a^*B(s_*+C_s s_*^2)<(1-a^*)/2.                     \label{eq:shadowbounds}
\end{equation}
The reference orbit satisfies $y_k^0=f_{v_{k+1}}(y_{k+1}^0)$.
Consequently, for every candidate in the ball,
\[
\|\mathcal By-y^0\|_\infty\le a^*b+a^*Bs_*<b.
\]
This proves that the ball is invariant. To compare two candidates,
use \eqref{eq:radialdependence} in the nonlinear term of
\eqref{eq:shadowoperator}. The result is
\[
\|\mathcal By-\mathcal B\widetilde y\|_\infty
\le\bigl[a^*+a^*B(s_*+C_s s_*^2)\bigr]
                                      \|y-\widetilde y\|_\infty.
\]
By \eqref{eq:shadowbounds}, the bracket is less than one.
The contraction mapping theorem gives a fixed sequence. Its fixed-point
equation is \(y_{k+1}=T_{v_{k+1}}(s_k,y_k)\), the required angular
recursion. It has \(|y_k|\le1-3d_L/4\), so the constructed
physical trajectory is exactly feasible for all times.

\smallskip
\emph{Keeping the other control infeasible.}
The constructed angles lie at least $3d_L/4$ from the endpoints.
Use this margin in the equation for the chosen control to locate
the angle relative to the other control's cut. For a chosen zero,
\(T_0(s_k,y_k)=y_{k+1}\le1-3d_L/4\), and \eqref{eq:local} gives
\[
 y_k\le a_0-a_1-3a_0d_L/4+a_0Bs_k.
\]
Substitution into \(T_1=L_1+e_1\) yields
\[
 T_1(s_k,y_k)\le-1-\frac{3a_0d_L}{4a_1}
                           +B(1+a_0/a_1)s_k.
\]
For chosen digit one, the symmetric computation gives
\[
 T_0(s_k,y_k)\ge1+\frac{3a_1d_L}{4a_0}
                           -B(1+a_1/a_0)s_k.
\]
Shrink \(s_*\) once more, before considering any particular sequence,
so that these last error terms are at most
\(a_*d_L/(4a^*)\). Both wrong angular excesses are then at least
\(d_L'=a_*d_L/(2a^*)\). Set \(s_L=s_*\).
All choices depend only on $L$ and the fixed system, and the same
estimates hold for every $0<s\le s_L$. We shall later thicken
these orbits over a radial interval, so we also record how the
chosen angles vary with the initial radius.
For a fixed candidate sequence, scalar contraction gives
$\sup_k|s_k(s,y)-s_k(s',y)|\le|s-s'|$.
If $\kappa_L<1$ is the common contraction constant of
\eqref{eq:shadowoperator}, the fixed-point equations therefore give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shadowcontinuous}
\|y^v(s)-y^v(s')\|_\infty
 \le\frac{a^*B}{1-\kappa_L}|s-s'|.
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

The remaining issue is the physical scale of this angular margin.
A word with radial cost $c$ per letter can force roughly
$\min\{n,-\log\delta/c\}$ digits. To count words while preserving
the run bound, concatenate blocks with fixed first and last digits.


\begin{lemma}[Forcing a block prefix]\label{lem:prefixpacking}
Fix $L\ge2$ and $1\le j\le L-1$, and put
\[
M_L=\binom{L-2}{j-1},\qquad
c_L=-\frac jL\log r_0-\left(1-\frac jL\right)\log r_1.
\]
For every precision sequence of exponent $\gamma>0$ and every
$0<t<\min\{1,\gamma/c_L\}$, for all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockpacking}
r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)\ge M_L^{\lfloor tn/L\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose blocks with a prescribed frequency, but fix their first
and last digits so that concatenation cannot create long runs.
Specifically, use all length-$L$ blocks starting with zero, ending
with one, and containing $j$ zeros. Every concatenation has runs of length
at most $L$ and radial product $e^{-Lc_L}$ per complete block.
Put $k_n=\lfloor tn/L\rfloor$ and $m_n=Lk_n$.
For each of the $M_L^{k_n}$ initial concatenations, append one
fixed allowable block forever. Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow} realises
these infinite inputs at the same positive radius $s_L$.
Every prefix through time $m_n$ has
$P_{v|k}\ge P_{v|m_n}=e^{-c_Lm_n}$.

If an arbitrary catalogue input first differs from a witness at
$j'\le m_n$, its preceding states agree with that witness.
By \eqref{eq:products}, their radius at $j'-1$ is at least
$C_R^{-1}s_Le^{-c_Lm_n}$. The wrong radial factor is at least
$r_*/2$, and the alternative angular excess is at least $d_L'$.
Its actual physical excess therefore obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physicalmargin}
|z_{j'}|-s_{j'}\ge E_Le^{-c_Lm_n},\qquad
E_L=\frac{s_Lr_*d_L'}{2C_R}>0.
\end{equation}
The ratio to $2\delta_n$ has logarithmic rate
$\gamma-c_Lt>0$. For large $n$, it violates
\eqref{eq:exitnecessary}, regardless of later reentry.
Thus an input serving a witness must agree with its first $m_n$
digits. The witnesses themselves are distinct: otherwise two
selected inputs would both be feasible from one state, contradicting
the margin at their first different digit. Each catalogue member
therefore serves at most one witness, proving \eqref{eq:blockpacking}.
The block length, positive radius, and margin remain fixed throughout
this horizon limit. Integer rounding and every $o(n)$ precision
fluctuation are absorbed by the strict choice of $t$.
\end{proof}

\section{The full-cover precision spectrum}\label{sec:rates}
We first prove the full-cover assertion \eqref{eq:spectrum} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and evaluate $S$. An upper catalogue
follows feasible prefixes until contraction makes further choices
irrelevant. The forced prefixes of Section~\ref{sec:shadow} give
the matching lower bound. Area, which is needed for the assertions
about a fixed initial set, enters in Section~\ref{sec:area}.

Choose $J\ge1$, depending only on the fixed system, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localentry}
\theta^J\sup_Q V\le\rho,
\qquad Q_\rho=Q\cap\{s\le\rho\}.
\end{equation}
Every exactly feasible $J$-prefix ends in $Q_\rho$. We can therefore
attach the local construction below to each of the at most $2^J$
initial prefixes. We call these first $J$ steps the transient.

A stopped tree records the finite prefixes used by the construction.
Starting from the empty word, continue each branch until its radial
product is at most the threshold, or until the permitted depth is
reached. The words where the branches stop are its leaves. The tree
contains all binary words subject to this rule; some may have no
feasible states in the nonlinear system.

\begin{lemma}[A catalogue obtained by radial stopping]\label{lem:stopping}
Put $M=\max\{1,4\sqrt2\Lambda C_R\rho\}$.
For $N\ge0$ and $0<t\le1$, let $\mathcal L_P(N,t)$ be the full
reference binary tree stopped at the first $P_w\le t$, or at
depth $N$, whichever occurs first. For $n\ge J$ and $0<\delta\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialupper}
r_F(n,\delta;Q)\le 2^J\#\mathcal L_P(n-J,\delta/M).
\end{equation}
The catalogue can retain only transient/leaf pairs whose complete
actual feasible initial region is nonempty.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every actual initial state choose an exactly feasible infinite
input. Its first $J$ letters form a feasible transient ending in
$Q_\rho$; the subsequent letters reach a reference leaf.
Keep every pair realised by an actual state, including singleton
regions and boundary states. Before stopping the assigned states
are exactly feasible. At a threshold leaf, \eqref{eq:products} gives
\[
s_{|w|}\le C_R\rho P_w\le C_R\rho\,\delta/M
                         \le\delta/(4\sqrt2\Lambda).
\]
Since $V=\Lambda s$ on $Q$, appending the same infinite zero tail
and using \eqref{eq:contract} keeps the Euclidean norm at most
$\delta/4$ at every later time. The origin belongs to $Q$,
so every constraint holds strictly on that tail. A horizon leaf
is exactly feasible through time $n$, and its later extension is
irrelevant. Every constructed input fixes the origin as well.
There are at most $2^J$ transients, proving \eqref{eq:radialupper}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counting the stopping leaves}
For the rest of this section assume $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$.
The counting uses only the length of a leaf and its number of zeros.
A class of words with the same frequency is also called a
\emph{type}. There are only polynomially many length/type pairs,
so their largest exponential count determines the rate.
Matching gives $P_w=\lambda_w^\beta$. Put
\[
t_\delta=(\delta/M)^{1/\beta},\qquad b_\delta=-\log t_\delta.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} uses the tree stopped at
$\lambda_w\le t_\delta$ or at depth $N=n-J$.
Put $c_i=-\log a_i$, $c_{\min}=\min_i c_i>0$,
$c_{\max}=\max_i c_i$, and
$\Gamma_{n,\delta}=\beta(b_\delta+c_{\max})/n$.
A threshold leaf has logarithmic angular cost in
$[b_\delta,b_\delta+c_{\max})$; an unfinished horizon leaf
has cost below $b_\delta$. Thus every nonempty leaf of length
$\ell\le n$, containing $j$ zeros with $p=j/\ell$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leafcost}
\ell\chi(p)<b_\delta+c_{\max},\qquad
\frac{\ell}{n}\le
       \min\{1,\Gamma_{n,\delta}/[\beta\chi(p)]\}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding term of the binomial law of parameter $j/\ell$
gives $\binom{\ell}{j}\le e^{\ell H_b(j/\ell)}$.
Endpoint types have one word. Each leaf type therefore has at most
$e^{nS(\Gamma_{n,\delta})}$ words. There are at most $(n+1)^2$
length/type pairs, including a possible empty word. By the actual
catalogue construction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:treeupper}
r_F(n,\delta;Q)\le 2^J(n+1)^2e^{nS(\Gamma_{n,\delta})}.
\end{equation}

Every function under the maximum defining $S$ is Lipschitz in
$\gamma$, with common constant at most
$\log2/(\beta c_{\min})$. Their maximum is continuous.
Here $\beta b_{\delta_n}=-\log\delta_n+\log M$, so
$\Gamma_{n,\delta_n}\to\gamma$ for every precision sequence.
The fixed transient and polynomial factors have zero rate, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrumupper}
\limsup_n n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)\le S(\gamma).
\end{equation}
This includes $\gamma=0$ and nonmonotone sequences.

The reference angular leaf weights sum to one, and each is at least
$a_*t_\delta$: a threshold leaf has parent weight above $t_\delta$,
whereas an unfinished leaf has weight above $t_\delta$ itself.
This identity concerns a reference tree, not a physical partition.
Enlarging a constant for $n<J$ yields the useful coarse bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:upper}
r_F(n,\delta;Q)\le C_U\delta^{-1/\beta}
       \quad(0<\delta\le1),\qquad r_F(n,\delta;Q)\le2^n .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Forcing enough prefixes}
The previous count includes all reference words, whether realised
or not. To match it from below, approximate each frequency by the
bounded-run blocks of Lemma~\ref{lem:prefixpacking}. The boundary
digits cost only a subexponential factor as the block length grows.
Let $\gamma>0$ and fix $p\in(0,1)$.
Choose $j_L\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$ with $j_L/L\to p$, and put
\[
M_L=\binom{L-2}{j_L-1},\qquad
B_L=L^{-1}\log M_L,\qquad \chi_L=\chi(j_L/L).
\]
The exact identity and elementary type bounds
\[
\binom{L-2}{j-1}=\frac{j(L-j)}{L(L-1)}\binom Lj,\qquad
\frac{e^{LH_b(j/L)}}{L+1}\le\binom Lj\le e^{LH_b(j/L)}
\]
give $B_L\to H_b(p)$ and $\chi_L\to\chi(p)$.
For the lower type bound the $j$-th term is a largest term of
the binomial law with parameter $j/L$, so is at least $1/(L+1)$.
The additional fraction has logarithm $o(L)$ for interior $p$.

The radial block cost in Lemma~\ref{lem:prefixpacking} is now
$c_L=\beta\chi_L$. Fix $L$ first and take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefixratio}
0<t<\min\{1,\gamma/(\beta\chi_L)\}.
\end{equation}
The lemma gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefixlower}
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)\ge tB_L.
\end{equation}
For fixed $L$ take the supremum over the strict $t$, next let
$L\to\infty$, and finally take the supremum over $p\in(0,1)$.
Endpoint frequencies have zero entropy, so continuity yields
$\liminf n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)\ge S(\gamma)$.
No witness radius or margin is required to remain uniformly positive
as $L\to\infty$; they are fixed in each horizon limit.
For $\gamma=0$, the bound $r_F\ge1$ and
\eqref{eq:spectrumupper} give the zero limit.
This proves \eqref{eq:spectrum}.

\subsection{Evaluation of the maximum}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:thresholds}]
Reparameterise the maximum by the frequency $q$ of the smaller
branch $a=a_*$. In this variable $\chi=c_b+d_bq$.
Write $x=\gamma/\beta$. The entropy inequality
\[
c_b+d_bq-H_b(q)=
q\log(q/a)+(1-q)\log((1-q)/(1-a))\ge0
\]
follows from $-\log v\ge1-v$ and extends to the endpoints
by continuity; equality holds at $q=a$.
For $x\le h$, the objective
$H_b(q)\min\{1,x/(c_b+d_bq)\}$ is at most $x$
and equals $x$ at $a$.

For $h<x<c_b+d_b/2$ put $q_x=(x-c_b)/d_b\in(a,1/2)$.
When $q\le q_x$ the objective is $H_b(q)\le H_b(q_x)$.
When $q\ge q_x$ it is $xH_b(q)/(c_b+d_bq)$.
This ratio decreases for $q>a$: the numerator of its
derivative is
\[
D(q)=(c_b+d_bq)H_b'(q)-d_bH_b(q),\qquad
D(a)=0,\qquad D'(q)=(c_b+d_bq)H_b''(q)<0.
\]
The maximum is therefore $H_b(q_x)$.
Finally, for $x\ge c_b+d_b/2$, the value at $q=1/2$ is
$\log2$, the universal entropy maximum.
Since $a<1/2$, $c_b+d_b/2>c_b+d_ba=h$.
The three pieces match at the thresholds, proving
\eqref{eq:thresholds}--\eqref{eq:closed} and the low-side assertion
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, once the fixed-set bounds are proved.
The gap formula follows by subtracting \eqref{eq:high}.
Strict increase of $H_b$ on $(a,1/2)$ proves the stated
strict gap and the exact saturation threshold.
\end{proof}

\section{Area estimates and fixed initial sets}\label{sec:area}
We have counted enough inputs to cover $Q$. To prove a lower
bound on an arbitrary positive-area set $K$, estimate the area
that one input can serve. If this is at most $b_n$ on a fixed
subset $T\subset K$ of positive area, every cover needs at least
$\area(T)/b_n$ inputs.

The local estimates involve the word products $\lambda_w$ and
$P_w$. Almost every initial state has only feasible tails whose
zero frequency tends to $a_0$. Their angular and radial costs per
letter therefore tend to $h$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialparameters}
\chi_R=-\sum_i a_i\log r_i>h,
\end{equation}
respectively; $\chi_R=\beta h$ when the powers match.
The proof below obtains this statement from the strip-area bound,
without assuming a probability law on nonlinear itineraries.

Two uniform bounds are needed on $T$: convergence of these word
costs, and bounded inverse area through the first $J$ steps.
Null critical sets allow both after deleting an arbitrarily small
fixed area. In the resulting estimate, the three terms count
an exit during those first steps, local survival, and a later
first exit. The parameter $m$ is how long we inspect the local
tail; it will be chosen to balance the last two terms.

\begin{proposition}[Area served by one input]\label{thm:area}
Under the standing assumptions, whether matched or mismatched,
there is a fixed integer $J\ge1$ with
the following property. For every positive-area compact $K\subset Q$
and every $0<\varepsilon<\area(K)$,
one can choose a compact $T\subset K\setminus\{0\}$ with
$\area(T)>\area(K)-\varepsilon$ and $C_T<\infty$
so that for every $\eta\in(0,h)$ there is $C_{T,\eta}<\infty$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualarea}
\area(T\cap A_u(n,\delta;K))\le C_T\delta+
C_{T,\eta}\left[e^{-(h-\eta)m}+\delta e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)m}\right]
\end{equation}
for every input $u$, integers $n\ge J$ and $0\le m\le n-J$,
and $0<\delta\le1$.
The integer depends only on the fixed system. The set and $C_T$
precede $\eta,n,\delta,u$; the second constant may also depend on $\eta$.
In particular, for $K=Q$ the set can have area greater than
$1-\zeta$ for any prescribed $\zeta\in(0,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Pulling area back through the first few steps}
For a finite word \(w\), let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:feasibledomains}
D_w=\{x\in Q:F_{w|k}x\in Q\ (1\le k\le|w|)\},
\qquad D_\varnothing=Q.
\end{equation}
These sets are compact. Write \(w^-\) for the word with its last
letter removed. At a first true exit under \(w\), all derivative
inputs still lie in \(Q\), so the relevant domain is \(D_{w^-}\).
The last output need not belong to \(Q\).

\begin{lemma}[Null pullbacks along feasible prefixes]\label{lem:nullpullback}
For every Lebesgue null set \(B\subset\R^2\), every control \(i\),
and every finite word \(w\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nullpullback}
\area(Q\cap F_i^{-1}B)=0,\qquad
\area(D_w\cap F_w^{-1}B)=0.
\end{equation}
For each nonempty word the compact set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compositecritical}
Z_w=D_{w^-}\cap\{x:\det DF_w(x)=0\}
\end{equation}
also has area zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We begin with one control. Away from its critical set, it is locally
invertible, and each local inverse preserves null sets. More
precisely, the inverse function theorem supplies an open inverse
chart at every point of \(Q\setminus\mathcal C_i\). A countable family of
these charts covers that set. Exhaust each image by relatively
compact subcharts; its \(C^1\) inverse is Lipschitz on sufficiently
small compact neighbourhoods. A Lipschitz map preserves null sets:
cover a bounded null set by squares with arbitrarily small total
area, and cover their images by discs whose total area is bounded
by a constant times that sum. Thus every chart pullback of \(B\)
is null. The omitted \(\mathcal C_i\) is null by
\eqref{eq:critical}. A Borel null superset handles an arbitrary
Lebesgue null set.

Now follow a word only while it remains feasible. For \(w=vi\),
this restriction gives
\[
D_w\cap F_w^{-1}B
\subset D_v\cap F_v^{-1}(Q\cap F_i^{-1}B).
\]
Induction proves the second assertion of \eqref{eq:nullpullback},
including the empty word.
Finally, on \(D_{w^-}\) the chain rule expresses \(\det DF_w\)
as a product of determinants evaluated at states in \(Q\).
If the factor at time \(j\) vanishes, the initial state belongs to
\[
D_{w|j-1}\cap F_{w|j-1}^{-1}\mathcal C_{w_j}.
\]
This is null by \eqref{eq:nullpullback}. Their finite union
contains \(Z_w\), which is compact by continuity and closedness
of the feasibility domain.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One compact set for all precision exponents}
A separate exceptional set for each precision sequence would not
prove the fixed-set assertion in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. We
therefore first obtain uniform frequency convergence on one compact
set. All later choices of $m$, $\delta$ and $n$ use that same set.

Keep the entry time $J$ and the neighbourhood $Q_\rho$ from
\eqref{eq:localentry}.

\begin{lemma}[A fixed compact set with uniform estimates]\label{lem:regularwindow}
For every positive-area compact \(K\subset Q\) and
\(0<\varepsilon<\area(K)\), there is a compact
\(T\subset K\setminus\{0\}\), with
\(\area(T)>\area(K)-\varepsilon\), and \(L_T<\infty\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitecharts}
\area(T\cap D_{w^-}\cap F_w^{-1}B)\le L_T\area(B)
\end{equation}
for all \(1\le|w|\le J\) and all Lebesgue measurable
\(B\subset\R^2\).
On the same set, for every \(\eta\in(0,h)\) there is \(D_{\eta}\ge1\) such
that every local exactly feasible tail \(v\), after any exactly
feasible \(J\)-prefix from \(T\), satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalproducts}
\begin{split}
D_{\eta}^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)|v|}&\le\lambda_v\le D_{\eta} e^{-(h-\eta)|v|},\\
D_{\eta}^{-1}e^{-(\chi_R+\eta)|v|}&\le P_v\le D_{\eta} e^{-(\chi_R-\eta)|v|}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The set and \(L_T\) precede \(\eta\), every input, tolerance and horizon.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Typical frequencies for all feasible words.}
The numbers $\lambda_v$ form a Bernoulli word distribution,
even though the nonlinear feasible regions need not partition
the triangle. This reference distribution is useful because
\eqref{eq:striparea} bounds the area of each such region by a
constant times $\lambda_v$. A union bound will transfer a tail
estimate for words to an area estimate for states. For \(d>0\), the total weight \(\sum\lambda_v\) of length-\(m\)
words with \(|\#0/m-a_0|>d\) is at most \(2e^{-2d^2m}\).
Indeed, for a Bernoulli variable \(X\) of mean \(a_0\),
the second derivative of
\(\log\mathbb E e^{t(X-a_0)}\) is a tilted variance, at most
\(1/4\). Its value and derivative at zero vanish, so it is at most
\(t^2/8\). Exponential Markov with \(t=4d\) and \(t=-4d\)
gives the bound.
By \eqref{eq:striparea}, the unions of the corresponding complete
local feasible regions have summable area. Borel--Cantelli, applied to rational \(d>0\), gives a Borel null set \(B_{\rm loc}\subset Q_\rho\),
including the origin, with the following property. At each point
outside it, the maximum deviation $|\#0/m-a_0|$ over all feasible
length-$m$ words tends to zero. Controlled invariance makes this
family nonempty. Because we bounded the union over all atypical
words, the conclusion holds for every feasible choice in an
overlap.

\smallskip
\emph{Choosing the compact set.}
We next transfer this almost-everywhere statement through all
possible transient words. Lemma~\ref{lem:nullpullback} shows that
\[
B_J=\bigcup_{|\sigma|=J}
       (D_\sigma\cap F_\sigma^{-1}B_{\rm loc})
\]
is null. The finite union
\(Z_J=\bigcup_{1\le|w|\le J}Z_w\) is a compact null set.
For $m\ge1$, define on $Q$
\[
\omega_m(x)=\max\left\{
 \left|\frac{\#0(v)}m-a_0\right|:
 |\sigma|=J,\ |v|=m,\ x\in D_{\sigma v}\right\}.
\]
Controlled invariance makes the maximum nonempty. It is measurable
because the family is finite and each feasibility domain is closed.
The local conclusion gives $\omega_m(x)\to0$ on $Q\setminus B_J$;
the maximum includes every feasible transient and tail, including
all choices in an overlap. Egorov's theorem on $K$, followed by removal of
\(B_J\cup Z_J\cup\{0\}\) and compact inner approximation,
gives \(T\) of the stated area with uniform convergence.
Allocate less than \(\varepsilon/2\) loss to Egorov and less than
\(\varepsilon/2\) to inner approximation.
Both logarithmic products are affine functions of frequency.
Uniform convergence gives \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}; finitely many
early lengths are absorbed in $D_\eta$.

\smallskip
\emph{A uniform inverse-area constant on that set.}
For a word \(w\) with \(1\le|w|\le J\), the compact set
\(M_w=T\cap D_{w^-}\) is disjoint from \(Z_w\).
The composition \(F_w\) is therefore regular at every point of
\(M_w\). Cover it by finitely many open inverse charts \(U_{w,k}\),
with compact closures inside regular inverse charts. Change of
variables on each chart gives
\[
\area(M_w\cap U_{w,k}\cap F_w^{-1}B)
\le
\left(\sup_{\overline U_{w,k}}|\det DF_w|^{-1}\right)\area(B).
\]
The supremum is finite. Sum over the charts and take the maximum
over the finite word family, omitting empty \(M_w\)'s, to obtain
\eqref{eq:finitecharts}. Chart overlaps only enlarge the bound;
every local inverse branch meeting \(M_w\) is included.
\end{proof}

The constant $L_T$ may increase when the allowed area loss decreases.
For ordinary precision lower bounds this causes no difficulty:
$T$ is fixed throughout the horizon limit. The full-cover upper
bounds in Section~\ref{sec:rates} include the states removed
in selecting $T$.

\subsection{The area served by one approximate input}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{thm:area}]
Choose $T,L_T$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:regularwindow}, and fix an
arbitrary input $u$. Partition its successful states by the first
time they leave $Q$. Exits by time $J$ are controlled by the
finite inverse charts. States surviving to $J$ enter $Q_\rho$,
where the strip and exit-layer estimates apply for the next $m$
steps.
Let \(Q_\delta=\{x:\dist(x,Q)<\delta\}\).
For \(0<\delta\le1\), the tubes around the three edges of \(Q\) give
\[
\area(Q_\delta\setminus Q)\le C_Q\delta,\qquad
C_Q=2(2+2\sqrt2)+3\pi .
\]
\smallskip
\emph{Exits during the transient.}
If a state in \(T\cap A_u(n,\delta;K)\) first truly exits at
\(j\le J\), put \(w=u|j\). Its earlier states lie exactly in \(Q\),
and it belongs to
\[
T\cap D_{w^-}\cap F_w^{-1}(Q_\delta\setminus Q).
\]
By \eqref{eq:finitecharts}, its class has area at most
\(L_TC_Q\delta\). Summing over the \(J\) possible first-exit times
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transient}
\area\{x\in T\cap A_u(n,\delta;K):
             \text{first true exit occurs by }J\}\le C_T\delta,
\quad C_T=JL_TC_Q .
\end{equation}
All derivative inputs here are in \(Q\); no nonsingularity condition
is needed at the last output, which may be outside.

\smallskip
\emph{Local survival and the later first exits.}
The remaining states follow the exactly feasible transient
$\sigma=u|J$ into $Q_\rho$. There are two possibilities during
the next $m$ steps. Those that remain in $Q$ follow the prescribed
length-$m$ tail $v$. Its region has area at most $C_A\rho^2\lambda_v$.
If this class is nonempty, one of its states comes from $T$, so
the uniform word estimate gives
$\lambda_v\le D_\eta e^{-(h-\eta)m}$. Pulling the region back
through $F_\sigma$ multiplies this upper bound by at most $L_T$.
Thus the survival class has area at most
$C_{T,\eta}e^{-(h-\eta)m}$.

For a first exit at local time $j\le m$, only the preceding
word $w$ of length $j-1$ must be exactly feasible. If the exit
class is nonempty, that word is realised after a transient from
$T$. Applying the two bounds in \eqref{eq:typicalproducts} to
the ratio in \eqref{eq:exitarea} gives
\[
\frac{\lambda_w}{P_w}
\le D_\eta^2
  \frac{e^{-(h-\eta)(j-1)}}{e^{-(\chi_R+\eta)(j-1)}}
=D_\eta^2 e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)(j-1)}.
\]
After the same transient pullback, the area is at most
$C_{T,\eta}\delta e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)(j-1)}$.
These classes include successful states that later reenter $Q$.
Summing over first-exit times gives
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{m}e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)(j-1)}
\le \frac{e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)m}}
              {e^{\chi_R-h+2\eta}-1},
\]
where $\chi_R>h$. The denominator is absorbed into $C_{T,\eta}$.
Adding this bound to local survival and \eqref{eq:transient}
proves \eqref{eq:actualarea}, also for $m=0$, when the sum is empty.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Positive-area and fixed near-full initial sets}
Return to matched powers, $r_i=a_i^\beta$.
To complete Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, apply Proposition~\ref{thm:area}
to a fixed positive-area compact $K$ and its positive-area set $T$.
A catalogue covering $K$ has at least
$\area(T)/\sup_u\area(T\cap A_u(n,\delta_n;K))$ elements.
For fixed $\eta\in(0,h)$, put
\[
m_n=\min\left\{n-J,
 \left\lfloor\frac{-\log\delta_n}{\chi_R+\eta}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Eventually $m_n\ge0$, and
$\delta_n\le e^{-(\chi_R+\eta)m_n}$.
This choice balances survival and exit: explicitly,
\[
\delta_n e^{(\chi_R-h+2\eta)m_n}
\le e^{-(h-\eta)m_n},\qquad
\delta_n\le e^{-(h-\eta)m_n}.
\]
Thus each term in \eqref{eq:actualarea} has that upper bound,
up to a constant independent of $n$. Consequently
\[
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K)
 \ge (h-\eta)\min\{1,\gamma/(\chi_R+\eta)\}.
\]
Since $\chi_R=\beta h$ here, letting $\eta\downarrow0$
gives the lower bound in
\eqref{eq:universal}; for $\gamma=0$ the bound $r_F\ge1$
also suffices. The upper bound follows from
$r_F(n,\delta;K)\le r_F(n,\delta;Q)$ and
\eqref{eq:spectrum}. Corollary~\ref{cor:thresholds} gives equality when $\gamma\le\beta h$.

For $\zeta\in(0,1)$ take the single near-full set $T$ constructed
in Section~\ref{sec:area} and put $K_\zeta=T\cup\{0\}$.
All exactly feasible length-$n$ words meeting $T$ have,
after their $J$-digit transient, frequency uniformly tending to
$a_0$. If $d_N\to0$ bounds this deviation for tails of length
$N=n-J$, there are at most
\[
2^J(N+1)\exp\!\left(
N\max_{|p-a_0|\le d_N} H_b(p)\right)=e^{(h+o(1))n}
\]
such words. Every state of $T$ has an exactly feasible prefix,
so these words, extended by any infinite tail, cover $T$
through time $n$. One additional input covers the origin.
These words are exactly feasible, so their upper rate $h$ is
independent of the requested tolerance. Bound \eqref{eq:upper} also
gives upper rate $\gamma/\beta$. The lower bound already proved
gives \eqref{eq:high}. The construction of $T$ used all-prefix frequency convergence,
removal of null critical pullbacks, and compact inner approximation;
it preceded every exponent, precision sequence and horizon.
This proves the remaining assertions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Catalogues with a failure allowance}
\label{sec:reliability}
Throughout this section $r_i=a_i^\beta$ and neither map has a
critical point on $Q$. We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability}.
Full coverage supplies the upper bound $S(\gamma)$. The other
upper bound, $L_a(\alpha)$, requires discarding rare word families.
For this we need an area bound on all of $Q$: the fixed exceptional
area removed in Section~\ref{sec:area} would eventually exceed
$e_n$. Nonsingularity gives the bound without that removal.

The lower bound has a different requirement. The individual
witnesses of Section~\ref{sec:shadow} have zero area and could all
be discarded. We thicken them into disjoint regions with a known
mass. A family whose total mass exceeds $e_n$ must then be
represented in the catalogue.

\subsection{A probability bound without an exceptional set}
Let $J$ and $\rho$ be as in \eqref{eq:localentry}, so every
exactly feasible $J$-prefix ends in $Q_\rho$.
Recall $D_w$, the complete exactly feasible domain, and $w^-$,
the word with its last digit removed.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform area transport through feasible prefixes]\label{lem:probtransport}
There is a fixed $K_J<\infty$ such that for every $1\le|w|\le J$
and measurable $B_0\subset\R^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wholeinverse}
\mu(D_{w^-}\cap F_w^{-1}B_0)\le K_J\area(B_0).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every family $\mathcal V$ of words of a common
length $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wordprobability}
\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{\substack{|\sigma|=J\\v\in\mathcal V}}
D_{\sigma v}\right)\le C_0\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}\lambda_v,
\qquad C_0=\max\{1,2^JK_JC_A\rho^2\}.
\end{equation}
The constants are independent of $N$, the word family, tolerance,
failure budget, and horizon.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Compactness gives $d_0=\min_{i,x\in Q}|\det DF_i(x)|>0$.
On $D_{w^-}$ all inputs to the derivative factors of $F_w$
belong to $Q$, even when its last output leaves $Q$. Thus
$|\det DF_w|\ge d_0^{|w|}$ there. Cover this compact domain by
finitely many local inverse charts for $F_w$, with compact closures
inside regular charts. Change of variables bounds the pulled-back
area in each chart by its inverse-determinant supremum times
$\area(B_0)$. Sum over the charts and maximise over the finitely
many words of length at most $J$. All inverse branches meeting the
domain are included; their overlap only enlarges the upper bound.
No global inverse is used.

An exactly feasible $\sigma$ sends $D_\sigma$ into $Q_\rho$.
If $\sigma v$ is feasible, the state after $\sigma$ lies in the
complete initial region for the tail $v$. Precisely,
\[
D_{\sigma v}\subset
D_\sigma\cap F_\sigma^{-1}(\mathcal I_v\cup\{0\}).
\]
By \eqref{eq:striparea}, this target has area at most
$C_A\rho^2\lambda_v$. Its possible point at zero has null
pullback by \eqref{eq:wholeinverse}. Apply that bound and sum
over all at most $2^J$ transients and all $v$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:wordprobability}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two catalogue upper bounds}
We keep exactly feasible words and discard only families whose
total area is below $e_n$. This avoids a tolerance error of order
$\delta_n$ from early exits, which need not fit the failure budget.
Fix $\varepsilon>0$, $n>J$, and $N=n-J$.
Keep every exactly feasible length-$n$ prefix $\sigma v$, with
$|\sigma|=J$, whose tail zero frequency $p$ satisfies
$I_a(p)\le\alpha+\varepsilon$. Empty complete regions may be omitted.
Append any infinite input to each kept horizon prefix.
Every uncovered state has an exactly feasible length-$n$ prefix
by controlled invariance, and all its feasible prefixes have
discarded tails. Hence the uncovered set is contained in the union
of the discarded word regions, whether or not these regions overlap.
Lemma~\ref{lem:probtransport} bounds its probability by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliablefailure}
C_0\sum_{I_a(j/N)>\alpha+\varepsilon}
\binom Nj e^{-N\chi(j/N)}
\le C_0(N+1)e^{-N(\alpha+\varepsilon)}<e_n
\end{equation}
for large $n$. Here $\binom Nj\le e^{NH_b(j/N)}$ follows
from the $j$-th term of the binomial probability sum. The catalogue
has at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliablecount}
2^J(N+1)e^{N L_a(\alpha+\varepsilon)}
\end{equation}
members. The fixed transient and polynomial factors have zero rate.
Continuity of $L_a$, proved below, gives upper rate $L_a(\alpha)$.

Combining this catalogue with the full-cover upper bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliableupper}
\limsup_n n^{-1}\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)
                       \le\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The lower bound below the first precision threshold}
For this range, it is enough to retain any fixed positive mass of
successful states. The one-input area estimate already bounds the
number of controls needed to cover that mass.
Take one regular compact $T\subset Q$ of area greater than $3/4$
from Proposition~\ref{thm:area}. For fixed $0<\varepsilon<h$, put
\[
m_n=\min\left\{n-J,
\left\lfloor\frac{-\log\delta_n}{\beta h+\varepsilon}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Then $\delta_n\le e^{-(\beta h+\varepsilon)m_n}$ and, using
$\chi_R=\beta h$, \eqref{eq:actualarea} bounds the area covered
inside $T$ by one input by a fixed multiple of
$e^{-(h-\varepsilon)m_n}$.
A successful catalogue covers more than $1/2$ area inside $T$
eventually. The union bound and $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliablefixedmass}
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)
                        \ge\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
Since $S(\gamma)=\gamma/\beta$ for $\gamma\le\beta h$
and $L_a(\alpha)\ge h$, this matches \eqref{eq:reliableupper}
for $0\le\gamma\le\beta h$. At $\gamma=0$, the upper bound and
$r_{e_n}\ge1$ give the zero limit.

\subsection{Giving the forced prefixes positive area}
Following a word $w$ expands angular differences by a factor
comparable to $\lambda_w^{-1}$. We therefore start with angular
width proportional to $\lambda_w$. Up to time $|w|$, the perturbed
trajectory will remain within a fixed small angular distance of
the central orbit. The proof first keeps the whole proposed region
feasible, then checks that the other control still violates the
constraint by a definite amount.
\begin{lemma}[Regions with a forced input prefix]\label{lem:massforcing}
For every integer $L\ge2$ there are $\tau_L,E_L>0$ such that
any family $\mathcal W$ of distinct words of a common length $m$,
each admitting an infinite extension with runs at most $L$, has
pairwise disjoint measurable regions $E_w\subset Q$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forcedmass}
\mu(E_w)=\tau_L\lambda_w.
\end{equation}
Every point of $E_w$ is exactly feasible under $w$.
If $n\ge m$ and $2\delta<E_LP_w$, every input whose actual
successful set $A_u(n,\delta;Q)$ meets $E_w$ has prefix $w$.
All constants are fixed before $m,n,\delta$ and the word family.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each word choose a bounded-run extension $v(w)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow} gives a common radius bound $s_L>0$,
continuous centres $y_w(s)$ for $0<s\le s_L$, and wrong-control
excess $d_L'>0$. Its construction gives centre angles at distance
at least $\Delta_L=3d_L/4$ from both boundaries at every step,
where $d_L=2a_*^{L+1}$.
Put $K_0=1/a_*+3B\rho$ and choose
\[
0<\epsilon_L\le\min\{1/(2C_A),\Delta_L/(4C_A),
                                      d_L'/(4K_0C_A)\}.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forcedregion}
E_w=\{(s,sy):s_L/2\le s\le s_L,
                         |y-y_w(s)|\le\epsilon_L\lambda_w\}.
\end{equation}
\smallskip
\emph{The proposed region is feasible.}
Fix the initial radius. Initially we may use the derivative estimate
only on the feasible component containing the centre. On the
interval between the
centre and any feasible angle in that component, \eqref{eq:products} bounds the
change in the angle after each prefix $w|j$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thickangle}
|Y_{w|j}(y)-Y_{w|j}(y_w(s))|
 \le C_A\epsilon_L\lambda_w/\lambda_{w|j}
 \le D_L,\qquad D_L=C_A\epsilon_L.
\end{equation}
The centre satisfies all the finitely many constraints strictly,
so it lies in the interior of $I_w(s)$. If the proposed interval
were not contained in $I_w(s)$, follow it from the centre to the
first endpoint of $I_w(s)$. Estimate~\eqref{eq:thickangle} applies
up to that endpoint by continuity. Every intermediate angle
there is still at distance at least $\Delta_L-D_L>0$ from the
boundary. The radii remain positive and below $\rho<1$ as well.
Thus all constraints remain strict, and continuity extends the
feasible interval past the endpoint, a contradiction. The whole
proposed interval is therefore feasible.
This argument applies even when the complete terminal image is
truncated; it does not assume that image is onto.

\smallskip
\emph{The other control still fails.}
At each prefix the thickened state and its centre lie on one image
graph with logarithmic radial slope at most one. Hence their log
radius ratio has absolute value at most $D_L\le1/2$, and their
radius difference is at most $2\rho D_L$.
The alternative angular map changes by at most
\[
(1/a_*+B\rho)D_L+B(2\rho D_L)=K_0D_L\le d_L'/4.
\]
Every thickened point therefore retains a wrong-control excess
at least $d_L'/2$ at every prefix, as well as selected-control
feasibility. Continuity of the centres makes $E_w$ measurable.

\smallskip
\emph{Area and separation.}
Integrating with the physical Jacobian $s$ gives
\[
2\epsilon_L\lambda_w\int_{s_L/2}^{s_L}s\,ds
 =\frac{3\epsilon_Ls_L^2}{4}\lambda_w;
\qquad \tau_L=3\epsilon_Ls_L^2/4.
\]
Two distinct equal-length words cannot share a point in their
regions: at their first difference one selected control would
be wrong, contradicting feasibility under both words.

If an arbitrary input first differs at $j\le m$, the preceding
trajectory is exactly the selected one. Its radius is at least
$C_R^{-1}(s_L/2)P_{w|j-1}\ge C_R^{-1}(s_L/2)P_w$.
The wrong output has radial factor at least $r_*/2$ and angular
excess at least $d_L'/2$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:massmargin}
|z_j|-s_j\ge E_LP_w,\qquad
                         E_L=\frac{s_Lr_*d_L'}{8C_R}>0.
\end{equation}
Either $z-s$ or $-z-s$ has this positive excess. Each function
has Euclidean Lipschitz constant $\sqrt2$ and is nonpositive on
$Q$, so the distance is at least $E_LP_w/\sqrt2>\delta$.
This violates the constraint at time $j$ and proves the forcing
assertion for every point of $E_w$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Which rare word families must be covered?}
Suppose $c=\gamma/\beta>h$. Fix an interior frequency $p$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strictprobtype}
\chi(p)<c,\qquad I_a(p)<\alpha.
\end{equation}
The first inequality leaves a physical margin larger than
$\delta_n$ through almost $n$ steps. The second leaves a total
mass larger than $e_n$. Both are strict so that fixed prefactors
and the allowed $o(n)$ fluctuations will not affect either comparison.
Choose $j_L/L\to p$ and use the length-$L$ blocks starting with
zero, ending with one, and containing $j_L$ zeros. Write
\[
M_L=\binom{L-2}{j_L-1},\quad B_L=L^{-1}\log M_L,
\quad\chi_L=\chi(j_L/L),\quad I_L=\chi_L-B_L.
\]
The binomial bounds proved in Section~\ref{sec:rates} give
$B_L\to H_b(p)$, $\chi_L\to\chi(p)$ and $I_L\to I_a(p)$.
Fix $L$ large enough that $\beta\chi_L<\gamma$ and $I_L<\alpha$.
Put $k=\lfloor n/L\rfloor$ and $m=Lk$.
The $M_L^k$ concatenations, extended by a fixed allowed block,
have runs at most $L$. Lemma~\ref{lem:massforcing} gives disjoint
regions of equal mass and equal radial product,
\[
\mu(E_w)=\tau_Le^{-\chi_Lm},\qquad P_w=e^{-\beta\chi_Lm}.
\]
The physical margin exceeds $2\delta_n$ eventually because its
logarithmic ratio has rate $\gamma-\beta\chi_L>0$.
One input can then cover points in at most one region. Hence $R$
inputs cover at most $R\tau_Le^{-\chi_Lm}$ of the union, whose
total area is $M_L^k\tau_Le^{-\chi_Lm}$. At most $e_n$ of this
area may be missed, so every successful catalogue satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rarecount}
R\ge M_L^k-\frac{e_n}{\tau_Le^{-\chi_Lm}}
 =M_L^k\left(1-\frac{e_n}{\tau_Le^{-I_Lm}}\right).
\end{equation}
The parenthesis tends to one, since $\alpha>I_L$.
Take the horizon limit with $L$ fixed, then let $L\to\infty$.
This proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strictlower}
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)
 \ge\sup_{\chi(p)<c,\ I_a(p)<\alpha}H_b(p)\qquad(c>h).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Evaluating the probability constraint}
It remains to show that the supremum in \eqref{eq:strictlower}
reaches the smaller of the two upper bounds. Relabel by the smaller
branch: let $a=a_*<1/2$ and use $q$ for
the frequency of the smaller branch. Define the relabelled functions
\[
\widehat\chi(q)=c_b+dq,\qquad
\widehat I(q)=q\log(q/a)+(1-q)\log((1-q)/(1-a)),
\]
where $c_b=-\log(1-a)$ and $d=\log((1-a)/a)$, with the
endpoint convention $0\log0=0$. If $a_0=a$, then $q=p$;
otherwise $q=1-p$. Thus
$\widehat\chi(q)=\chi(p)$, $\widehat I(q)=I_a(p)$, and
$H_b(q)=H_b(p)$. This relabelling changes neither the dynamics
nor either variational maximum. Put $J_a=\widehat I(1/2)>0$.
Then $h=\widehat\chi(a)=H_b(a)$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:thresholds} gives the three pieces of $S$.
To evaluate $L_a$, frequencies $q<a$ have entropy at most $h$
and cannot improve on the feasible frequency $a$. If $q>1/2$,
reflection preserves entropy and lowers relative entropy:
\[
H_b(1-q)=H_b(q),\qquad
\widehat I(1-q)-\widehat I(q)=d(1-2q)<0.
\]
It therefore suffices to optimise over $[a,1/2]$. On this interval
$H_b$ increases, and $\widehat I$ increases strictly away from
its minimum at $a$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reliabilityclosed}
L_a(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
H_b(q_\alpha),&0<\alpha<J_a,\ \widehat I(q_\alpha)=\alpha,
                                      \ a<q_\alpha<1/2,\\
\log2,&\alpha\ge J_a.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The inverse of this strictly increasing continuous restriction of
$\widehat I$ is continuous; at $J_a$, $q_\alpha$ tends to $1/2$.
Thus $L_a$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, including its saturation
point.
For $c>h$ set $q_c=\min\{1/2,(c-c_b)/d\}$, and set
$q_\alpha=1/2$ if $\alpha\ge J_a$.
Every $a<q<\min\{q_c,q_\alpha\}$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:strictprobtype}. Approaching its endpoint in
\eqref{eq:strictlower} yields
\[
\liminf_n n^{-1}\log r_{e_n}(n,\delta_n;Q)
 \ge H_b(\min\{q_c,q_\alpha\})
 =\min\{S(\gamma),L_a(\alpha)\}.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:reliableupper} and
\eqref{eq:reliablefixedmass}, this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability}. At the common-cost endpoint $c=h$, the proof
uses the fixed-mass argument, while the saturation and reliability
endpoints use strict types approaching from below. Integer rounding
changes $m$ by less than fixed $L$; the transient changes $N$ by
fixed $J$. The strict gaps in \eqref{eq:reliablefailure} and
\eqref{eq:rarecount} absorb every $o(n)$ fluctuation in both
sequences. No constants are required to be uniform as $L\to\infty$,
or over different fixed systems.

\begin{corollary}[Linear and fixed-failure specialisations]\label{cor:failure}
The matched linear pair has rate \eqref{eq:jointcost} for every
stated pair of sequences. Under the weaker standing null-critical
hypotheses with matched powers, each fixed $e\in(0,1)$ instead satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixedfail}
\lim_n n^{-1}\log r_e(n,\delta_n;Q)=\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The linear pair is nonsingular on $Q$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:reliability} applies.
For fixed failure, choose the single compact set in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with area greater than $1-e$; its catalogue
gives the upper rate in \eqref{eq:fixedfail}.
For the lower rate choose a compact set $T$ of area greater
than $(1+e)/2$. A catalogue with failure at most $e$ covers at
least $\area(T)-e>0$ area there. The fixed-mass proof above,
which needs only Proposition~\ref{thm:area}, gives the lower rate.
\end{proof}

\section{An injective example with a different reliability cost}
\label{sec:counter}
We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:counter}. The construction changes
area while preserving the correspondence between full covers.
Choose a bijection $H:Q\to Q$ and set $F_i=\mathsf A_iH$.
We arrange that $H$ is the identity near the origin and that every
first step from $Q$ reaches this identity region. The trajectory
from $x$ then agrees, after time zero, with the linear trajectory
from $H(x)$. Full covers have exactly the same size.

To change reliability, arrange $2^k$ disjoint source regions,
each of mass proportional to $b^k$, whose images force different
$2k$-digit prefixes. A catalogue with $R$ inputs can cover at
most $R$ of those regions. If their total mass $(2b)^k$ decays
more slowly than $e_n$, almost all $2^k$ prefixes are required.

We realise this geometry with two Cantor constructions having
the same binary addresses. Source intervals shrink by $b=2/5$;
target intervals shrink by $\lambda=a_0a_1$. The slower source
decay gives the excess probability mass.

\subsection{Source and target intervals}
Fix the parameters of the theorem and put $\lambda=a_0a_1$.
The inverse reference maps are $f_0(y)=a_0y-a_1$ and
$f_1(y)=a_1y+a_0$. For the control blocks $01$ and $10$, write
\[
B_0=f_0\circ f_1=\lambda y+a_0^2-a_1,\qquad
B_1=f_1\circ f_0=\lambda y+a_0-a_1^2.
\]
Their invariant interval has endpoints and length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cantorendpoints}
A=\frac{a_0^2-a_1}{1-\lambda},\quad
B=\frac{a_0-a_1^2}{1-\lambda},\quad
\ell=B-A=\frac{2\lambda}{1-\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Indeed $A+1=2a_0^2/(1-\lambda)>0$ and
$1-B=2a_1^2/(1-\lambda)>0$, so $[A,B]\subset(-1,1)$.
The maps take this interval to its left and right portions of
relative length $\lambda<1/2$. The corresponding Cantor set
$\mathcal C$ consists of angles with infinite block controls $01,10$.

On $J_\varnothing=[-1/2,1/2]$ use the source maps
\[
b=2/5,\qquad h_0(y)=by-(1-b)/2,
\qquad h_1(y)=by+(1-b)/2.
\]
At level $k$, the source intervals retain total length $(2b)^k$,
the mass scale used in the reliability obstruction. Their Cantor
set $\mathcal D$ is null because this length tends to zero.
For $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^k$ put
\[
J_\sigma=h_{\sigma_1}\circ\cdots\circ h_{\sigma_k}(J_\varnothing),
\qquad Z_\sigma=B_{\sigma_1}\circ\cdots\circ B_{\sigma_k}([A,B]).
\]
Within each family these closed intervals are separated by positive
gaps. Their lengths are $b^k$ and $\ell\lambda^k$, respectively.
Both address maps preserve order and are unique, including endpoints.
The target Cantor set is null as $(2\lambda)^k\to0$.

\subsection{An angular map with prescribed compression}
We need a smooth map carrying each whole source interval onto
its target, not just the Cantor points. The following lemma supplies
it; the gap interpolation is proved in Appendix~\ref{app:compression}.
The inequality $\lambda<b^2$ is what permits two derivatives
to tend to zero at the Cantor set.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:flatmap}
For the source and target intervals just defined, there is a
strictly increasing $C^2$ homeomorphism $g:\R\to\R$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flatmapproperties}
\begin{gathered}
g(y)=y\quad(|y|\ge1),\qquad g([-1,1])=[-1,1],\\
g(J_\sigma)=Z_\sigma,\qquad
g'(y)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ y\in\mathcal D.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Moreover $g''=0$ on $\mathcal D$ and $|g(y)-y|\le2$ on $\R$.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Planar controls and equality of full-cover counts}
Insert $g$ only above radius $1/4$, leaving the linear parts
unchanged near zero. Let $E(t)=e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$ and zero
otherwise, and set
\[
\psi(s)=\frac{E(s-1/4)}{E(s-1/4)+E(1/2-s)}.
\]
Define
\[
H(s,z)=
\begin{cases}
(s,z),&s\le1/4,\\
(s,z+s\psi(s)[g(z/s)-z/s]),&s>1/4,
\end{cases}
\]
and set $F_i=\mathsf A_i\circ H$.
The smooth function $\psi$ is zero on $s\le1/4$ and one on
$s\ge1/2$. The quotient is used only away from zero; its needed
derivatives are locally bounded at every joining point, and flatness
of $\psi$ gives global $C^2$ maps. Near the origin $H$ is identity,
so the required first derivatives agree exactly.

\smallskip
\emph{Feasibility and contraction.}
On every fibre the angular map
$g_s(y)=(1-\psi(s))y+\psi(s)g(y)$ is an increasing homeomorphism
of $[-1,1]$ onto itself. Therefore $H(Q)=Q$, and one of the
two linear controls is feasible at $H(x)$ for each $x\in Q$.
Its radial factor is less than one. This proves controlled invariance
including every boundary and the origin.

Put $m_* =\max_i\{a_i^\beta,a_i^{\beta-1}\}<1$,
$\Lambda=1+10m_* /(1-m_*)$, and $V=\max\{\Lambda|s|,|z|\}$.
The bound $|H^z-z|\le2|s|$ gives, on the whole plane,
\[
V(Hx)\le(1+2/\Lambda)V(x),\qquad
V(\mathsf A_i x)\le m_*(1+1/\Lambda)V(x).
\]
Consequently the common contraction factor can be taken as
\[
\theta=m_*(1+1/\Lambda)(1+2/\Lambda)
 \le m_*[1+5/\Lambda]<(1+m_*)/2<1.
\]
This applies to arbitrary input tails outside $Q$ as well.

\smallskip
\emph{Critical points and injectivity.}
For $s>1/4$ the determinant is
\[
\det DH=1-\psi(s)+\psi(s)g'(z/s),\qquad
\det DF_i=a_i^{2\beta-1}\det DH;
\]
for $s\le1/4$ the first determinant equals one.
Thus the critical set in $Q$ is exactly
$\{(s,sy):1/2\le s\le1,\ y\in\mathcal D\}$.
It has area zero by Fubini in physical coordinates. Local inverse
charts off that null set show that every null target set has a null
pullback in $Q$. No positive-area set is collapsed to a null set.
Each fibre map is strictly increasing and identity outside $[-1,1]$,
so $H$ is globally bijective. It is proper, since its first coordinate
is unchanged and $|H^z-z|\le2|s|$. Thus it and both $F_i$ are
global homeomorphisms, although their Jacobian determinants vanish
on the displayed null set.

\smallskip
\emph{Why only the first step changes.}
The fixed radial factors satisfy
\[
a_1^\beta=(1-2^{-10})^{4096}<e^{-4}<1/4,
\qquad a_0^\beta<1/4.
\]
After the first input from $Q$, the radius lies below $1/4$,
regardless of whether its angle is feasible. Every subsequent input
therefore uses the identity part of $H$, even along an approximate
trajectory that has left $Q$. For every input and every $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onetransient}
F_u^j x=\mathsf A_u^j Hx\qquad(x\in Q).
\end{equation}
At time zero both $x$ and $Hx$ belong to $Q$. It follows that
$A_u^F(n,\delta;Q)=H^{-1}A_u^{\mathsf A}(n,\delta;Q)$ on $Q$.
Since $H(Q)=Q$, full covers correspond, proving
\eqref{eq:counterfull}. For probability covers the same correspondence transports $\mu$
to $H_*\mu$. This measure is absolutely continuous, but it is not
uniform area: the interval compression deliberately changes its
weights. We next estimate the cost when both systems are instead
measured using uniform initial area.

\subsection{The mass that a reliable catalogue cannot discard}
We first check that each entire target interval forces its block
prefix, then compute the area of its source preimage. Every infinite
sequence of blocks $01,10$ has runs at most two.
The reference endpoint-distance identities used in
\eqref{eq:refmargin} give $|y_j|\le1-d$ at all shifted positions,
with $d=2a_*^3$. For a chosen zero, the current angle is at most
$a_0-a_1-a_0d$, so the alternative one gives at most
$-1-a_0d/a_1$; the symmetric bound holds for a chosen one.
Thus every wrong-control angular excess is at least
$d_*=(a_*/a^*)d>0$.
This holds on each complete interval $Z_\sigma$ through its
$2k$-digit block prefix: its endpoints have such bounded-run
extensions, and the linear prefix maps are monotone.

For each $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^k$ define an actual physical region
\[
E_{\sigma,k}=\{(s,sy):1/2\le s\le1,\ y\in J_\sigma\}.
\]
Here $\psi=1$ and $g(J_\sigma)=Z_\sigma$.
The regions are disjoint and each has mass
$(3/8)b^k$, so their union has mass $(3/8)(2b)^k$.
Every point requires its selected $2k$-digit control prefix at
sufficiently small tolerance. To check this for an arbitrary input,
let its first differing digit be $j\le2k$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:onetransient} and $s\ge1/2$ give the
preceding physical radius at least
$\tfrac12\lambda^{\beta k}$: a partial block product before
$2k$ is no smaller than the complete product $\lambda^k$.
The wrong output radial factor is at least $a_*^\beta$ and
its angular excess is at least $d_*$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cantorwrong}
|z_j|-s_j\ge c_0\lambda^{\beta k},
\qquad c_0=\tfrac12a_*^\beta d_*>0.
\end{equation}
Its distance from $Q$ is at least this value divided by $\sqrt2$.
For $k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and
$\gamma=-\beta\log\lambda$, the logarithmic ratio to $\delta_n$
has positive rate $-\tfrac12\beta\log\lambda$.
Every successful input from the region must therefore have the
selected prefix.

One catalogue input can consequently cover points of at most one
region. A catalogue of $R$ inputs with failure at most $e_n$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cantorcount}
R\ge2^k\left(1-\frac{8e_n}{3(2b)^k}\right).
\end{equation}
Since $-\tfrac12\log(2b)=\tfrac12\log(5/4)<1/8=\alpha$
by $\log(1+x)<x$, the parenthesis tends to one.
Thus the lower limit is at least $\tfrac12\log2$.
Both strict exponent gaps absorb all the allowed $o(n)$ fluctuations.

Finally $S(\gamma)=\log2$: at $p=1/2$ the threshold ratio
$\gamma/[\beta\chi(p)]$ is two. For $q=1/16$,
\[
H_b(q)=\tfrac14\log2+\tfrac{15}{16}\log(16/15)
 <\tfrac14\log2+\tfrac1{16}<\tfrac12\log2,
\]
and
\[
I_a(q)>\tfrac58\log2-H_b(q)
 >\tfrac38\log2-\tfrac1{16}>\tfrac18,
\]
using $\log2>1/2$. Relative entropy is strictly increasing for
$q>a_0$; its sublevel $I_a(q)\le1/8$ therefore has right endpoint
below $1/16$. Binary entropy is increasing up to $1/2$, so
$L_a(1/8)<H_b(1/16)<\tfrac12\log2$.
This proves the strict inequality \eqref{eq:counterrate} and completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:counter}.

\subsection{An exponential lower bound for inverse area}
Lemma~\ref{lem:regularwindow} removes a fixed area before bounding
inverse images. Could we instead retain mass $1-e_n$ and allow
the inverse-area constant to depend subexponentially on $n$?
For this example the answer is negative. Test the bound on the
first-control images of the forced regions: almost all their
source mass must be retained, whereas their images are much smaller.
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:inversecost}
For the fixed system of Theorem~\ref{thm:counter}, let
$e_n=\exp(-n/8+o(n))$, with $0<e_n<1$, and
$k_n=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For every sequence of measurable
sets $T_n\subset Q$ with $\mu(T_n)\ge1-e_n$, any finite
constants $L_n$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:windowinverse}
\mu(T_n\cap F_0^{-1}B)\le L_n\area(B)
       \quad\text{for every measurable }B\subset\R^2
\end{equation}
must, for all sufficiently large $n$, obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inversegrowth}
L_n\ge\frac{1}{2\ell\det\mathsf A_0}
              (b/\lambda)^{k_n},\qquad
\liminf_n\frac{\log L_n}{n}
              \ge\tfrac12\log(b/\lambda)>0.
\end{equation}
Here $b=2/5$, $\lambda=a_0a_1$, and
$\ell=2\lambda/(1-\lambda)$ are unchanged.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $k\ge1$, restrict the regions $E_{\sigma,k}$ above to
addresses with $\sigma_1=0$, and denote their union by $E_k^0$.
There are $2^{k-1}$ disjoint intervals. Since $H$ preserves $s$
and maps each full $J_\sigma$ onto $Z_\sigma$, its image
$B_k^0=H(E_k^0)$ has angular intervals of length $\ell\lambda^k$.
The physical Jacobian $s$ gives the exact areas
\[
\mu(E_k^0)=2^{k-1}b^k\int_{1/2}^1s\,ds
          =\tfrac3{16}(2b)^k,
\qquad
\area(B_k^0)=\tfrac3{16}\ell(2\lambda)^k.
\]
Every target angle in these intervals follows a block word starting
with $01$ and is exactly feasible under its first zero control.
Hence $F_0(E_k^0)=\mathsf A_0B_k^0\subset Q$; the test uses
an actual feasible branch. At $k=k_n$,
\[
\frac1n\log\frac{e_n}{\mu(E_{k_n}^0)}
 \longrightarrow-\tfrac18+\tfrac12\log(5/4)<0.
\]
Thus every allowed set retains at least half of $E_{k_n}^0$
eventually. Taking the compact measurable set
$B=\mathsf A_0B_{k_n}^0$ in \eqref{eq:windowinverse}, and using
$\det\mathsf A_0=a_0^{2\beta-1}>0$, gives
\[
\tfrac12\mu(E_{k_n}^0)
 \le\mu(T_n\cap F_0^{-1}B)
 \le L_n\det\mathsf A_0\,\area(B_{k_n}^0).
\]
Division yields the first bound in \eqref{eq:inversegrowth}.
Since $k_n/n\to1/2$ and $b>\lambda$, the second follows.
The argument includes all the permitted $o(n)$ errors and odd horizons.
\end{proof}

If no finite $L_n$ exists, a subexponential inverse-area bound is
already impossible. Thus, for this example and this failure exponent,
the fixed-loss estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:regularwindow} cannot
be upgraded by choosing sets with loss $e_n$ and subexponential
constants.

\section{A nonlinear example with overlapping feasible regions}\label{sec:examples}
Both rate formulas apply to the following pair of diffeomorphisms,
although its feasible angular intervals behave differently from
the linear partition. This checks the part of the proof where
truncated images and empty slices must be allowed.
Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses} gives three further assumption tests:
angle-dependent radial motion, a feasible fold, and a collapse of
positive area. They separate the derivative assumptions without
changing the main counting problem.

\label{sec:inward}
In this section the powers match: $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta>1$,
so $r_i=a_i^\beta$ and $t_i=a_i^{\beta-1}$.
The branches will overlap, but a sufficiently long constant word
will serve no point on a given positive-radius fibre. We also
check that a homeomorphism preserving $Q$ cannot simultaneously
conjugate this pair to the linear one.

Let \(t=\max a_i^{\beta-1}<1\),
\(\Lambda=1+4t/(1-t)\), \(v=t(1+1/\Lambda)<1\), and fix
\[
       0<c<\min\{1-a^*,\ (1-v)/v,\ 1\},\qquad
       g(s)=1-\frac{cs}{1+s^2}.
\]
Consider the family
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 F_0(s,z)&=(r_0s,\ g(s)t_0(z+a_1s)),\\
 F_1(s,z)&=(r_1s,\ g(s)t_1(z-a_0s)).                         \label{eq:inward}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Both are global smooth diffeomorphisms: \(g\in[1-c/2,1+c/2]\)
is positive, the first coordinate has the explicit inverse
\(s=s'/r_i\), and the second is an affine invertible function of
\(z\) at that radius. Their derivatives at the origin are \eqref{eq:jets}.
For the stated \(V\),
\begin{equation}
V(F_i x)\le\max\{r^*,(1+c/2)v\}\,V(x),
 \qquad \max\{r^*,(1+c/2)v\}<1.                            \label{eq:inwardnorm}
\end{equation}
This verifies the required common norm inequality on the whole
plane.

For \(0<s\le1\), \(a^*<g(s)<1\). The complete one-step angular
domains are
\begin{equation}
D_0(s)=[-1,a_0/g(s)-a_1],\qquad
 D_1(s)=[a_0-a_1/g(s),1].                                   \label{eq:inwarddomains}
\end{equation}
The two interior cut points lie strictly inside \((-1,1)\), and the overlap
has angular width \(1/g(s)-1>0\), physical width
\(s(1/g(s)-1)=O(cs^2)\) near zero. Together with nonsingularity
and \eqref{eq:inwardnorm}, this verifies the assumptions of
Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and~\ref{thm:reliability}.

Yet for every fixed \(0<s_0\le1\), a sufficiently long all-zero word
is infeasible for every initial angle. To verify this, write
\(s_k=r_0^ks_0\) and \(q_k=y_k+1\). Under zero input,
\begin{equation}
q_{k+1}=\frac{g(s_k)}{a_0}q_k+1-g(s_k).                    \label{eq:inwardrecursion}
\end{equation}
The multiplier is uniformly greater than one, since
\(g(s)>a^*\), and \(q_1\ge1-g(s_0)>0\) for \(y_0\ge-1\).
The sequence eventually exceeds two, violating \(y\le1\).
Monotonicity means that this also holds for the smallest initial
angle, hence for all of them. The complete fixed-radius section
is therefore empty for sufficiently long all-zero words.

This statement concerns \(I_{0^m}(s_0)\) at a fixed radius, not the
whole physical region \(\mathcal I_{0^m}\). Choosing the radius smaller
can make a given finite word feasible. Indeed any finite word admits
a bounded-run infinite extension, and Lemma~\ref{lem:shadow} realises that
extension at a sufficiently small positive radius. Thus every finite
word is realised somewhere in $Q$, despite the empty sections at
larger radii.

There is also a constraint-preserving conjugacy obstruction.
For \eqref{eq:inward}, \(F_0\) sends the positive lower edge of $Q$ strictly
inside $Q$, and its upper edge outside; \(F_1\) does the symmetric
thing. The image of the top edge has constant radius \(r_i<1\)
and crosses the opposite angular boundary exactly once. Thus
\begin{equation}
F_i(\partial Q)\cap\partial Q
                  \text{ consists of exactly two points}.  \label{eq:inwardboundary}
\end{equation}
In contrast, for any target pair whose zero control sends the whole
lower edge of $Q$ into $\partial Q$ and whose one control sends the
whole upper edge into $\partial Q$, both corresponding intersections
contain a continuum. A homeomorphism \(H\) of the plane with
\(H(Q)=Q\), simultaneously conjugating the two labelled controls
(even allowing a label permutation), would map each intersection
in \eqref{eq:inwardboundary} onto the corresponding target intersection,
which is impossible because a homeomorphism preserves finiteness.
Thus this example is not reducible to
such a boundary-edge-preserving pair by a fixed $Q$-preserving simultaneous
control homeomorphism, and in particular not by such a \(C^1\)
or bi-Lipschitz map. This obstruction concerns the stated example
and conjugacies that preserve the constraint.

For example, take $a_0=1/3$, $a_1=2/3$, $\beta=2$ and $c=1/100$.
The choice $\Lambda=9$ in \eqref{eq:inwardnorm} gives a global
factor at most $67/90<1$. Corollary~\ref{cor:thresholds} evaluates
this same inward family at exponent $\gamma=\log4$:
\[
2\left(\log3-\tfrac23\log2\right)<\log4<\log(9/2),\qquad
\lim_n n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)
 =H_b\!\left(\frac{\log(4/3)}{\log2}\right).
\]
This holds for every precision sequence of that exponent.
The first inequality follows from $(4/3)^3>2$ and the second
from $4<9/2$. The spectrum therefore remains valid despite the
empty fixed-radius sections.


\section{What the area comparison leaves unresolved}
The difference between full coverage and reliability is already
visible during finitely many initial steps. Such steps multiply
the number of words by a bounded factor. Their inverse-area bound,
however, need not stay bounded when the discarded mass must tend
to zero. Corollary~\ref{cor:inversecost} makes this failure
quantitative: every set retaining mass $1-e_n$ in the counterexample
requires an inverse-area constant with a positive exponential rate.

The results do not determine the reliability rate of every system
with null critical sets. In particular, the exact rate and its
existence as a limit remain unknown for the Cantor example, and
the folded example in Appendix~\ref{app:hypotheses} is covered
only by the precision theorem. Two precision questions also remain
outside the formulas proved here: arbitrary positive-area initial
sets above $\beta h$, and the general high-precision regime for
unequal radial powers.

\section{A finite-horizon probability comparison for linear controls}
\label{app:linearfinite}
The linear model has an exact interval partition, so its catalogue
can be compared with a finite weighted selection problem before
passing to exponential rates. Select enough stopping intervals to
carry the required probability, and count them. This immediately
gives an upper catalogue bound. For the lower bound we show that
one arbitrary approximate input can meet only a bounded number
of neighbouring intervals. Throughout this appendix the maps are exactly
$\mathsf A_i$ in \eqref{eq:jets}, with $r_i=a_i^\beta$.
For its inverse angle maps $f_0(y)=a_0y-a_1$ and
$f_1(y)=a_1y+a_0$, define for a word $w=i_1\cdots i_\ell$
\[
I_w=f_{i_1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_\ell}([-1,1])
      =[d_w-\lambda_w,d_w+\lambda_w].
\]
These are its complete feasible initial angle intervals. Each inverse
branch maps $[-1,1]$ into itself, so membership checks every
intermediate angle, and all intermediate radii are at most one.
For every real initial angle, including infeasible ones, the
algebraic trajectory at time $\ell$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearleaftrajectory}
s_\ell=s\lambda_w^\beta,\qquad
y_\ell=(y-d_w)/\lambda_w.
\end{equation}

For $n\ge1$ and $0<t<1$, let $\mathcal L(n,t)$ consist of
the prefixes stopped at the first $\lambda_w\le t$ or at length
$n$, whichever occurs first. No leaf is a proper prefix of another. The tree has
closed leaf intervals with disjoint interiors partitioning $[-1,1]$.
Their weights obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearleafweights}
\sum_{w\in\mathcal L(n,t)}\lambda_w=1,
\qquad |I_w|=2\lambda_w,\qquad \lambda_w\ge a_*t.
\end{equation}
The last bound follows from the parent weight being above $t$
at a threshold leaf; an unfinished horizon leaf has weight above
$t$ itself. Shared endpoints and the apex are retained.
For $0<e<1$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedcover}
C(n,t;e)=\min\left\{\#\mathcal J:
 \mathcal J\subset\mathcal L(n,t),\quad
 \sum_{w\in\mathcal J}\lambda_w\ge1-e\right\}.
\end{equation}
The weights are actual interval masses in the linear model.
Indeed, uniform physical probability factors as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearfactorprob}
d\mu=s\,ds\,dy=(2s\,ds)(dy/2).
\end{equation}
The entire physical region over a leaf therefore has mass $\lambda_w$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:linearfinite}
For each fixed matched linear system there are constants $M>1$
and an integer $B\ge1$ such that for every $n\ge1$,
$0<\delta<1$, and $0<e<3/4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearfinite}
\frac1B C(n,\delta^{1/\beta};4e/3)
\le r_e^{\mathsf A}(n,\delta;Q)
\le C\bigl(n,(\delta/M)^{1/\beta};e\bigr).
\end{equation}
The constants do not depend on the horizon or either budget.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the lower bound, we show that one arbitrary input can serve
angles in only a bounded number of leaves. Work on the slab
$1/2\le s\le1$, whose probability is $3/4$, and put
$t=\delta^{1/\beta}$. For any infinite input $u$, let $v$
be its stopped prefix in
$\mathcal L(n,t)$. No feasibility is imposed on this input.
The functions $z-s$ and $-z-s$ are nonpositive on $Q$ and
Euclidean $\sqrt2$-Lipschitz. At every successful state the
constraint at the actual time $|v|\le n$ therefore implies
$(|z_{|v|}|-s_{|v|})_+<\sqrt2\delta$. By
\eqref{eq:linearleaftrajectory}, its initial angle satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leafextension}
\dist(y,I_v)<2\sqrt2\delta\lambda_v^{1-\beta}
 \le C_0t,\qquad C_0=2\sqrt2 a_*^{1-\beta}.
\end{equation}
Here $\beta>1$ and \eqref{eq:linearleafweights} give the last
inequality. It remains valid when the input had earlier left $Q$
and reentered; it is a necessary condition at a constrained time.

Expand $I_v$ by $C_0t$ at both ends. Every other leaf interior
has length at least $2a_*t$ and lies outside the interior of $I_v$.
On either side at most $\lceil C_0/(2a_*)\rceil+2$ leaves
can meet the extension: all except a possible first and last one
have their interiors entirely in a segment of length $C_0t$.
This also counts closed endpoint contacts. Including $v$, the
expanded interval meets at most
\[
B=5+2\lceil C_0/(2a_*)\rceil
\]
leaves. This bound uses the extension width $C_0t$, even when
an unfinished horizon leaf itself is much longer than $t$.
For $R$ catalogue inputs let $U\subset[-1,1]$ be the union
of all leaf intervals so encountered. It uses at most $BR$ leaves
and contains every successful angle in the slab. Every angle
outside $U$ fails on the entire slab, so \eqref{eq:linearfactorprob}
gives $(3/4)\mu_y(U^c)\le e$, where $d\mu_y=dy/2$.
Those leaves have weight at least $1-4e/3$, whence
$C(n,t;4e/3)\le BR$. Minimising over actual catalogues proves
the lower comparison for arbitrary approximate inputs.

For the upper bound use the common linear contraction norm from
Section~\ref{sec:planar} and set $M=4\sqrt2\Lambda$.
Choose leaves realising \eqref{eq:weightedcover} at
$t'=(\delta/M)^{1/\beta}$. Each state over such a leaf
has its exact feasible prefix. If the leaf hits the threshold,
its terminal norm satisfies
\[
V(\mathsf A_w(s,sy))=\Lambda s\lambda_w^\beta
                    \le\Lambda\delta/M=\delta/(4\sqrt2).
\]
Append the same infinite zero tail. Global toward-origin contraction
keeps its Euclidean norm at most $\delta/4$ at every remaining
time; since $0\in Q$, every remaining constraint holds strictly.
A leaf stopped only at the horizon is exactly feasible through
time $n$ and can have any continuation. The selected physical
regions have total probability at least $1-e$ by
\eqref{eq:linearfactorprob}. Endpoints and the apex can use any
of their feasible prefixes. One input per selected leaf proves
the upper comparison.
\end{proof}

For nonlinear controls the exact interval partition is lost.
Section~\ref{sec:reliability} replaces it with a uniform upper
bound on word-region probabilities and a lower bound from regions
with forced prefixes. Those estimates establish exponential rates,
rather than the finite-horizon comparison above.


\section{Mismatch and the common-cost criterion}\label{sec:mismatch}

The main probability results use matched radial powers. This appendix asks
whether matching is necessary for a common low-precision rate
across all positive-area compact initial sets. When the powers differ, the angular interval size $\lambda_w$
no longer fixes the radial scale $P_w$. The earlier geometric
estimates kept both products, so they still apply. We now count
leaves using their radial weights.
For this purpose define
\[
\gamma_0=-\log\max_i r_i>0,\qquad r_0^D+r_1^D=1.
\]
The root $D$ is unique in $(0,1)$, since the left side decreases
from two to zero and $r_0+r_1<1$. Also $\chi_R\ge\gamma_0$.

\begin{theorem}[First-order matching criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
For every fixed system satisfying the standing assumptions, the
following property holds if and only if $\beta_0=\beta_1$:
there are $\gamma_1>0$ and a function $c:(0,\gamma_1)\to[0,\infty)$
such that, for every fixed positive-area compact $K\subset Q$,
every $\gamma\in(0,\gamma_1)$, and every precision sequence of
exponent $\gamma$,
\[
\lim_n n^{-1}\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K)=c(\gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}



\begin{theorem}[Low-precision mismatch]\label{thm:mismatch}
Suppose $\beta_0\ne\beta_1$. For each $\zeta\in(0,1)$ there is
one compact $K_\zeta\subset Q$, of area greater than $1-\zeta$,
such that simultaneously for every $0<\gamma<\gamma_0$ and
every precision sequence of that exponent,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatchrates}
\lim_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)}n=\gamma D,\qquad
\lim_n\frac{\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)}n
                =\gamma h/\chi_R<\gamma D.
\end{equation}
The set is selected before the exponent, sequence, and horizon.
Every other fixed positive-area compact $K\subset Q$ has lower
rate at least $\gamma h/\chi_R$ in this range.
\end{theorem}


\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch}]
\emph{Full coverage.}
For $0<\gamma<\gamma_0$, even the slowest radial branch reaches
the precision scale before the horizon. Thus the depth cap does
not affect the stopping count. More precisely, for every
$0<\gamma<\gamma_0$,
all branches of the tree in Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} cross
$P_w\le\delta_n/M$ by depth $n-J$ for large $n$, since
\[
P_w\le e^{-\gamma_0(n-J)}<\delta_n/M
       \quad\text{at that depth}.
\]
The ratio has logarithmic rate $\gamma-\gamma_0<0$, so the
statement includes every $o(n)$ tolerance fluctuation.
For the uncapped radial tree, write $N(t)$ for its leaf count.
Replacing a parent by its children preserves $\sum P_w^D=1$,
and each leaf satisfies $r_*t<P_w\le t$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatchtree}
t^{-D}\le N(t)\le(r_*t)^{-D},\qquad
r_F(n,\delta_n;Q)\le2^JN(\delta_n/M).
\end{equation}
This gives the upper rate $\gamma D$ with a catalogue satisfying
every intermediate constraint. Optimality follows from the lower
bound below.

For the lower rate put $p_*=r_0^D$, so $1-p_*=r_1^D$, and
$c_R(p)=-p\log r_0-(1-p)\log r_1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rootentropy}
H_b(p_*)=D c_R(p_*).
\end{equation}
Choose $j_L/L\to p_*$ and use the blocks in
Lemma~\ref{lem:prefixpacking}, with
$c_L=c_R(j_L/L)$ and $B_L=L^{-1}\log\binom{L-2}{j_L-1}$.
The binomial calculation in Section~\ref{sec:rates} gives
$B_L\to H_b(p_*)$ and $c_L\to c_R(p_*)$.
For each fixed $L$, $c_L\ge\gamma_0>\gamma$.
Every strict $0<t<\gamma/c_L<1$ therefore gives lower rate $tB_L$.
Take its supremum first, then let $L\to\infty$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:rootentropy} gives the lower rate $\gamma D$.
The positive-radius witnesses and wrong-control margins are those
of the common physical lemma, not midpoints of assumed full images.
This proves the first limit in \eqref{eq:mismatchrates}.

\smallskip
\emph{A fixed set of positive area.}
For any fixed positive-area compact $K$, take the set $T$ in
Proposition~\ref{thm:area}. Fix $0<\eta<h$ and set
\[
m_n=\min\left\{n-J,
       \left\lfloor\frac{-\log\delta_n}{\chi_R+\eta}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
For large $n$ it is nonnegative, and
$\delta_n\le e^{-(\chi_R+\eta)m_n}$.
Every term of \eqref{eq:actualarea} is then bounded by a constant
times $e^{-(h-\eta)m_n}$. Covering the positive area of $T$ implies
lower rate $\gamma(h-\eta)/(\chi_R+\eta)$, since
$\gamma<\gamma_0\le\chi_R$. Let $\eta\downarrow0$.
This proves the asserted universal lower bound $\gamma h/\chi_R$.

For $K=Q$, choose the single set $T$ of area greater than $1-\zeta$
in the area proof, and put $K_\zeta=T\cup\{0\}$.
Keep only stopped pairs in Lemma~\ref{lem:stopping} realised on $T$.
They cover it with the complete tails already constructed.
Let $q_\delta=-\log(\delta/M)$ and $c_{\max}=-\log r_*$.
Each retained tail of length $\ell$ has
\[
q_\delta\le-\log P_w<q_\delta+c_{\max},\qquad
\ell\le\frac{q_\delta+c_{\max}+\log D_{\eta}}{\chi_R-\eta},
\]
by \eqref{eq:typicalproducts}. At this length,
$\lambda_w\ge D_{\eta}^{-1}e^{-(h+\eta)\ell}$, while all reference
length-$\ell$ weights sum to one. There are at most
$D_{\eta}e^{(h+\eta)\ell}$ such words. Sum over these lengths and the
at most $2^J$ transients, and include one input for the origin.
For fixed $\eta\in(0,h)$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:typicalstop}
\log r_F(n,\delta_n;K_\zeta)
\le\frac{h+\eta}{\chi_R-\eta}
[q_{\delta_n}+c_{\max}+\log D_{\eta}]
+O_{\eta}(\log(1+q_{\delta_n}))+O_{\eta}(1).
\end{equation}
Because $q_{\delta_n}=\gamma n+o(n)$, letting $\eta\downarrow0$
gives upper rate $\gamma h/\chi_R$, matching the lower bound.
The set was selected through uniform frequency convergence before
$\eta$, the precision exponent, the sequence, and the horizon.

\smallskip
\emph{Why the two rates differ.}
Let $b_i=r_i^D$. The weights $b_0,b_1$ sum to one, and the
relative-entropy identity gives
\[
D\chi_R-h=\sum_i a_i\log(a_i/b_i)\ge0.
\]
Concavity of the logarithm proves the inequality, with equality
only when $b_i=a_i$ for both labels. Equality would require
$\beta_0D=\beta_1D=1$, contrary to mismatch.
Thus $D>h/\chi_R$, completing the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}]
If $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
the common rate $c(\gamma)=\gamma/\beta$ on $(0,\beta h)$.
If $\beta_0\ne\beta_1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:mismatch} separates
$Q$ and one fixed nearly full-area compact set for every
$0<\gamma<\gamma_0$. Any interval adjacent to zero contains
such an exponent, contradicting the common-rate property.
\end{proof}

The necessity of matching is established within the first-derivative
family \eqref{eq:jets} under the standing assumptions. The argument
only uses exponents adjacent to zero; it does not determine the
high-precision mismatch spectrum.

\section{Further examples testing the hypotheses}\label{app:hypotheses}
The first example satisfies nonsingularity despite angular
dependence of the radius. The second retains null critical sets
but introduces a fold. The third drops even the nullity condition
and shows how positive-area initial sets can have zero cost.

\subsection{Radial motion depending on angle}\label{sec:radialfeedback}
The graph estimates did not require the radius to evolve independently
of the angle. The following nonsingular example makes this dependence
explicit while preserving all the standing assumptions.
For any parameters \eqref{eq:parameters}, choose a nonnegative
smooth cutoff $\kappa$ equal to one on $\overline B(0,3/2)$ and
supported inside $B(0,7/4)$. An explicit choice, with the flat
function $E$ above, is
\[
\kappa(x)=\frac{E(169/64-|x|^2)}
 {E(169/64-|x|^2)+E(|x|^2-9/4)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, and $\kappa(x)=0$ for $|x|\ge13/8$.
Put $\phi(s,z)=z^2\kappa(s,z)$,
$M_\phi=\max\{1,\|x\cdot\nabla\phi\|_\infty\}$, choose
$0<c<1/(2M_\phi)$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radialfeedback}
H(x)=e^{-c\phi(x)}x,\qquad F_i(x)=\mathsf A_iH(x).
\end{equation}
These are global smooth diffeomorphisms. Indeed, on each ray
$x=te$, $t\ge0$, the image radius has derivative
$e^{-c\phi(te)}[1-cte\cdot\nabla\phi(te)]>0$ and tends to
infinity, since $H$ is identity outside a compact set. It maps
each ray bijectively onto itself. Moreover
\[
\det DH(x)=e^{-2c\phi(x)}[1-cx\cdot\nabla\phi(x)]>0.
\]
The inverse function theorem gives a smooth inverse, including
at zero, and $DH(0)=I$ because $\phi(x)=O(|x|^2)$ there.
Thus the first derivatives are \eqref{eq:jets} and there are no
critical points. Since $\phi\ge0$, $V(Hx)\le V(x)$ globally;
the linear norm estimate in Section~\ref{sec:planar} verifies
\eqref{eq:contract}. The scalar factor preserves angle and decreases
radius. The linear feasible angular domains therefore still cover
each fibre of $Q$, proving controlled invariance at all its states.

The triangle lies in $B(0,3/2)$, so on $Q$ the first coordinate
is $r_i s e^{-cz^2}$. At a fixed positive $s$ it varies with
$z=sy$. Radial motion is consequently not autonomous. With
matched powers both Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and
\ref{thm:reliability} apply; with unequal powers the statements
of Appendix~\ref{sec:mismatch} apply. The example requires no independence between the angular and radial
coordinates.

\subsection{A fold inside a feasible branch}\label{sec:fold}
The ordinary precision theorem allows critical points on a null set.
Here this freedom produces genuine collisions within an open feasible
branch, even though every input still contracts the norm toward zero.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fold}
For every parameter choice \eqref{eq:parameters}, matched or
mismatched, there are global $C^\infty$ maps satisfying the
standing assumptions which agree with $\mathsf A_i$ near the origin
and identify two distinct interior points of $Q$. For at least
one control both points lie in the interior of its feasible domain
$Q\cap F_i^{-1}Q$, and their common output lies in $\operatorname{int}Q$.
The maps have critical points in $Q$, but their critical sets
there have area zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We insert a small angular oscillation away from the origin. It
keeps angles inside the triangle but reverses the angular derivative
on part of a fibre, producing a fold.
Fix $c=1/5$, let $E(v)=e^{-1/v}$ for $v>0$ and zero otherwise,
and put
\[
\psi(s)=\frac{E(s-1/4)}{E(s-1/4)+E(1/2-s)}.
\]
The denominator is positive, $0\le\psi\le1$,
$\psi=0$ for $s\le1/4$, and $\psi=1$ for $s\ge1/2$.
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fold}
H(s,z)=
\begin{cases}
(s,z),&s\le1/4,\\
(s,z+cs\psi(s)\sin(2\pi z/s)),&s>1/4,
\end{cases}
\qquad F_i=\mathsf A_iH.
\end{equation}
The quotient is used only away from $s=0$. On a neighbourhood
of each joining point its derivatives are bounded; flatness of
$\psi$ at $1/4$ therefore gives global smoothness. On the open
set $\{s<1/4\}$, $H$ is identity, so the maps equal
$\mathsf A_i$ there, including all derivatives at the origin.

On $Q$ the angular action of $H$ is
$g_\tau(y)=y+c\tau\sin(2\pi y)$, where $\tau=\psi(s)$.
For $0\le y\le1/2$ it lies in $[0,1/2+c]$;
for $1/2\le y\le1$ it lies in $[1/2-c,1]$.
Oddness handles negative angles, and $g_\tau(\pm1)=\pm1$.
Thus $H(Q)\subset Q$. At every $H(x)$ one linear control
is feasible because its two angular domains cover $[-1,1]$.
The radial factors are less than one, and the origin is fixed.
This proves controlled invariance, including all boundary states.

Put $m_* =\max\{r_0,r_1,t_0,t_1\}<1$ and choose
\[
\Lambda=1+\frac{2m_*(1+2c)}{1-m_*}.
\]
On the whole plane $|H^z-z|\le c|s|$, and hence
\[
V(Hx)\le(1+c/\Lambda)V(x),\qquad
V(\mathsf A_i x)\le m_*(1+1/\Lambda)V(x).
\]
Consequently \eqref{eq:contract} holds globally with
\[
\theta=m_*(1+1/\Lambda)(1+c/\Lambda)
 \le m_*[1+(1+2c)/\Lambda]<(1+m_*)/2<1.
\]
All input trajectories therefore converge to the origin uniformly
on bounded initial sets, including trajectories outside $Q$.

For $s>1/4$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:foldcritical}
\det DH=1+2\pi c\psi(s)\cos(2\pi z/s),\qquad
\det DF_i=(r_i^2/a_i)\det DH.
\end{equation}
For $s\le1/4$ the first determinant is one. On every fixed
positive-radius fibre the determinant has either no zeros or finitely
many zeros in $|z|\le s$. Fubini proves that the critical set
in $Q$ has area zero. At $s=3/4$ the first determinant is positive
at $y=0$ and negative at $y=1/2$, since $2\pi/5>1$.
It therefore has actual zeros in $Q$.

A vanishing determinant by itself does not locate a collision
inside a feasible branch. We exhibit one explicitly. At this same
radius write $g(y)=y+c\sin(2\pi y)$. Then
\[
g(1/4)=9/20<1/2,\qquad
g(2/5)=2/5+(1/5)\sin(\pi/5)>1/2,\qquad g(1/2)=1/2.
\]
The middle inequality uses $\sin(\pi/5)>\sin(\pi/6)=1/2$.
There is $y_1\in(1/4,2/5)$ with $g(y_1)=1/2$.
Thus $(3/4,(3/4)y_1)$ and $(3/4,3/8)$ are distinct interior
points with the same image under both controls. Put $\xi=a_0-a_1$.
If $\xi\ne1/2$, angle $1/2$ is strictly inside one linear
feasible branch: use zero when $1/2<\xi$, and one when $1/2>\xi$.
Its output angle is strictly between $-1$ and $1$, and its
output radius is in $(0,1)$, so the common output is in
$\operatorname{int}Q$.

If $\xi=1/2$, move the collision off that cut point.
Since $g(3/5)=3/5-(1/5)\sin(\pi/5)<1/2$, choose
\[
\max\{9/20,g(3/5)\}<v<1/2.
\]
The intermediate value theorem supplies one preimage of $v$
in $(1/4,2/5)$ and another in $(1/2,3/5)$. They give distinct
interior states at radius $3/4$ with equal images. Now
$-1<v<\xi$, so their common output under zero is in
$\operatorname{int}Q$. In both cases continuity and interiority
of the initial and output points put each initial point in the
interior of $Q\cap F_i^{-1}Q$ for the selected control.
\end{proof}

With matched powers, this fold has the precision spectrum of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; with unequal powers it satisfies the
low-precision conclusions of Appendix~\ref{sec:mismatch}.
Its reliability spectrum remains undetermined, since the controls
have critical points. The feasible collisions also rule out a
$Q$-preserving simultaneous conjugacy to injective controls:
conjugacy preserves injectivity. This conclusion concerns conjugacies,
not general semiconjugacies.

\subsection{Local agreement with remote positive-area collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}
The next construction agrees with the linear controls on an open
neighbourhood of zero and retains all-input convergence, but violates
the null-critical-set assumption. It separates local data from the
global cost on a fixed positive-area initial set.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:collapse}
For every matched choice $\beta_0=\beta_1=\beta$ in
\eqref{eq:parameters} and every fixed $0<\sigma<1/2$, there
are global $C^\infty$ maps $G_0,G_1$ equal to $\mathsf A_i$
on $\{s\le\sigma\}$, with $Q$ controlled invariant and a
common global toward-origin norm contraction. The fixed compact set
$K_\sigma=Q\cap\{s\ge2\sigma\}$ has area $1-4\sigma^2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapseone}
r_G(n,\delta;K_\sigma)=1\qquad(n\ge0,\ \delta>0).
\end{equation}
For every finite $\gamma\ge0$ and every precision sequence of
that exponent, the full-state cost satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapsebounds}
\min\{h,\gamma/\beta\}
\le\liminf_n\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
\le\limsup_n\frac{\log r_G(n,\delta_n;Q)}n
\le\min\{\log2,\gamma/\beta\}.
\end{equation}
In particular its limit is $\gamma/\beta$ for
$0\le\gamma\le\beta h$. Its critical sets in $Q$ have positive area.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We interpolate between the linear controls near zero and maps
onto the two boundary rays at larger radii. This leaves a linear
core of positive area, while making one input sufficient on the
outer part of the triangle. Using the same flat function $E$, put
\[
\psi(s)=\frac{E(s-\sigma)}{E(s-\sigma)+E(2\sigma-s)}.
\]
This is smooth, zero for $s\le\sigma$ and one for $s\ge2\sigma$.
Define, with $r_i=a_i^\beta$ and $t_i=a_i^{\beta-1}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapse}
\begin{split}
G_0(s,z)&=(r_0s,(1-\psi(s))t_0(z+a_1s)-\psi(s)r_0s),\\
G_1(s,z)&=(r_1s,(1-\psi(s))t_1(z-a_0s)+\psi(s)r_1s).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
There is no division by $s$. These global smooth maps agree with
$\mathsf A_i$ on the open neighbourhood $\{s<\sigma\}$ of zero.
Write $R_0(s,z)=(r_0s,-r_0s)$ and $R_1(s,z)=(r_1s,r_1s)$.
Then $G_i(x)=(1-\psi(s))\mathsf A_ix+\psi(s)R_ix$.
Choose
\[
\Lambda=1+\max_i\frac{t_i a_{1-i}}{1-t_i},\qquad
V(s,z)=\max\{\Lambda|s|,|z|\}.
\]
The operator bounds are
\[
\|\mathsf A_i\|_V\le
 \max\{r_i,t_i+t_i a_{1-i}/\Lambda\}<1,
\qquad \|R_i\|_V\le r_i<1.
\]
Convexity of the norm gives one $\theta<1$ with
$V(G_ix)\le\theta V(x)$ on all of $\R^2$. Thus every input
converges exponentially toward zero uniformly on bounded sets,
without an assumption that the trajectory remains in $Q$.

For $s>0$ and $y=z/s$, put $t=\psi(s)$ and $\xi=a_0-a_1$.
The output angles are
\[
U_0=(1-t)(y+a_1)/a_0-t,\qquad
U_1=(1-t)(y-a_0)/a_1+t.
\]
For $t<1$ the complete feasible angular domains are
\[
J_0(s)=\left[-1,\min\{1,\xi+2a_0t/(1-t)\}\right],\qquad
J_1(s)=\left[\max\{-1,\xi-2a_1t/(1-t)\},1\right].
\]
They follow directly from $-1\le U_i\le1$ and cover $[-1,1]$.
For $t=1$ both controls are feasible on the entire fibre.
The radius decreases and the origin is fixed, so recursive choice
gives an exactly feasible infinite input from every point of $Q$.

On $K_\sigma$ a single application of $G_0$ sends all states
to the lower ray. At every radius,
$G_0(s,-s)=(r_0s,-r_0s)$, so the same input $0^\infty$
keeps every point exactly in $Q$ at every subsequent time.
The set is nonempty and its area is
$\int_{2\sigma}^1 2s\,ds=1-4\sigma^2$, proving
\eqref{eq:collapseone}.

To estimate the full-cover cost from below, retain the inner
linear region. Put $c=\sigma/2$ and
$Q_c=Q\cap\{s\le c\}$, whose area is $c^2>0$.
For every input and every $x\in Q_c$, the radius is
$s_k=s\prod_{j=1}^k r_{u_j}\le c<\sigma$.
Therefore $\psi(s_k)=0$ at all times, independently of the
second coordinate. Its entire trajectory is the linear one even
if it leaves $Q$ and later reenters. With the same ambient
constraint $Q$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collapsecore}
r_G(n,\delta;Q_c)=r_{\mathsf A}(n,\delta;Q_c)
                       \qquad(n\ge0,\ \delta>0).
\end{equation}
The positive-area lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, applied
to the linear pair and this fixed core, proves the lower bound
in \eqref{eq:collapsebounds}. It concerns arbitrary approximate
inputs and every intermediate constraint.

For the upper bound choose a fixed integer $J_\sigma\ge1$ with
$(\max_i r_i)^{J_\sigma}\le\sigma$. Every state of $Q$ has an
exactly feasible prefix of that length, ending in $Q$ with
$s\le\sigma$. Its subsequent dynamics is linear for all later
inputs, since the radius continues to decrease even outside $Q$.
Concatenate all such binary prefixes with a linear catalogue on
the actual endpoint, using horizon $n-J_\sigma$. The first
$J_\sigma$ times are exactly feasible and the remaining times
satisfy the original Euclidean tolerance. By \eqref{eq:upper},
\[
r_G(n,\delta;Q)\le
2^{J_\sigma}r_{\mathsf A}(n-J_\sigma,\delta;Q)
\le 2^{J_\sigma}C_U\delta^{-1/\beta}
\quad(n\ge J_\sigma,\ 0<\delta\le1).
\]
The common tails in that linear bound satisfy all remaining times;
no limit at a shifted precision exponent is assumed. Controlled
invariance also gives $r_G(n,\delta;Q)\le2^n$.
These prove both upper rates and their equality with the lower rate
for $0\le\gamma\le\beta h$, including arbitrary $o(n)$ errors.
Finally the triangular derivative gives
\[
\det DG_i(s,z)=(1-\psi(s))a_i^{2\beta-1}.
\]
It vanishes on $K_\sigma$, a positive-area set sent in one
feasible step to a null ray.
\end{proof}

For every finite $\gamma>0$, the full-state lower rate in
\eqref{eq:collapsebounds} is positive while the same fixed
$K_\sigma$ has zero rate. Decreasing $\sigma$ makes its area
closer to one but changes the system. This is not a single-system
claim about zero-cost sets of arbitrarily near-full area.
Thus local agreement and contraction under every input do not
suffice for the positive-area precision law. The example shows
failure after dropping the null-critical-set assumption; it does
not establish that assumption as necessary for each individual
system. Such collapsed regions are allowed in the general
noninjective framework of \cite{CZ2024}.

\section{Construction of the angular compression}\label{app:compression}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:flatmap}]
The source and target sets are those of Section~\ref{sec:counter},
with source ratio $b=2/5$, target ratio $\lambda=a_0a_1<b^2$,
and target interval length $\ell$. Define $g$ on the Cantor sets
by the order-preserving address correspondence. We extend it
across their gaps, verify its derivatives, and then join it to
the identity.

Use $E(t)=e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$ and $E(t)=0$ otherwise, and set
\[
\phi(t)=E(t)E(1-t),\qquad
\Sigma(t)=\frac{\int_0^t\phi(v)\,dv}{\int_0^1\phi(v)\,dv}
\quad(0\le t\le1),
\]
extended by zero and one outside that interval. The function is smooth,
strictly increasing on $(0,1)$, and flat at both endpoints.
Map each point of $\mathcal D$ to the point of $\mathcal C$ with
the same address. On a source gap $(u,v)$ whose corresponding
target gap is $(U,V)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gapinterpolation}
g(y)=U+(V-U)\Sigma((y-u)/(v-u)).
\end{equation}
The ordered endpoint correspondence and increasing interpolation
give the full interval identity $g(J_\sigma)=Z_\sigma$.
At level $k$, the gap lengths are $(1-2b)b^k$ and
$(1-2\lambda)\ell\lambda^k$. For $j=1,2$ the gap derivatives obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gapderivative}
|g^{(j)}|\le C_j(\lambda/b^j)^k.
\end{equation}
Since $\lambda<2^{-10}<b^2$, both bounds tend to zero with level.
Define $q_1,q_2$ by these derivatives inside gaps and by zero on
$\mathcal D$. They are continuous, by \eqref{eq:gapderivative}
for high-level gaps and endpoint flatness for the remaining finite
collection.

To identify these functions as derivatives, first integrate $q_1$.
Its integral over each source gap is the corresponding target gap
length. Since the target Cantor set $\mathcal C$ is null, these
lengths exhaust every increment of $g$ between Cantor points.
Including a partial integral when $x$ lies in a gap gives
\[
g(x)=A+\int_{-1/2}^xq_1(y)\,dy\qquad(x\in J_\varnothing).
\]
Next integrate $q_2$. The gap estimates give
\[
\sum_{\text{gaps}}\int|q_2|
                  \le C\sum_{k\ge0}(2\lambda/b)^k<\infty.
\]
Each complete-gap integral of $q_2$ is zero, and a partial-gap
integral equals $q_1(x)$. Now using nullity of the source set $\mathcal D$,
$q_1(x)=\int_{-1/2}^xq_2(y)\,dy$. The fundamental theorem of
calculus gives $g'=q_1$ and $g''=q_2$. Thus $g$ is $C^2$
on $J_\varnothing$, flat to second order on $\mathcal D$, with positive
first derivative on all gaps.

\smallskip
\emph{Joining to the identity outside the Cantor construction.}
On $[-1,-1/2]$ and $[1/2,1]$, the endpoint values and derivatives
are already prescribed. Choose a positive derivative density with
the required integral; this preserves monotonicity and gives the
correct two derivatives at each join. On $[L,R]$
with prescribed increment $T>0$, set
$d=\min\{(R-L)/4,T/4\}$ and $v(x)=E(x-L)E(R-x)$.
For the left join use $c(x)=1-\Sigma((x-L)/d)$, and for the right
use $c(x)=\Sigma((x-R+d)/d)$. In either case $\int c\le d<T$.
Integrate the positive interior density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extensiondensity}
q(x)=c(x)+\frac{T-\int_L^R c}{\int_L^R v}\,v(x).
\end{equation}
For $(-1,-1/2,A+1)$ start at $g(-1)=-1$; for
$(1/2,1,1-B)$ start at $g(1/2)=B$.
The identity endpoint has derivatives $1,0$, and the Cantor endpoint
has derivatives $0,0$. Extending by identity beyond $[-1,1]$
therefore gives a global $C^2$ map satisfying
\eqref{eq:flatmapproperties}. It is strictly increasing and onto,
and $|g(y)-y|\le2$ globally.

\end{proof}

\section{Constants for the local derivative estimates}
\label{app:localconstants}
This appendix verifies the choice of $B$ and $\rho$ used in
Section~\ref{sec:geometry}. It uses only the prescribed derivative
at the origin and a local $C^2$ bound. In particular, none of these
constants depends on the word, horizon or precision.

Let
\(L\) bound the Euclidean bilinear operator norm of both second
derivatives on \(\overline B(0,2)\), and let
\(r_*=\min r_i\), \(r^*=\max r_i\), \(a^*=\max a_i\),
\(a_*=\min a_i\). Take
\(B=10^4(1+L)/r_*^3\), and shrink \(\rho>0\) so that
\begin{equation}
\rho\le\min\left\{\tfrac14,\frac{r_*}{8(1+L)},
  \frac{1-r^*}{8B},\frac{1/a^*-1}{16B},
  \frac1{16B},\frac{a_*}{16B}\right\}.                        \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Taylor's integral formula gives \(|R_i-r_i|\le2Ls\),
\(|\partial_sR_i|,|\partial_sN_i|\le2L\), and
\(|\partial_yR_i|\le2Ls\),
\(|\partial_yN_i-t_i|\le2Ls\).
Put $N_i^0(y)=r_iL_i(y)$. The same Taylor estimate gives
$|N_i-N_i^0|\le2Ls$. Since $|N_i^0|\le2$, the choice of
$\rho$ ensures $|N_i|\le3$ and $R_i\ge r_*/2$.
The logarithmic bounds follow directly:
\[
|\log R_i-\log r_i|\le4Ls/r_*,\qquad
|\partial_s\log R_i|\le4L/r_*,\qquad
|\partial_y\log R_i|\le4Ls/r_*.
\]
For the angular bounds, use $T_i=N_i/R_i$ and
$\partial_yN_i^0=t_i$. In particular,
\[
\begin{split}
T_i-L_i&=\frac{N_i-N_i^0}{R_i}
              +N_i^0\left(\frac1{R_i}-\frac1{r_i}\right),\\
\partial_sT_i&=\frac{\partial_sN_i}{R_i}
                         -\frac{N_i\partial_sR_i}{R_i^2},\\
\partial_yT_i-\frac1{a_i}
 &=\frac{\partial_yN_i-t_i}{R_i}
       +t_i\left(\frac1{R_i}-\frac1{r_i}\right)
                         -\frac{N_i\partial_yR_i}{R_i^2}.
\end{split}
\]
Here $|R_i^{-1}-r_i^{-1}|\le4Ls/r_*^2$ and $0<t_i<1$.
The three right-hand sides are bounded respectively by $Cs$, $C$
and $Cs$, with $C=40(1+L)/r_*^2$. Our choice of $B$ dominates
both $C$ and $4L/r_*$, proving every estimate in \eqref{eq:local}.
